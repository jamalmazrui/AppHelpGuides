MyChart Help Guide

This guide gathers Epic’s own help for MyChart, as it stood on 2 August 2026.

Whose MyChart this is. Every healthcare organisation runs its own MyChart, with its own web address, its own help desk and its own rules. This guide is not any one of those. It is Epic’s own documentation for the product itself, which applies wherever MyChart is used, and it is what you need before you ever reach a hospital’s help desk.

A limit worth knowing in advance. A great many of these articles end by telling you to contact your healthcare organisation. That is not evasion on Epic’s part: your record lives with your hospital, Epic cannot see it and cannot reset your password, and each organisation decides its own rules on proxy access, self-signup and much else. This guide tells you what MyChart can do; only your own organisation can tell you what it has switched on.

The arrangement is Epic’s own. Its help index lists every article under twelve headings, and those are the categories here, unchanged.

MyChart is published in several languages. This is the United States English edition.

This document holds 12 categories, 76 articles and about 9,113 words.

Accessing MyChart

Can I access MyChart from outside of the country?

Each healthcare organization hosts their own MyChart servers and therefore decides which IP addresses in the world can access their databases, websites, and app content. It is up to the healthcare organization to decide how much security they want to have and which countries to allow.

If you are having trouble accessing MyChart while traveling internationally, contact your healthcare organization directly to ask about using MyChart in your current country. You can usually find their contact information on the FAQ page, which is linked at the bottom of their MyChart website.

How do I download the MyChart app?

On your mobile device, download MyChart from the Apple’s App Store or the Google Play Store using these links:

https://itunes.apple.com/us/app/mychart/id382952264?mt=8

https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=epic.mychart.android&hl=en

Some healthcare organizations have their own version of the MyChart mobile app. If you are seen at one of these healthcare organizations, you need to download their app instead.

How do I sign up for MyChart?

You can create an account through your healthcare organization to access your information through MyChart. If your organization gave you an activation code on your After Visit Summary or in an email or text message, follow the instructions provided to create your MyChart account.

If you don’t have an activation code:

  1. On your healthcare organization’s MyChart login page, select Sign up.
  2. Follow the steps to verify your identity and create your MyChart account.

What if I can’t find my healthcare organization?

You can see a full list of the healthcare organizations available in MyChart by selecting Find your MyChart at the top of this screen. From there, you can search by state or enter the name of your organization.

If your organization is not on the list, they might not use MyChart. They might offer a different patient portal, and you can contact them directly about accessing your account.

If you’re looking for your healthcare organization’s contact information, you might find it by:

  • Selecting Find your MyChart and searching for your organization. After navigating to your organization’s MyChart login page, select FAQ at the bottom of the screen.
  • Selecting Need help? at the bottom of this screen and selecting Other > Other. In the search box under What is your healthcare organization’s name?, enter the name of the organization you’re seen at and select it. If your organization provided their contact information, it might appear. If not, you can select the button to take you to their MyChart site.

What’s required to use MyChart?

You need access to one of the following:

  • A computer connected to the internet and an up-to-date browser (such as Edge, Chrome, Firefox, or Safari)
  • A mobile device or tablet with the MyChart mobile app

You also need an email address or a phone number that can receive text messages.

Logging in to MyChart

How do I change my email address or phone number on file?

You might be able to update the information yourself. To change the email address or phone number associated with your MyChart account, log in to MyChart and go to the Personal Information activity from the MyChart menu. 

For help with changes you are not able to make yourself, contact your healthcare organization’s MyChart help desk. You can usually find their contact information on the FAQ page, which is linked at the bottom of their MyChart website.

How do I change my username or password?

To change your username, contact your healthcare organization’s MyChart help desk. You can usually find their contact information on the FAQ page, which is linked at the bottom of their MyChart website. Depending on the organization’s policies, an administrator might be able to change it for you.

If you forgot your username, select Forgot login information? on the MyChart login screen to recover it. 

You can change your password in MyChart after logging in by going to Account Settings. If you are not able to log in, you can reset your password by selecting Forgot login information? on the login screen.

How do I log in to MyChart using a passcode?

You can log in to the MyChart mobile app using a 4-digit passcode instead of your username and password.

To set up a passcode:

  1. Log in to the MyChart app on your device, using your existing username and password.
  2. Go to Account Settings, which you can find under the menu.
  3. Select the Use Passcode option to toggle it on.
  4. Enter a passcode.

The next time you log in on the mobile app, you will see an option to Log in with passcode instead of your username and password.

How do I log in to MyChart using a passkey?

You can log in to MyChart without a password by adding a passkey to your account. Passkeys are encrypted digital keys that allow you to log in to MyChart with your fingerprint, face, or personal identification number (PIN). They can be saved on your desktop computer, in your phone’s password manager, or in a security key, a small external device that looks like a thumb drive. You can use passkeys to log in across devices, such as by scanning a QR code on your laptop with your mobile device.  

Create a Passkey:

  1. Log in to your MyChart account on a web browser.
  2. Go to Account Settings, which you can find by searching the menu.
  3. Go to Manage passkeys at the top of the page.
  4. Select Add new passkey.  

Follow the prompts from your operating system or browser to add a passkey.  

Rename a Passkey:

  1. Log in to your MyChart account on a web browser.
  2. Go to Account Settings, which you can find by searching the menu.
  3. Go to Manage passkeys at the top of the page.
  4. Select the pencil icon next to the passkey you want to rename.  
  5. Enter your desired name.

Remove a Passkey:

  1. Log in to your MyChart account on a web browser.
  2. Go to Account Settings, which you can find by searching the menu.
  3. Go to Manage passkeys at the top of the page.
  4. Select Remove passkey on the passkey you want to remove.  

Log In with a Passkey:

  1. Select the Username field to see the auto-fill prompt with your username and the associated passkey.
  2. Alternatively, select Log in with passkey next to Sign up, below the login fields.

How do I log in to MyChart using biometrics?

You can log in to the MyChart mobile app using your fingerprint or facial recognition instead of your username and password.

Set Up Fingerprint Login:

  1. Log in to the MyChart app on your device, using your existing username and password.
  2. Go to Account Settings from the menu.
  3. Select the Use Biometric Login or Fingerprint option to toggle it on. 

The next time you try to log in on the mobile app, you see an option Log in with biometrics or Log in with Touch ID instead of your username and password.

Set Up Face ID:

  1. Log in to the MyChart app on your device, using your existing username and password.
  2. Go to Account Settings from the menu.
  3. Select the Face ID option and toggle it on. 

The next time you try to log in on the mobile app, you see an option to Log in with Face ID instead of your username and password.

How do I log in to MyChart with my username?

MyChart might look a little different depending on if you are using a web browser on your computer or the MyChart mobile app on your phone.

Web Browser

  1. Go to your healthcare organization’s MyChart website.
  2. Enter your MyChart username.
  3. Select Continue.
  4. Enter your password and select Log in. 

Mobile App

  1. Open the MyChart app and search for your healthcare organization by location or name.
  2. Enter your MyChart username.
  3. Select Continue.
  4. Enter your password and select Log in.

I forgot my username or password. What should I do?

On the MyChart login screen for your healthcare organization, select Forgot login information? and follow the instructions below based on what you see:

  • If you see What account do you want to recover?, select Recover your MyChart account and follow the prompts to recover your username and password.
  • If you see Recover your account, enter the email address or phone number you previously used for two-step verification for your account. Enter the code sent to you and follow the prompts to recover your account.
  • If you see I forgot my username and I forgot my password, choose the option that fits your situation. If you forgot both your username and password, start with I forgot my username first. Answer some security questions to verify your identity and recover your username or password. 

If you still need help, contact your healthcare organization’s MyChart help desk. You can usually find their contact information on the account recovery page you were on or on their MyChart website’s FAQ page.

I’m not receiving codes for two-step verification. What should I do?

If you are having trouble receiving emails or text messages, the email address or phone number on your account might be incorrect or outdated. Contact your healthcare organization’s MyChart help desk to update it. You can usually find the contact information on their FAQ page, which is linked at the bottom of their MyChart website.

What should I do if I’m locked out of my account?

If you try to log in unsuccessfully too many times, you might be locked out of the account. In this case, you won’t be able to reset your password yourself. For help resetting passwords and regaining access to your account, contact your healthcare organization’s MyChart help desk. You can usually find the contact information on their FAQ page, which is linked at the bottom of their MyChart website.

Using MyChart

How do I prepare for my upcoming appointment?

You can save time and minimize contact by verifying your information and answering appointment-related questions before you arrive. Log in to MyChart and look for the Get Ready option for your upcoming appointment.

How do I schedule an appointment in MyChart?

To schedule or request an appointment, go to Find Care > Schedule an Appointment.

Depending on your reason for scheduling or the type of appointment you choose, you either schedule the appointment yourself or request to have your healthcare organization contact you.

What should I do if some of my information in MyChart is incorrect?

The information you see in MyChart comes directly from your medical record at your provider’s office. Your information is reviewed and updated after each visit.

If something looks wrong, ask your provider to update any incorrect information at your next visit. You can also contact the medical records department at your healthcare organization to request a correction to your chart.

Sharing Your Data

Can I give an app access to a family member’s health information?

Yes. If you have proxy access to another person’s MyChart account, you might be able to give an app access to their health information. During the sharing process, you select whose information to share. The app can access information for only the person you select.

Can I share my health information with another app?

Yes. You can share your health information, such as lab results, medications, allergies, and immunization records, with another app. Before any information is shared, you must approve the sharing, usually by logging in to your MyChart account. Review the app’s terms and conditions, and privacy policy before you share. 

What health information can apps access?

Apps can ask to see certain parts of your health record, such as your medications, allergies, test results, or visit history. Apps can only request a standard set of information called the United States Core Data for Interoperability (USCDI). Different apps might request different information types based on the app’s use case. For more information on USCDI, go to the HealthIT.gov website: https://isp.healthit.gov/united-states-core-data-interoperability-uscdi.

Can I choose what information to share with an app?

Yes. You can select or clear individual information types before you share your health information. The app receives only the information types you approve. 

Can I choose how long an app has access to my health information?

Yes. You select how long the app has access, such as one hour, one day, or one year. During this time, the app might also receive any new information added to your record.  

After access ends, the app cannot receive new information from your health record unless you share access to the app again. The app can still see information you already shared. Review the app’s privacy policy to learn how it handles your health information after you stop sharing information.

Why can’t I find my healthcare organization when trying to share access with an app?

Your healthcare organization might not have turned on support for the app, or the name you are searching for might not match exactly. Confirm you are searching for the correct organization name or contact the app’s support team.

How can I use MyChart Central to give an app access to my data?

You might be able to use MyChart Central to give an app access to your health information across multiple healthcare organizations at the same time. This is useful if you receive care from more than one organization and want to share your information from all of them with a single app. 

To give an app access to your health information using MyChart Central: 

  1. When a supported app requests access to your information, log in to MyChart with your Epic ID. 
  2. If you want to edit what information types to share, select Edit what will be shared.
  3. You can also remove any organizations you don’t want to share your health information from by selecting the Edit your healthcare organizations button. 
  4. The default sharing period is one year. To choose a different sharing period, select the Edit access time button. 
  5. To confirm, select Share Data button. The app can then access your information from all of the organizations you chose to share information from. 

Sharing your information from multiple organizations using MyChart Central might not be supported by your app or organization. You can still use your Epic ID to share information from each linked organization one at a time if the workflow is not supported. 

Can I choose different information types or access times for different organizations?

To edit information or access time settings to be different for each organization you are sharing information from, log in using your organization’s username and password instead of your Epic ID in the app’s sharing workflow.   

Why can’t I see all of my organizations when sharing health information through MyChart Central?

Only organizations that are linked to your MyChart Central account and support the app appear in the list. If an organization is missing, it might not support this feature. Make sure all of your MyChart accounts are linked by going to the Link My Accounts activity in MyChart Central.

How do I give an app access to my health information or my family member’s health information?

To authorize an app: 

  1. Start the sharing process. This might happen from inside the app, or from your organization’s MyChart. 
  2. When prompted, log in to your MyChart account. You can use your Epic ID if your organization participates in MyChart Central. Your MyChart password is never shared with the app.
  3. If you have access to another person’s account, such as a child, select whose information to share.
  4. Review how the app uses the information.
  5. Choose which types of information to share and how long the app has access.
  6. Confirm you want to share the information.

How do I see which apps have access to my health information?

In MyChart, go to the Linked Apps and Devices activity to see the apps that can access your health information. This page shows each app’s name, what information it can access, and when its access expires. You can also manage apps for your linked organizations in MyChart Central if your organization supports this feature.   

The Who’s Accessed My Record? page in MyChart shows a history of each app’s access. You can see the name of each app, the date and time of access, and the type of information accessed. 

To see access history: 

  1. Go to Document Center.
  2. Select Who’s Accessed My Record?
  3. From the dropdown menu, select Third-Party Apps.  

If you shared your information with an app using MyChart Central, access records appear at each organization from which you shared your health information.  

How do I know which apps have access to my family member’s health information?

If you have proxy access, you can view that family member’s Linked Apps and Devices activity to see which apps have access to their information. You can also check the Who’s Accessed My Record? activity for their account to see a history of app access. 

To see access history:

  1. Log in to MyChart.
  2. Switch to your family member’s view by selecting them from the list in the top right corner of MyChart. 
  3. In the MyChart menu, select Linked Apps and Devices or Who’s Accessed My Record? 
  4. To remove an app’s access, select Stop sharing.

What should I do if I do not recognize an app that has access to my health information?

  1. Go to the Linked Apps and Devices activity in MyChart and revoke the app’s access immediately.
  2. Contact your healthcare organization’s help desk to report the issue so they can secure your MyChart account.
  3. Review the Who’s Accessed My Record? page to see what information the app might have accessed.

What do I need to know about security and sharing my health information?

An app you choose to share your health information with never sees your username or password. When you share your information with another app, you log in on your healthcare organization’s secure login page.  

What should I do if I think another app has misused my information?

Revoke the app’s access from the Linked Apps and Devices activity in MyChart. To remove an app’s access:

  1. Log in to your MyChart account.
  2. In the MyChart menu, search for Linked Apps and Devices. 
  3. Find the app you want to remove and select Stop sharing. 

You can also file a complaint with the app developer, or if you believe the app has violated your rights, contact the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services or your country’s data protection authority.

What does it mean if an app is affiliated with my healthcare organization?

An affiliated app is one your healthcare organization has a formal agreement with. If an app is affiliated, you’ll see a banner on the review page when you share your information that says the app is affiliated. 

You should still review the privacy policy and terms and conditions before sharing information.

What happens if I deny or revoke access to an app?

If you deny access, the app cannot see any of your health information. If you change your mind, you can go back to the app later and give it access again. Denying access does not affect your MyChart account or your care. 

If you revoke access, the app stops receiving new information from your health record. However, the app might still have information it already received. To request deletion of that information, contact the app directly. The app’s information practices might be available on the review page you saw when you first authorized the app.  

How do I revoke an app’s access to my health information?

To revoke access: 

  1. Go to the Linked Apps and Devices activity in MyChart or MyChart Central.
  2. Find the app you want to remove and select Stop sharing.

After access has been revoked, the app can no longer receive new information from your health record.

Linking accounts and sharing your record

How do I request or provide proxy access to another person’s MyChart account?

You can request proxy access to your family member’s account. With proxy access, you can see their health information and your own from within the same MyChart account. 

You can also give a family member or friend access to your medical records when you need help managing your appointments or other medical needs.

Each healthcare organization sets their own policies around proxy access, including who can have it, how access changes based on the patient’s age, and what a proxy can see and do in MyChart. 

Get or Give Proxy Access

To give someone access to your medical record: 

  1. Go to the Sharing Hub activity and select Manage friends and family access.
  2. Select Invite friends or family and enter the name and email address for the person you want to invite.
  3. After they receive the email and confirm that they know you, they can access your information through their own MyChart account. 

When you view someone’s account as a proxy, the name of the account you are viewing appears at the top of the activity. You can switch between MyChart accounts by selecting the Switch menu in the top right corner.

If this option isn’t available, contact your healthcare organization to find out how to get or give proxy access. Some organizations require you to contact them directly rather than setting it up yourself in MyChart. For example, some organizations require you to sign a paper form before getting proxy access.

How do I share my medical record with other providers?

There are several ways to share your medical record, depending on who needs to access it.   

You can use the Share Everywhere feature in MyChart to give any provider temporary access to basic information in your chart. For example, this can be helpful if you are away from home on a trip and need medical care. Just go to the Share Everywhere activity and follow the prompts to give someone access. 

Care Everywhere lets your healthcare provider securely access and update your record from other healthcare organizations so that they can provide the best care for you. If you want to opt out of sharing your information through Care Everywhere, you can discuss that with your healthcare provider at your next visit or contact the medical records department at your healthcare organization. 

For more detailed information about sharing your record, refer to this page.

I have two or more MyChart accounts from different organizations. Can I link them together?

MyChart accounts can be linked so you can see all your information in one view. We call this Happy Together. Depending on your healthcare provider’s version of Epic, Happy Together might not be available to you yet. If your organizations both participate, you should be able to link them by going to the Link My Accounts page in your MyChart account.

For more information about Happy Together, go to this page.

Why can’t I see my child’s information in MyChart?

Proxy access to a child’s account changes as they get older. Because each organization has their own interpretation of state and federal legislation that guarantees privacy rights to adolescents, each organization determines the ages at which proxy access might become limited or be removed. 

If you have questions about limited access to your child’s MyChart account, contact your healthcare organization directly to address your concerns. You can usually find the contact information on their FAQ page, which is linked at the bottom of their MyChart website.

MyChart Central

Can I view my family’s health information in MyChart Central?

MyChart Central is focused on providing a single login experience to your linked MyChart accounts. It does not display your family’s health information directly.

If you have proxy access to a family member’s account, a Friends and family option appears on the account card for any linked accounts. Selecting Friends and family shows the MyChart accounts for any family members or friends that you can access at that healthcare organization. To view a family member’s or friend’s health information, select the arrow to go to your MyChart account for that organization.

How do I delete my MyChart Central account?

Deleting your MyChart Central account does not delete any of your health information at your linked MyChart accounts. Your data is stored and managed by your healthcare organization.

You can delete your MyChart Central account at any time:  

  1. Log in to your MyChart Central account. 
  2. Go to Account Settings. 
  3. At the bottom of the page, select Delete Your Account. 
  4. Confirm your choice when prompted. 

Your MyChart Central account stops working immediately. Your linked MyChart accounts remain active. If you want to delete your individual MyChart accounts, contact each healthcare organization separately.

Important: If your Epic ID was the only way you could access a healthcare organization’s MyChart, you need to set up a new username and password with that organization to log in to MyChart. To set up new login credentials, reach out to your healthcare organization’s MyChart support team.

How do I link MyChart accounts to MyChart Central?

To link your MyChart accounts to your MyChart Central account:

  1. Log in to your MyChart Central account on a web browser or the MyChart mobile app.
  2. Select Link new account on the home page.

If you cannot find your healthcare organization, your organization might not have joined the MyChart Central network yet.  You can view a list of participating organizations here and select the MyChart Central only checkbox. If your healthcare organization is not participating yet, check back later.

How do I log in to MyChart Central using a passkey?

A passkey is like a password, but it is securely stored on your phone or computer so that you do not have to remember it. If you are used to using biometric login options, like fingerprint or face ID, you might want to try using a passkey to log in to MyChart Central. When you use a passkey, your device verifies your identity in a secure way without sharing your password. This makes logging in faster, easier, and safer. 

To set up a passkey for your Epic ID: 

  1. Log in to MyChart Central on your personal device.
  2. Go to Account Settings.
  3. Select Manage passkeys.
  4. Select Add new passkey.
  5. Follow the prompts to create your passkey. 

The next time you log in on that device, select Log in with passkey and follow the prompts.

How do I log in to MyChart Central?

Web Browser:

  1. In your web browser, go to central.mychart.org to access MyChart Central’s login page.
  2. Enter your Epic ID and password.
  3. Select Log in.

Mobile App:

  1. Open the MyChart mobile app and search for MyChart Central.
  2. Enter your Epic ID and password.
  3. Select Log in.

How do I log in to MyChart with my Epic ID?

Your Epic ID and the password you choose for your MyChart Central account can log you in to any MyChart account that you have linked to MyChart Central.

Web Browser:

  1. On your healthcare organization’s MyChart website, enter your Epic ID.
  2. Select Continue. You are redirected to MyChart Central.
  3. Enter your MyChart Central password and select Log in. 

Mobile App:

  1. Open the MyChart mobile app and search for your healthcare organization. You can search by location or by organization name.
  2. Enter your Epic ID.
  3. Select Continue.
  4. Enter your password and select Log in.

How do I sign up for MyChart Central?

To sign up for MyChart Central, you must:

  • Be at least 18 years old and live in the United States.
  • Have an existing MyChart account at an organization that participates in MyChart Central.

To check if your healthcare organization participates in MyChart Central:

  1. Select Find your MyChart at the top of this page.
  2. Select the checkbox to show only healthcare organizations that participate in MyChart Central.

If your healthcare organization is not participating yet, check back later. If your healthcare organization is participating, sign up for MyChart Central using either option below:

Keeping your data secure

How do I protect myself from MyChart scams and fraud?

Scammers sometimes try to trick patients by using the MyChart name or logo to make emails, text messages, phone calls, and websites look official. Some might try to steal your login information or promise free gifts if you enter payment details. A few simple habits go a long way toward keeping you safe. Always remember: stop and check if it doesn’t feel right.

How to protect yourself

  • Check the sender and the web address. Before you click a link, enter login information, or provide payment details, look closely at the sender’s full email address and the complete website URL. Small misspellings and extra words are common signs of a fake. Each healthcare organization has its own URL for MyChart. You can look up your healthcare organization’s official MyChart website using the search tool at mychart.org. MyChart.com, MyChart.org, and Epic.com are all official Epic websites.
  • When in doubt, ask. Not sure if a message or website is real? Don’t enter your login or payment info yet. Call your healthcare organization’s help desk first and ask.
  • Guard your password and verification codes. Epic and your healthcare organization will never ask for your password or verification codes. The only safe place to enter them is the MyChart website or app. No one should ask for them by phone, text, or email.
  • Keep your communications private. Epic and your healthcare organization will never ask you to change your email address or phone number to one you don’t control. Don’t forward messages from MyChart to others.
  • Be skeptical of “free” offers. Some messages promise free gifts or prizes, such as a “free first aid kit,” in exchange for your info. These are most likely scams. MyChart does not run giveaways.
  • Never run commands on your computer. MyChart will never tell you to press keyboard shortcuts or to run a command to “verify,” “fix,” or “unlock” your account.

If you suspect you were a victim of fraud

  • Report the email as spam in your email client. This helps protect others!
  • Reset your MyChart password for your healthcare organization or for your Epic ID with MyChart Central.
  • Review the email address, phone number, and other contact information on file in MyChart.
    • How do I change my email address or phone number on file?
  • Contact your healthcare organization’s help desk.
    • Find your MyChart

How is MyChart secure?

Your healthcare organization takes great care to keep your health information private and secure. Access to your information in MyChart is controlled through secure activation codes and personal login credentials, like a username and password.  

If you use the MyChart mobile app, after you have logged in with your password, you can also turn on additional ways to log in, such as by using a four-digit passcode (PIN), facial recognition, or fingerprint login. MyChart also supports passkeys, which can be saved to your device and allow you to log in without needing to remember your username and password. 

MyChart also uses two-step verification to help ensure that your account stays secure even if someone else has your username or password. When you log in, you must enter a code that is sent to you by email or text message, in addition to using your username and password.

You might receive email or text notifications that contain links to MyChart. These notifications allow you to log in to MyChart securely using a link that expires after a short time. Keeping your MyChart account secure means keeping your phone number and email account secure. If you think that your phone number or email account might be compromised, you can change the phone number or email account on file on the Personal Information activity in MyChart. 

When you need to contact your healthcare organization, all MyChart messaging is done while you are logged in to the website or mobile app, which keeps your information more secure than conventional email.

What can my provider see?

Your healthcare provider has access to your full medical record so they can provide the best care for you. If you are seen at more than one organization, your provider might be able to see information from your record at another organization if both organizations participate in Care Everywhere.

MyChart is a window that shows you what is in your medical record. Your MyChart information comes directly from your electronic medical record at your provider’s office.

What is two-step verification?

MyChart uses two-step verification to keep your account secure even if someone else has your username or password. After entering your login information, you receive an email or text message with a code to enter to log in to MyChart.

You can also use an authenticator app instead. An authenticator app is a phone app that generates a unique code every few seconds. You enter this code to verify your identity when you log in.

To change your verification method: 

  1. From the MyChart menu, go to Account Settings > Two-step verification.
  2. Select Verify with email or text message or Verify with authenticator app and follow the instructions on the screen.  

The next time you log in to MyChart, you receive a text message or email with a code to enter (if you selected Verify with email address) or you enter a code from your authenticator app (if you chose Verify with authenticator app).

Where is my data stored?

Your data is stored with your healthcare organization. Epic does not have access to your medical data and cannot reset your passwords for you. To update your information outside of MyChart, contact your healthcare organization directly. You can usually find their contact information on their FAQ page, which is linked on the bottom of their MyChart website.

Deleting and deactivating your account

  1. Go to Account settings > Account management.
  2. Select Deactivate account.
  3. Select Deactivate on the Deactivate Your Account page.

How do I reactivate my MyChart account?

If your account is deactivated and you’d like to reactivate it, contact your healthcare organization’s MyChart help desk. You can find their contact information on their FAQ page, which is linked on the bottom of their MyChart website.

What happens if I deactivate my account?

If you deactivate your account, you lose access to your MyChart account. You are logged out automatically and can no longer log in. You also stop receiving communications directing you to log in to MyChart.  

  • If you have connected your account to any other organizations, you can no longer see information from your deactivated account.
  • Other MyChart users who have access to your medical information keep that access even after you deactivate your account.
  • Your medical information is not deleted. Your healthcare organization may keep your information on file to ensure you continue to receive the best possible care.

You can deactivate your account without being logged in. If you do not remember your username or password and want to deactivate your MyChart account, contact your healthcare organization’s MyChart help desk. You can usually find the contact information on their FAQ page, which is linked on the bottom of their MyChart website. 

You can sign up for MyChart again in the future. To reactivate your account, contact your healthcare organization.

Why can’t I delete my account?

MyChart is just a window that allows you to see your legal medical record at your healthcare organization. Epic does not store any of your medical information. Your information is always with your healthcare organization, and there are laws and regulations for them to keep it. 

You can deactivate your MyChart account in Account Settings in the MyChart mobile app or by contacting your healthcare organization’s MyChart help desk. You can find their contact information on their FAQ page, which is linked on the bottom of their MyChart website.
