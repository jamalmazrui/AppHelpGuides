Google App Help Guide

The Google app is Google’s search app. On iPhone and iPad, it lets you search by typing, by voice, or with your camera, and it holds AI Mode, Search Live and the Discover feed of stories. On Windows, the Google app for desktop opens a search box over whatever you are doing when you press Alt+Space. That box searches the web, your files, your installed apps and your Google Drive.

About this guide. Gathered 27 November 2026 from Google’s own pages:

  • Google Search Help, including its articles on the Google app for iPhone and iPad and on the Google app for desktop
  • The Google app’s App Store listing
  • The Google app for desktop page

It covers the iPhone and iPad app, the Windows app, and Google Search on the web. It does not cover the Android app or Google’s other apps, such as Gemini or Chrome. Google updates its apps often, and several of its help pages say their settings are being changed. So a button name here may differ slightly from what you hear. Google’s help pages name buttons as they look on screen, and VoiceOver may read a button by a similar label.

AI features

AI Mode on iPhone

AI Mode answers longer questions in plain sentences, with links to web pages where you can read more. You can keep asking follow-up questions.

  1. Open the Google app and tap AI Mode on the home screen.
  2. Type your question in the “Ask anything” bar near the bottom of the screen.
  3. To ask a follow-up, type it in the same bar.

Other ways to ask:

  • By voice: tap the microphone in the “Ask anything” bar and speak. Tap it again for each new question.
  • With a picture: in the “Ask anything” bar, tap Lens to take a photo, or tap the menu icon to upload an image.
  • With a file: the same menu lets you upload a PDF, a file on your phone, or a Google Drive file.

History. Tap the AI Mode history icon at the top of the screen to see past questions saved to your account. To delete one, tap More next to it, then Delete. To delete them all, tap More at the top of AI Mode history, then Delete all. Deleted searches may still show in My Activity for up to a day.

AI Mode is not offered for every account, country or language. To pick up where you left off, turn on history and personalized recommendations.

AI Mode on the web

  1. Go to google.com/ai. Or search on google.com and choose AI Mode.
  2. Type your question in the “Ask anything” bar near the bottom of the page. Type follow-ups in the same bar.

You can also:

  • select the microphone in the bar and speak your question;
  • open the menu in the bar to upload images, PDFs, files on your computer, or Google Drive files;
  • paste a web page’s address into the bar and ask about that page.

To see past questions, choose AI Mode history. To delete one, choose More, then Delete. To delete them all, choose Delete all.

AI Overviews

An AI Overview is a short summary written by AI that can appear at the top of search results, with links to dig deeper. Google shows one when it judges that a summary will help.

  • Rate it: each overview ends with a thumbs-up and a thumbs-down button. Thumbs-down lets you report a problem.
  • Ask more: on a phone, choose “Show more” to continue in AI Mode.
  • Skip it: AI Overviews cannot be turned off. But after you search, you can choose the Web filter to see only plain text links.

Search Live

Search Live is a spoken, back-and-forth conversation with Google. You talk, it answers aloud and gives web links. You can also share your camera so it can see what you see. It works in the Google app on phones, wherever AI Mode is offered.

Google’s steps for Search Live are written for Android, so labels on iPhone may differ a little.

  1. Make sure the Google app may use your microphone.
  2. Open the Google app.
  3. Below the Search bar, tap Live.
  4. Start talking.

While you talk:

  • Camera: tap Video to turn the camera on, and tap it again to stop. The camera turns off if you leave the app or lock the screen, and it does not come back on by itself.
  • Mute: tap Mute to turn off the microphone, and Unmute to turn it back on.
  • Transcript: tap Transcript to end the talk and read it as an AI Mode conversation. Older transcripts are in AI Mode history.
  • End: tap Exit.

Ask people’s permission before you record them or bring them into a conversation.

Getting started

Installing on iPhone

Get the Google app from the App Store. The developer is Google LLC. It needs iOS 17 or later on iPhone, or iPadOS 17 or later on iPad.

Installing on Windows

The Google app for desktop runs on Windows 10 or later. You must be 13 or older. Download it from the Google app for desktop page. Turn on history and personalized recommendations for the best results. AI Mode inside it is not offered for every account, country or language.

Opening and closing the Windows app

To open it:

  • Press Alt+Space at any time.
  • Or open the Google icon in the notification area.
  • Or press the Windows key, type Google, and open the Google app.

If Alt+Space does nothing, the app may not be running. Look for the Google icon in the notification area, or start the app from the Start menu.

To change the shortcut:

  1. Open the app and choose your profile picture at the top right.
  2. Open Configurations.
  3. Choose the current shortcut under “Keyboard shortcut.”
  4. Press the keys you want to use instead.

To close it:

  • Press Alt+Space or Escape to hide it. It keeps running so the shortcut still works.
  • To quit fully, open the context menu of the Google icon in the notification area and choose Quit.

Signing in and switching accounts

On iPhone, Google apps share your Google Account. To see or switch accounts:

  1. Open the Google app.
  2. Tap your profile picture or initial.
  3. Tap the account that’s signed in to choose another one or add one.

To take an account off your device, tap your profile picture or initial, then Manage accounts on this device. Tap the account, then Remove account. This removes it from every Google app on the device.

On Windows, you must be signed in to search your Google Drive.

Images and sounds

Finding a song

The Google app can name a song playing near you, or one you hum.

  1. Open the Google app.
  2. In the search bar, tap the microphone, then tap “Search a song.”
  3. Let it hear the song playing, or hum, whistle or sing the tune.
  4. Pick from the possible matches to listen, read the lyrics or watch the video.

You can also start a song search from a Google Search widget.

Google Lens on iPhone

Google Lens searches with a picture instead of words. It can identify things, find similar products to buy, translate text, and help with homework.

  1. Open the Google app.
  2. Tap the Google Lens icon in the search bar.
  3. Either point the camera at something and tap Search, or tap the photo picker and choose a photo.
  4. Scroll down to the results.

More options:

  • Ask aloud: press and hold the shutter button to ask a spoken question about what the camera sees. This works in English only.
  • Add words: tap “Add to your search” and type words to narrow the results.
  • Search part of a picture: drag the corners of the selection box. A smaller area gives more exact results.
  • Copy text: tap and select the words you want.

To control which of your photos Google can see, open the iPhone Settings app, then Apps, Google, Photos. Choose None, Limited access or Full access.

Google Lens on Windows

  1. Open the Google app with Alt+Space.
  2. Choose the Lens icon.
  3. Select an area of your screen.
  4. Choose Ask anything, Translate, Copy as text, or Copy as image. An area with no text offers only Copy as image.

Press Escape to go back to the app. Only the part of the screen you select is sent to Google.

You can also choose the Share screen icon, pick a screen, window or app, and ask questions about it.

Privacy and safety

Deleting search history on iPhone

Delete everything:

  1. Open the Google app.
  2. Tap your profile picture or initial, then Search history.
  3. Tap the Delete down arrow, then Delete all time.

The Manage History menu under the Search bar also offers Delete today and Delete custom range. Deleted history cannot be brought back.

Delete automatically:

  1. Tap your profile picture or initial, then Search history, then Controls.
  2. Tap “Auto-delete activity older than” and choose 3, 18 or 36 months.
  3. Tap Next.

When you’re signed out, searches are kept only on your device. To clear them, open Settings in the Google app, then the privacy section, and tap Clear app history. This does not affect your other devices.

Deleting search history on the web

  1. Go to your Search history.
  2. To delete it all, open the Delete menu and choose Delete all time. To delete a range of dates, choose Delete custom range. You can also delete one day or one search.

To delete automatically, choose Auto-delete in the “Saving history” section, pick 3, 18 or 36 months, and choose Next.

Incognito mode and Face ID

Searches you make in Incognito mode are not saved to your Google Account.

On iPhone, you can require Face ID to get back into an Incognito session after 15 minutes away. Open Settings in the Google app, go to Privacy & Security, and turn on “Enable Face ID for Incognito mode.”

SafeSearch

SafeSearch hides explicit results. It has three choices:

  • Blur: blurs explicit images, but explicit text and links may still show.
  • Filter: hides all explicit results.
  • Off: shows everything.

On iPhone: open the Google app, tap your profile picture or initial, then Settings, then SafeSearch, and choose.

On the web: go to google.com/safesearch and choose.

If you find a lock icon, someone else, such as a parent, school or network, controls the setting, and you can’t change it.

What the Windows app keeps on your computer

The Google app for desktop builds a list, called an index, of your files and apps so it can find them fast. That index stays on your computer. When you use Lens, only the part of the screen you select is sent to Google.

Searching

Saving results to collections

On iPhone, you can save web pages and images, and group them into collections. You must be signed in.

Save a page or image:

  1. Search in the Google app and open the result or image.
  2. Tap Save.
  3. To put it in a collection, tap Edit, then Add beside the collection, or New to make one.

Find what you saved:

  1. Tap Tabs at the bottom of the app.
  2. Tap Saved at the top.
  3. Tap “View all collections.”

To make an empty collection, tap Create on the collections screen, name it, and tap Done.

Searching on Windows

Press Alt+Space and type. Then:

  • Web: press Enter, then choose All to see Google Search results.
  • AI Mode: type a question and press Enter. Type follow-ups in the box under the answer.
  • An app: type its name, such as Notepad, and press Enter.
  • A file: type the file name, move to it, and press Enter.
  • A Google Drive file: type the file name and press Enter. You must be signed in.
  • Ask about a file: choose the new tab icon in the search box, select files, and choose Open. You can also drag files into the app.

Using a screen reader with Google Search

Google designs its search results pages to work with screen readers and keyboards. Headings are the fastest way to move around.

On a computer:

  • Level 1 headings mark the major parts of the page.
  • Level 2 headings mark groups of results, such as “Videos.”
  • Level 3 headings mark each result and each ad.

On a phone:

  • Level 1 is the Google logo and home page link.
  • The first level 2 heading is the search results, and the second is the ads.
  • Level 3 headings mark each result and each ad.

On a computer, press Tab until you reach one of these links, then press Enter:

  • Skip to main content jumps to the first result.
  • Accessibility help opens Google’s accessibility help.
  • Accessibility feedback opens a form to report a problem.

For more, visit Google Accessibility.

Voice search

  1. Open the Google app.
  2. Tap the microphone.
  3. Ask your question.

To change voice settings, tap your profile picture or initial, then Settings, then Voice and Assistant. There you can:

  • add languages under Language, so you can search in more than one;
  • choose whether answers are spoken aloud;
  • use voice search with a Bluetooth headset.

Settings and shortcuts

Discover

Discover is a feed of stories, articles and videos picked for your interests. It appears on the Google app’s home screen.

  • See topics you follow: tap your profile picture or initial, then Settings, then Manage your interests, then Following.
  • Hide a story’s topic or source: tap More at the bottom right of a story, then “Not interested in” the topic or “Don’t show stories from” the source.
  • Undo that: go to Settings, Manage your interests, Not interested, and uncheck the topic.
  • Turn Discover on or off: use the Discover switch in Settings, under General.

Lock Screen, Control Center and Action Button

You can start Google searches without opening the app first.

Lock Screen widgets (iOS 16 or later):

  1. On the Lock Screen, touch and hold until Customize appears, then tap it and choose Lock Screen.
  2. Tap Add Widgets, then choose Google.
  3. Pick a widget, such as Search, Lens or Voice Search, and tap it.
  4. Tap Close, then Done.

Lock Screen buttons (iOS 18 or later): in Customize, remove the Flashlight or Camera button, tap the plus in the empty circle, find Google, and choose an action such as Lens, Voice Search or Incognito. Tap Done.

Control Center (iOS 18 or later): open Control Center, tap the plus at the top left, tap Add a Control, find Google, and choose an action such as AI Mode or Lens.

Action Button (iPhone 15 Pro and later): open the iPhone Settings app, tap Action Button, choose Shortcut, tap Choose a Shortcut, find Google, and pick an action such as Lens or Voice Search.

Notifications

First, open the iPhone Settings app, tap Notifications, tap Google, and make sure Allow Notifications is on. Then, in the Google app:

  • Follow a topic, like a team or a TV show: search for it and tap the bell to Get Notified.
  • Pin a live score to the Lock Screen: search for a game and tap Pin Live Score. It appears one hour before the game.
  • Stop one topic: tap the Notifications icon at the top right, tap Menu next to the alert, and choose Turn off notifications.
  • Stop a kind of alert, like weather: tap your profile picture or initial, then Settings, Notifications, Notification categories, and turn off that category.
  • Stop all alerts: in Settings, Notifications, turn off Allow Notifications.

Opening settings on iPhone

  1. Open the Google app.
  2. Tap your profile picture or initial.
  3. Tap Settings.

Settings are grouped into Your App Experience, Privacy & Security, Autofill and passwords, and More Info and Support. If you’re signed out, you see only Privacy & Security and More Info and Support. Useful settings include:

  • Default apps: which apps open your search results.
  • Google Translate: offers to translate pages in other languages.
  • Siri Shortcuts: start a search or Lens translation with a phrase you say to Siri.
  • Autoplay video previews: on, off, or only on Wi-Fi.

Widgets inside the app

Small widgets for weather, stocks, and sunrise and sunset sit above Discover on the app’s home screen.

  1. Swipe left on the Weather widget.
  2. Tap Customize.
  3. Choose the widgets you want.

Weather is always shown. If you find no widgets, sign in and make sure Discover is turned on.

Sending feedback

  • On iPhone: tap your profile picture or initial, then Help & feedback, then Send feedback.
  • On Windows: choose your profile picture, then Send Feedback. Type your note, add a screenshot or log if you like, and choose Send.
  • On the web: use the Accessibility feedback link on a results page to report an accessibility problem.
