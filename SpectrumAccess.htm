Spectrum Access Help Guide

This guide gathers what Charter publishes on the web about Spectrum Access, its audio description app, and about accessibility on its services generally. The text was taken from access-app.spectrum.net and spectrum.com on 31 July 2026 and is Charter’s own; nothing has been summarised or rewritten.

Spectrum Access plays audio description through your own headphones while a film plays on the television, so nobody else in the room has to hear it. It is free to anyone, not only to Spectrum customers, and runs on iPhone, iPad and Android. It works in a cinema as well as at home, following the soundtrack through the microphone.

Read this before judging the guide’s size, because it is very small. Almost everything Spectrum Access knows how to tell you is inside the app itself. What Charter publishes on the web is a page describing the app, a page about exploring what is described, a page of reviews from people using it, and its accessibility policy. That is the entire estate: 4 pages, and all of them are here.

It also took two attempts to get even that. Charter’s support site refuses automated readers, so the accessibility pages had to be read through a browser; a first run reading plainly saw a challenge page where the instructions should have been.

The lists of described titles the app carries change constantly and are not gathered here. For a standing reference on what exists to watch and how to reach it – which films and series are described, which cinemas provide description, how to turn it on for each streaming service – see the Audio Description Project guide, which covers that ground properly.

The sections are editorial: there are too few pages for Charter to have grouped them.

Headings run six deep at most. Level one is this title, level two is a section, level three is an article, and anything below that came from inside the article itself.
