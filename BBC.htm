BBC Help Guide

This guide gathers the BBC’s official help for BBC.com and the BBC app: getting started, subscriptions and billing, watching and listening, supported apps and devices, technical problems, newsletters, captions, and data policy. It was taken from the BBC’s own help centre on 28 July 2026.

Finding it took some doing. The BBC’s help is not what a search engine returns for “BBC help” — that search space is thoroughly polluted — so the address came from the BBC itself: help.bbc.com is linked from the foot of every BBC News page as “BBC.com Help and FAQs”. This volume covers the BBC on the web and the BBC app on a phone; there is no Windows program and no Alexa skill.

The BBC keeps three other help estates, none of them included here, each substantial enough to deserve its own volume: BBC iPlayer answers at bbc.co.uk/iplayer/help/questions, BBC Sounds at bbc.co.uk/sounds/help/questions, and reception advice for television and radio at bbc.co.uk/reception — that last one runs to tens of thousands of words on aerials, retuning, DAB and audio description. Ask and any of them can be gathered.

The sections below are the BBC’s own categories, and each article carries the name of the section it sits in. Subscription support is handed off to a ticket form on a separate site, which holds nothing to read, so it is not here.

Headings run three deep: this title, then a category, then an article, with each article’s own subheadings nested beneath. Every article is listed in the table of contents below.

Apps & Devices

Am I able to cast content?

Section: Supported Apps & Devices

At this time, casting from the BBC.com website is not supported. However, AirPlay is supported in the BBC mobile app, allowing you to stream documentaries from your iOS device to compatible Apple TVs.

If you’re experiencing issues connecting via AirPlay or can’t find the device you’d like to stream to, please refer to the Apple AirPlay Support website for troubleshooting guidance.

We’re aware of interest in additional casting options, such as Chromecast, and will update this FAQ if more casting features become available in a future app update.

Why am I unable to install the BBC app from my country or region?

Section: Supported Apps & Devices

If you’re seeing the message ‘App not available in your country or region’ when trying to download the BBC app from the Apple App Store or Google Play Store, this is usually related to the country setting on your app store account.

Why this happens

The BBC app is available in many countries, but if your Apple App Store or Google Play Store account is set to a country where the app is not listed, you may see this message – even if you’re currently living in a supported location.

In some cases, users living outside the UK may have set their App Store/Google Play country to the UK, which would result in the BBC app not being available to them.

What you can do

To resolve this, check and update your account’s country/region setting to match your current country of residence:

  • Apple App Store:
    • Follow the Apple Support guide to change your Apple ID country or region: 0
    • Then download the BBC app from the App Store for iPhone, iPad, and iPod Touch.
  • Google Play Store:
    • Follow the Google Support guide to change your Google Play country: 0
    • Then download the BBC app from Google Play for Android devices.

Note: App availability is based on the country set on your account, not just your physical location.

Still having trouble?

If you’re still unable to install the BBC app after updating your country setting, you can continue using the BBC.comwebsite in your browser on a mobile device as an alternative.

How long are audio programs available for?

Section: Audio

Most of our programs are available to listen to on demand. If you’re listening to a series, it’s best to check that all the episodes are currently available, so you don’t miss out on any parts of the story.

Episode availability is clearly displayed both on BBC.com and on the BBC app.

I’m living in the UK. Why am I getting advertisements on podcasts?

Section: Audio

When you download or play a podcast, we detect whether you’re in the UK, regardless of how you access it. If our systems believe you’re outside the UK at that time, you may hear advertisements.

If you’re actually in the UK but you hear advertisements on the podcasts you download or listen to, have a look at the Why do you think I’m outside the UK? BBC Sounds help page for steps to try to resolve this.

Information on Closure of BBC Sounds

Section: Audio

When will BBC Sounds become unavailable outside the UK?

We can confirm that BBC Sounds closed, outside the UK, on Monday, 21 July 2025.** **

Why did you make this change?

BBC Studios is a commercial subsidiary of the BBC and is focused on bringing our trusted, world-class journalism and storytelling to international audiences. Earlier this year, BBC Studios launched a new audio section on bbc.com/the BBC app (a commercial service) outside the UK.

Revenue generated by these services is returned to the BBC to support its public purpose in the UK.

As part of this announcement, we said we would close BBC Sounds outside the UK – and make it exclusively available to listeners in the UK.

For how long will UK residents be able to use BBC Sounds whilst abroad?

UK listeners who go on holiday will still be able to use the BBC Sounds app abroad for around a month.

Why is the BBC featuring advertising on podcasts?

Section: Audio

We are inserting advertising into BBC podcasts outside the UK as part of the BBC’s wider strategy to commercialize audio content made available to non-license fee paying international audiences.

BBC Studios is tasked with growing commercial revenue to support license fee funds, reducing pressure on the license fee, and ensuring maximum value from our programs and services internationally.

How do I save content to read later?

Section: Basics

Found a news or BBC Feature article that you’d like to come back to and read later? Provided you are signed into your BBC account, you will be able to save articles that will then be associated with your account. This means that you will be able to revisit the article and read it on a different device that you have chosen to sign into.

To Save an article

The ‘Save’ icon is displayed near the top-right of the article, directly below the headline. By clicking on ‘Save’, it should then temporarily go grey whilst the icon now indicates ‘Saved’.

To access your Saved list

To access your list of saved articles, you can then navigate to your account option in the very top-right corner and then select the second item; Saved. Alternatively, you can navigate directly to 0.

To remove an article from the Saved list

Within the saved list, you can click on the ‘Saved’ icon below the article title that you with to remove. Once you click on that item, it will disappear from your saved list within a few seconds.

Please note that this save functionality is not currently available for LIVE News Articles and Sports articles.

How do I enable closed captions for Video Content?

Section: Closed Captions & Subtitles

We can help! We aim to caption as much of our content as we can. The steps for enabling closed captions may vary depending on your preferred device or application.

Here is a step-by-step guide for a few options:

Web Browser

  1. Hover over the video with your cursor to bring up the progress bar & options
  2. Click on the small speech bubble icon in the lower right
  3. Choose the ‘on’ option to switch on closed captions
  4. You can also adjust the size and color of the closed captions by clicking again on the speech bubble and then again on Settings
  5. You will then be presented with a range of closed captions controls which allow you to adjust subtitle size, subtitle color, and background color

Android and iOS

  1. If closed captioning is available for a video or live news stream, a speech bubble or an ‘S’ icon will appear on the top right-hand side or bottom-right-hand side of the video player. If you’re on a mobile or tablet, tap the screen to bring up these options
  2. If a white bar isn’t below the ‘S’ icon, click on the ‘S’ icon to turn on closed captioning If a white bar appears below the ‘S’ icon, closed captioning is already turned on for the episode or movie. Click the ‘S’ icon to turn it off
  3. If the show is playing and you can see other elements of the video player like the volume slider and progress bar, but an ‘S’ icon does not appear on the lower right-hand side of the video player, then closed captioning is unavailable for this episode or movie

Why did my renewal payment fail?

Section: Billing & Payment

If your renewal payment didn’t go through, there could be a few reasons. Some of the most common ones are outlined below.

Expired or incorrect payment details

  • Your credit or debit card may have expired
  • The card details entered (card number, CVV, or expiry date) may be incorrect
  • Your payment method may no longer be valid (for example, if your card was cancelled or replaced)

Insufficient funds

  • There may not have been enough funds in your account at the time of payment
  • Your bank may have declined the payment due to a low balance

Bank or card restrictions

  • Your bank may have flagged the payment for security reasons
  • Some banks require additional authorization for recurring subscription payments
  • Your card may not be enabled for online or international transactions

Subscription or account issues

  • Your subscription may have already been cancelled, in which case you’ll need to re‑subscribe rather than complete a renewal payment
  • There may be an issue or mismatch with your billing details

Temporary technical issues

  • There may be a temporary issue with your bank, card provider, or our payment processor

What can I do to fix this?

If you’ve received an email or pop‑up notification about a failed renewal payment, try the steps below.

  • Check your payment details. Log in to your BBC account and make sure your payment method is up to date. If your card has expired or changed, update your card details in your account.
  • Verify your funds. Ensure there are sufficient funds in your account to cover the renewal payment.
  • Check with your bank. If your payment continues to fail, contact your bank or card provider. They can confirm whether the payment was blocked or declined and whether any additional authorization is required.
  • Try a different payment method. If your usual payment method isn’t working and contacting your bank isn’t convenient, you can try using a different payment method.
  • Contact our Customer Care team. If you’ve tried all of the steps above and still need help, our Customer Care team will be happy to assist you.

Once your payment details are updated and active (whether it’s the same card or a new payment method), our system will automatically retry the charge within a few days. Please note that our Customer Care team can’t manually trigger the payment, so make sure there are enough funds available to avoid further issues.

Help fixing problems with playing audio through the app

Section: Problems Listening to Podcasts / Radio

If you’re having trouble playing audio through the BBC app, please try the following steps:

Check your internet connection

Ensure you’re connected to a stable Wi-Fi or mobile data network. If possible, switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data to test the connection.

Restart the app

Close the app completely and reopen it to see if that resolves the issue.

Update the app

Make sure the app is updated to the latest version. Go to your app store to check for updates.

Restart your device

Sometimes restarting your phone or tablet can help resolve playback issues.

Clear the app cache (Android only)

If you’re using an Android device, clearing the app cache can sometimes help. Go to your device settings, find the app in the list, and select “Clear Cache.”

Reinstall the app

If the issue persists, uninstall and reinstall the app. This can resolve any software glitches causing the problem.

If none of these steps solve the issue, you can also try accessing the website through your mobile device’s browser as an alternative.

If the problem still continues, please use our webform by clicking Contact Technical Support for further assistance.

Help fixing problems with playing audio through the website

Section: Problems Listening to Podcasts / Radio

If you’re having trouble playing audio through BBC.com, please try the following steps:

Check your internet connection

Make sure you’re connected to a stable network, and try reloading the page.

Refresh the page

Sometimes, simply refreshing the webpage can fix the issue.

Clear your browser cache

Clearing the cache can help resolve playback problems. Instructions for clearing your cache can usually be found in your browser’s settings.

Update your browser

Ensure your browser is up to date with the latest version, as outdated browsers may cause playback issues.

Try a different browser or device

If the issue persists, test playback on a different browser or device to see if it works there.

Disable browser extensions

Certain browser extensions can interfere with playback. Try disabling them temporarily to see if that resolves the issue.

If these steps don’t resolve the problem, please use our webform by clicking Contact Technical Support for further assistance.

I forgot my password, how can I reset it?

Section: Password & Login Issues

If you’ve forgotten your password for your BBC account, you’ll need to reset it. Here’s how to do that:

  1. Go to the I’ve forgotten my password page.
  2. When requested, enter the email you used to register with the BBC. (If you registered with a fake email, you won’t be able to change your password, so you’ll need to register for a new account.)
  3. We’ll then send you an email with a link to reset your password. You might need to check your junk or spam folder (if it doesn’t come through, follow the steps above again).
  4. When you receive that email, click the link, and you’ll be taken to a page where you can create a new password.

Be sure to remember your new password for next time, but don’t write it down anywhere in case someone finds it.

I’m having problems with my password

Section: Password & Login Issues

Remember, your password needs to include:

  • Eight or more characters
  • At least one letter
  • At least one number or symbol

When you’re picking a password, we will alert you if it’s been part of a data breach on the internet – in other words, if it’s on a list that hackers can access. Choosing a different password will help to keep your account secure. Your password stays hidden, so no one sees it during this process.

I’m in the UK. Why am I presented with BBC Audio instead of BBC Sounds?

Section: Problems Listening to Podcasts / Radio

It is likely you are using a VPN (Virtual Private Network) and are accessing BBC.com, which is intended for audiences outside the UK. If you disable your VPN, you should be redirected back to bbc.co.uk, intended for UK users.

If you enter the wrong password too many times, you’ll be locked out for 30 minutes. This is to keep your account secure, so we won’t be able to help in the meantime.

To unlock your account - you’ll need to wait at least 30 minutes and then try again.

Uninstall and Reinstall your BBC App

First, remove the app, then follow our steps to install (link to BBCS FAQ) the BBC app again.

Refer To Your Device Manufacturer for More

Your device manufacturer can offer more help, especially if it’s an issue with your device’s software, firmware, or operating system.

Check BBC.com’s Status

We do our very best to avoid any disruptions to the BBC.com and BBC app. However, if there is a technical glitch, we’ll attempt to acknowledge it at the top of our Help Center.

Why do I need JavaScript and how do I enable it? (Section: Problems Listening to Podcasts / Radio)

Section: Problems Listening to Podcasts / Radio

JavaScript is a common computer language used by many websites, including BBC.com, in order to run correctly. If you do not have JavaScript enabled, you will be unable to stream audio and video content, and some other features may not work as expected.

If you already have JavaScript enabled but it is not recognized, try refreshing the page or clearing your browser cache

How to enable JavaScript

Chrome on Desktop (on mobile, it is always enabled)
  1. Go to Chrome Menu (three dots in the upper right corner)
  2. Click on ‘Settings’
  3. Click ‘Privacy and Security’
  4. Select ’Site settings’
  5. Scroll and select ’JavaScript’
  6. Select ‘Sites can use JavaScript’
Safari on Mac OSX
  1. Launch Safari from your desktop or Dock
  2. From the main menu at the top of your screen, click ‘Safari’ and then ‘Preferences’
  3. Click the Security icon
  4. In the ‘Web content’ section, ensure that the ‘Enable JavaScript’ check box has been checked
  5. Close this window
Safari on iOS
  1. From a Home screen on your iPhone or iPad, navigate to ‘Settings’, then choose ‘Safari’
  2. Tap ‘Advanced’. You may need to scroll down to find this
  3. Tap the ‘JavaScript’ switch to turn on or off
Microsoft Edge
  1. Click the three dots in the upper right-hand corner of the browser window
  2. Select ‘Settings’ from the drop-down menu
  3. Select ‘Cookies and site permissions’ from the left-hand menu
  4. Underneath the ‘All Permissions’ heading, select the ‘JavaScript’ menu item
  5. Toggle the ‘Allowed’ setting to the right so that the toggle turns blue
  6. Close the settings window and refresh your browser
Firefox
  1. In the URL bar, type about:config
  2. In the search box, type javascript
  3. Check ‘javascript.enabled’ is set to ‘true’
