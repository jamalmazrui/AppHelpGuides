Best Practices for using Committee Tracking Tools

Researching through Committee Coverage

When clicking into ‘Committee Coverage’ from the ‘Bills’ page, you’ll see two distinct types of content: hearing coverage and markup/vote coverage.

Hearing coverage includes agendas for scheduled hearings, and is usually much shorter.

Markup and vote coverage contains in-depth summaries of actions that occurred and breakdowns of votes taken, and is therefore usually much longer.

Leverage Specific Search Engines

Committee Coverage and committee reports both have their own individual search engines that allow you to dive deep and find the exact content you’re looking for. Search by keyword, bill, date or committee to find coverage of any specific hearing or markup.

Saving Info to Return to Later

If you’d like to save a Committee Coverage page or any other document to come back to later, simply click ‘Save to Projects’ on the top toolbar near the righthand side. This will allow you to store content in a folder to return to later, if you’d like to see any new recent actions, amendments and more.

Head to ‘Review’ → ‘My Projects’ to access anything you save later on.

Is Committee Coverage different from committee reports?

Yes! Committee Coverage is proprietary to CQ, meaning that the coverage is written by CQ’s research and editorial teams. Therefore, the information is released much faster than committee reports - usually within 24 hours.

You can also access coverage of hearings and markups going back to the 101st Congress (1989) by using the search filter by date.

Markups are done by Senate and House committees. They publicly release what happened during a markup in a committee report. This is a publicly available report available to everyone. Additionally, CQ provides committee reports and their own in-house markup coverage (CQ Committee Coverage) which means we send our own reporters to markups to also cover the event. This offers a more comprehensive summary of what happened during a markup, includes amendments, vote breakdowns and often comes out earlier than committee reports.

Overview

Unique and proprietary to CQ, House Action Reports is a newsletter that includes five distinct document types specifically designed to prepare House members and their staff for the day ahead and the week ahead on the House floor. The newsletter includes everything from Floor schedules to summaries of bills to be considered and more.

Content Included

House Action Reports encompasses five distinct document types sent to your inbox. They include:

Legislative Day

Lists the day’s legislative agenda for the House floor, including a list of all amendments and motions (with summaries) expected to be offered, as well as the rules under which each measure will be considered.

Legislative Week

Published on Friday nights, Legislative Week summarizes and analyzes every bill scheduled for consideration in the coming week, including updates for any items that get scheduled mid-week.

Fact Sheets

CQ’s comprehensive coverage of the biggest, most complex bills (appropriations, omnibuses, overhauls and more) and break them down into intelligible analysis.

Floor Summaries

Highlights and explains changes to legislation agreed to during debate and consideration, including summaries of each amendment’s impact on the legislation, sponsor(s) and the vote breakdown.

Conference Summaries

A section-by-section breakdown and analysis of the changes made to legislation by the conference committee, including pertinent comparisons to the original House bill.

Budget Tracker

The Budget Tracker is the first thing you’ll see when you click into CQ Budget. Here, you can find where every budget bill stands in the process as well as links to additional details on that bill through two different views.

Bear in mind that you can click into any stage of the process for each budget bill to see more details on that action or step as well as access any associated bill pages listed on the left hand side

The “At a Glance” view offers a simplified, streamlined version of the detailed budget tracker. Here, you can see where each bill is from a higher level - like in the house, House, Senate, Conference or President’s desk - through this color-coded key.

CQ VoteWatch Methodology

CQ combines its comprehensive database of congressional floor votes with unmatched analytic coverage of legislative activity to produce a new way of examining voting patterns. What had been, for decades, an annual review of which members back their party, and the president is now updated daily, allowing unprecedented analysis of lawmakers’ voting habits.

CQ Vote Watch Methodology

CQ Roll Call (previously Congressional Quarterly) has analyzed voting patterns of members of Congress since 1945. The three current studies — presidential support, party unity, and voting participation — have been conducted consistently since 1953. This is how they are done:

Selecting votes

CQ Roll Call bases its vote studies on all floor votes for which senators and House members were asked to vote “yea” or “nay.”

Party Unity

Party unity votes are any “yea” or “nay” roll call votes that have a majority of one party opposing a majority of the other party.

Presidential Support

CQ Roll Call editors select presidential support votes yearly based on the president’s or authorized spokespeople’s explicit statements. Support scores show the percentage of roll call votes on which members of Congress voted in agreement with the president’s position.

Individual scores:

Member scores are based only on the votes each cast. This makes individual support and opposition scores total 100 percent. The same method is used to identify the leading scorers.

Overall scores

To be comparable to individual member scores, calculations of average scores by chamber and party are similarly based on all relevant votes cast. Members of the chamber or caucus missing votes do not lower the average, and the support and opposition scores will total 100 percent. This breaks from the magazine’s longstanding tradition of basing the chamber and party averages on all eligible votes. So, the overall scores here are not comparable to those found in CQ Weekly or CQ Almanac.

Rounding

Rounding does not raise any score to 100 percent or reduce any score to zero.

Boolean Operators and Keywords

If you wish to get even more granular in your searches, using CQ’s Boolean Operators can help. Using boolean means combining certain words, phrases and symbols with keywords of your choice to drill down to specific, relevant search results.

Linked above is the guide to CQ’s Boolean, which is explained in-depth below, but you can always access it by clicking the link on the Advanced Search page (highlighted below).

What are the Boolean Operators?

Enter:

To Receive Documents that Contain:

“juvenile delinquent”

this phrase exactly as you entered it

juvenile delinquent

both words, together or apart, in any order. NOTE: juvenile and delinquent is equivalent to juvenile delinquent and returns the same results

juvenile or delinquent

either word, but not necessarily both. NOTE: juvenile, delinquent is equivalent to juvenile or delinquent and returns the same results

juvenile not delinquent

the first but not the second word

juvenile delinquent

both words, within about 600 words of each other, in any order

juvenile <near/5> delinquent

both words within 5 (or any number you specify) words of each other, in any order

juvenile (criminal or delinquent)

either of the words in parentheses follows the other word, as a phrase

juvenile and (criminal or delinquent)

either of the words in parentheses, and also the other word. The parentheses take precedence over the “and” connector

juvenile and criminal or delinquent

both the word juvenile and the word criminal, or the word delinquent. The “and” connector takes precedence over the “or” connector

How do I use them?

Once you’ve selected all the criteria you’d like for your search, make sure you have one open for “Keywords.” This is where you will type your chosen keywords and Boolean operators.

Note that CQ Federal will expand any keywords used into all word endings, unless otherwise instructed. For example, “boat” would include “boats” and “boating.”

If you’d like to have the search yield only that word in the form you type it, for example, just “boat,” adding “+” prior to the word, “+boat,” will look only for the word as written.

Bear in mind that precedence defines the order in which the elements of a “words or phrases” search are evaluated. In CQ Federal, the order of precedence is:

  • parentheses ( )
  • and
  • or
  • not The best way to understand Boolean precedence is through the lens of Order of Operations in math, such as equations in parentheses being done first.
Example Boolean Searches

Here are some common searches that help illustrate use cases for Boolean.

Enter:

To Receive Documents that Contain:

juvenile <near/5> (delinquent, criminal)

Entering “delinquent” and “criminal” in parentheses separated by a comma tells the system to find juvenile within five words of either delinquent or criminal.

juvenile <near/8> (delinquent, +criminal, felony), minor <near/8> (delinquent, +criminal, felony)

This tells the system to find the word juvenile within eight words of either delinquent, only the word criminal, or felony, or to find the word minor within eight words of either delinquent, criminal or felony.

“juvenile (delinquent, criminal)”

Using quotes tells the system to search for that specific phrase. This example would tell the system to find either “juvenile delinquent” or “juvenile criminal.”

Once you’ve conducted your Boolean search, hit “Search” to see your results. You can filter through your results, but most importantly, by clicking into your chosen result, you can jump to the keywords you were searching for within the document.

  • Statements of Administration Policy (SAPs): The Office of Management and Budget (OMB) issues statements outlining the administration’s official position on major or controversial legislation pending before Congress. These SAPs offer a reliable guide to the president’s support for or opposition to these bills and resolutions.
  • Office of Management and Budget (OMB) Documents: A part of the Executive Office of the President, OMB’s predominant mission is to assist the president in overseeing the preparation of the federal budget and to supervise its administration in Executive Branch agencies. With these releases, you’ll get critical insight into the president’s spending plans. Plus, OMB evaluates the effectiveness of agency programs, policies and procedures, assesses competing funding demands among agencies and sets funding priorities. OMB ensures that agency reports, rules, testimony and proposed legislation are consistent with the president’s budget and with administration policies.
  • Agency Documents: Documents from agencies that include, but are not limited to, press releases, OIG reports, and reports to Congress.
  • Judicial Documents: Indictments, briefs, court orders, opinions and decisions, and other documents filed in major federal and state court cases involving constitutional issues, campaigns, elections, regulations and terrorism.
  • Other Documents: Documents from NGOs, lobbying coalitions, newsworthy letters and trade associations.

What is LawTrack?

LawTrack is a unique CQ feature that allows you to see how pending federal bills will affect existing federal laws, making it easy to identify and track potential changes to US law or code.

Not only does LawTrack offer a summary of potential changes to US law or code, but it also allows you to drill down into the ramifications of a pending bill line-by-line in the section of the code or U.S. public law it would modify.

How can I use LawTrack?

Example

Say, for example, we wanted to understand how PL 116-92 (the FY2020 National Defense Authorization Act) may be impacted by pending legislation. After searching for PL 116-92 in LawTrack, we’ll find:

  • Bill details, such as the name, title, sponsors, cosponsors and introduction date.

  • A summary of the pending bills that would affect this law and the number of changes.

  • Public laws that changed this law since first enacted, and laws this law has changed.

  • By clicking yellow text to the right of the pending bills that may affect PL-116-92, you can see the exact impact, line-by-line, that the bill will have on the law you’ve selected (as shown below). Or, if we were looking for the impact a pending bill such as HR3684, may have on current laws or US code, we would find:

  • Bill details.

  • A summary of the number of laws and number of changes this bill may have if passed.

  • Details on the specific sections of the code or U.S. public law it would modify, line-by-line.

Conducting a Search

LawTrack allows you to search by the following criteria:

  • Public Law or US Code: Type in name or number of a law or US code to see how it may be affected by pending legislation.
  • Words in Law Text: Type in keywords you wish to locate in law text to see how those laws may be affected by pending legislation.
  • Bills: Type the name or number of the bill(s) you’re tracking to see what current laws or code they may affect. When using the LawTrack search fields, wait to let the system pull your results before clicking enter. A dropdown will appear with your selected law, code, word or bill - when it does, click it to select it for your search.
Finding the Impact

Once you’ve conducted a search, click “Find Impact” and take a look at the summary section. This includes bill details, the overall number changes a pending bill may have on an existing law, and vice versa.

Then, you can dive deeper. Click the orange text to view the text of a bill, past or pending, and the specific section of the code or U.S. public law it would modify line-by-line.

Setting an Alert

To track pending bills that may affect laws or US code you care about, follow these steps:

  • Navigate to ‘Create → Alert’ in the upper left hand corner.
  • Select ‘Bills’ as your alert type; click continue.
  • Select “Alert me to bills I might want to track” as the type of alert you’d like to receive; click continue.
  • Select ‘Laws’ under “Find bills by one of the following.”
  • Enter the law number you wish to track; click continue.
  • Enter an alert name, delivery frequency, and click continue. All set!
