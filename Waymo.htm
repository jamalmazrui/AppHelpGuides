Waymo Rider Help Guide

This guide gathers Waymo’s official help for riders of its fully autonomous ride-hailing service: getting started, how the service works, the cars themselves, your account, the rules of riding, and how to get help during or after a trip. It was taken from Waymo’s own help centre on 28 July 2026.

Waymo runs its help on Google’s help platform, where articles are numbered rather than named, and this guide follows Waymo’s own arrangement — the sections below are the topics Waymo files its articles under. A few articles it does not file under any topic are gathered at the end, along with the pages from Waymo’s own website.

Waymo is a phone app. There is no Windows program, no web application for riding, and no Alexa skill, so the usual four-surface scope narrows to the app.

Worth knowing what is in here for a blind rider: Waymo publishes guidance on accessibility features in the app and the car, on travelling with a guide dog or service animal, on how to find the car and identify it when it arrives, and on speaking to Rider Support from inside the vehicle. Those sit in the service and cars sections.

The community forum is not included — that is other riders talking, not Waymo’s own guidance. Where Waymo served the same article at more than one address, the fullest copy was kept.

Accessibility features in the app
At pickup
Wayfinding

Add stops

During a stop, please take your stuff with you. A different car will arrive for your next pickup.

At a glance

Adding stops has a number of benefits:

  • Change plans on the go
  • Continue your trip after picking up or dropping off a friend
  • Plan all your stops

Important things to know:

  • We can’t keep the same car for you, so** during a stop, please take your stuff with you**.
  • A different car will arrive for the next part of your trip.
  • Please don’t leave items (or passengers) unattended in the vehicle.
Creating a trip with multiple stops

Creating a trip with more than one stop is easy and allows you to go to multiple destinations while out and about, like doing some shopping, grabbing a curbside meal, dropping off a friend at home, or grabbing a coffee on the way to work.

You can:
  • Make up to 4 stops
  • Use a trip with stops for round trips
  • Spend 5 min inside the car while at a stop. If exiting, you can spend up to 30 min at a stop before your trip automatically ends.

Waymo will save your itinerary until you end the trip, or for a max of 30 minutes.

You can’t:
  • Have the car pick up someone else before you get in
  • Go through a drive-thru
During a stop
You can:
  • Pick up or drop off someone and continue to the next stop
  • Stay in the car for up to 5 minutes while dropping off or picking up someone
    • Wait times aren’t guaranteed and vary by service area
    • Don’t leave children alone in the car
  • Get out and stay at your destination for up to 30 minutes
You can’t:
  • Leave your belongings or an additional passenger in the car
    • The car will drive away and serve other passengers.
  • Forget to press the big blue button in the app to get the car to come back to you
    • The button is how the car knows you’re ready to resume your trip.
Pickup
  • Depending on traffic and road conditions (or if you adjust the pickup spot), the car will pick you up at the same place you were dropped off
  • If adjustments for pickup spots are available, there will be a little box on the map next to your pickup spot that says “Adjust.” Tap it and choose from other nearby options.
Pricing

  • Waymo charges a small fee for each additional stop.

  • Even if your final dropoff doesn’t change, adding or deleting stops (or re-arranging the order of stops) may alter the distance and duration and therefore the price of the trip.

    • Program or promo specific rules may apply.

  • Discounts may be used when adding stops, as long as the discount is valid and applies to any leg of the trip.

    • Please check the terms and conditions of the promotion for more details.

  • After 30 minutes, your itinerary will no longer be saved, and you will have to pay the base fare for your next trip.

Choose your pickup time

Let us know when to pick you up. Just enter your trip details, choose your desired pickup time, and we’ll see you then!

Does the car come exactly at the stated time?

Most of the time, yes. But arrival could be within 5 minutes of the stated time.

In most cases, if the car comes early it won’t leave before your originally-chosen pickup time. However, the car might have to circle the block and come back if it arrives early.

Can I edit a ride that’s set for later?

Sure! You can use the app to adjust your pickup and dropoff location, and change the pickup time up until your vehicle is on its way. Just remember that if you make changes, the price of the trip will most likely update.

Can I cancel a ride that’s set for later?

You can! Though please note that a standard cancellation fee may apply if you cancel when the car is already on its way to you.

Is the price different when I choose a pickup time?

The price may be different than the same trip booked immediately. You’ll always know the price of your trip before you book though and that won’t change unless you edit your trip.

Will the price change after I choose a time?

We determine your trip’s fare when you book it. Once scheduled, the price for your ride will only change if you edit your trip.

Which times are available?

At this time, you can choose a pickup time from 5 minutes up to 1 hour out.

Fully autonomous FAQs

Our autonomous driving system will be doing the driving, so once the vehicle arrives you’ll unlock the doors, hop in, buckle up, and press Start ride to get moving. You can call us any time through the car or app.

Waymo’s autonomous driving system is designed to be cautious and defensive, so some of its movements will feel different than a human driver’s. For example, you might notice more sudden braking than you’re used to. As with any car trip, please wear your seatbelt at all times.

How does the car drive?

Waymo’s autonomous driving technology uses a detailed 3D map that helps it know where it is in the world and decide the route for your trip; all on its own.

The car’s sensors are constantly scanning for others on the road, like vehicles, cyclists and pedestrians, and that information is processed in real time by the car’s software to make smart driving decisions.

Learn more about How our cars drive.

Are fully autonomous cars safe?

Yes. We’ve been testing and refining our technology for over a decade. Our cars have millions of miles of experience behind them; that’s equal to hundreds of years of human driving experience!

Routing

Our cars use 3D mapping technology and sensors to get both a static and play-by-play picture of what’s happening on the road, and to plan your route accordingly.

Waymo cars map your route ahead of time

Do you plug your destination into a navigation app at the beginning of car trips? Waymo cars do the exact same thing. Our maps highlight information like road profiles, traffic lights, and other fixed road features, to help every Waymo car plan its route before your trip starts.

Once you’ve requested a ride, two lines will show up on the map. The green line shows your car’s planned route to get to you. The blue line shows the planned route to your destination.

Waymo cars may adjust to unexpected changes on the road

You might sometimes notice your route changing or becoming a little longer. Like any human driver, our cars are flexible enough to change course if they encounter a situation that’s difficult to navigate.

Waymo chooses routes based on current road conditions, such as construction zones, territory boundaries, traffic, and more. No matter which way the vehicle goes, you’ll only be charged for the fastest route.

How do cars know to change their route?

Waymo cars use sensors and software to scan their surroundings for potential road blockages and to predict the actions of nearby cars and pedestrians.

Waymo cars occasionally need to reroute

Our maps are designed for our autonomous vehicles. The best route for a Waymo car might be different than the route a human driver would take.

For example, Waymo cars might need to reroute if they:

  • Want to avoid a busy intersection
  • Miss a turn or aren’t able to join a shared turn lane because of traffic

How our cars drive: Sense, Solve, Go

The Waymo Driver sees and understands the world through a number of sensors, including cameras outside the car, and powerful artificial intelligence (AI) software — a process that we call “Sense, Solve, Go.”

Sense

Our sensors:

  • Lidar
  • Cameras
  • Radar

Here’s a look at each of the sensors that allow the car to safely navigate the complexities of driving while getting people and things where they need to go.

Lidar

As one of the Waymo Driver’s most powerful sensors, lidar paints a 3D picture of its surroundings, allowing us to measure the size and distance of objects 360 degrees around our vehicle and up to 300 meters away.

Cameras

Our vision system is designed to capture more detail and provide sharper images in the toughest driving environments. With our 5th-generation Waymo Driver, our cameras provide a 360 vision system that allows us to identify important details like pedestrians and stop signs greater than 500 meters away.

Fun fact: If you’re riding in our car and hear a whirring sound on the roof, it’s likely our sensors being cleaned! We use cleaning tools like wipers and nozzles to get rid of dust, grime, and debris.

Radar

Radar adds to both lidar and cameras with its unique ability to see and measure an object’s speed and direction, helping us to test even in tough weather conditions such as rain, fog, and snow.

Solve

The Waymo Driver uses all that data to calculate a safe routewith the help of AI and machine learning, allowing the car to respond in real time to the evolving traffic environment. In other words, the Waymo Driver is able to capture driving data that is used to continuously improve its performance.

Go

Once the Waymo Driver has determined a path to take, our advanced motion control system allows for smooth acceleration and braking in heavy traffic.

The Waymo Driver is designed to be cautious and defensive. Learn how we handle uncommoninterruptions to rides likepulling over, collisions, and security events.

Ride interruptions

If your autonomous car stops for any reason, and can’t continue on its own, our Support team will contact you and Roadside Assistance will be dispatched to help you.

Why would my autonomous ride be interrupted?

Waymo’s autonomous driving system is designed to be cautious and defensive. Though unlikely, your car may need to safely come to a stop if it decides that’s the best thing to do.

Sometimes, the car just needs a moment to decide how to proceed. It may be able to determine the best course of action and start moving again on its own.

Your ride could also be interrupted if there’s a mechanical issue, a flat tire, or a collision. No matter the reason, we’ll reach out immediately and send someone to help if needed.

What to expect if your ride is interrupted
What should I do?

If your ride stops unexpectedly, stay in the car with your seat belt fastened. In the event of a collision, please stay in the car with your seat belt fastened, unless there is an urgent need to exit. Please don’t touch any controls inside the car, like the steering wheel, accelerator, or brake pedal.

Support will reach out soon, but you can always get help using the in-car screen or the app at any time.

What will Support do?

Support will immediately escalate the issue to get you moving again as soon as possible.

  1. You’ll get a call from us through the in-car speakers or your phone.
  2. We’ll let you know if the car can continue on its own.
  3. If it can’t, Support will send Waymo Roadside Assistance and will stay on the line with you until they arrive.
What will Roadside Assistance do?

If we send Waymo Roadside Assistance, you’ll see this information on the in-car screen:

  • Map location of the Roadside Assistance vehicle
  • Picture and name of the Roadside Assistance specialist

Note: The team member who assists you will be wearing a yellow safety vest.

Here’s what to expect with Roadside Assistance:

  1. Roadside Assistance team members will reach you within a few minutes and park nearby.
  2. A team member will unlock the Waymo car’s doors from outside (you might hear a clicking sound when they do this).
  3. They’ll introduce themselves and hop in the driver’s seat.
  4. Then they’ll drive the Waymo car to your dropoff spot (no need to tell them the address).

What to do after a collision

We want to make sure you know exactly what to do if you experience a collision with a Waymo vehicle — whether you’re a rider, witness, or involved party.

For riders

If you’re riding in a Waymo vehicle and experience a collision:

  1. Stay calm and prioritize your safety. If you’re injured, call 911 immediately. You can also contact Support for assistance. When the Waymo vehicle detects a collision, the vehicle notifies Waymo’s Support team, who then reviews and confirms the crash.
  2. Please note, depending on the severity of the crash and other factors, the Waymo vehicle may continue driving after a collision occurs to reach a safe spot to stop.
  3. If you’re a teen rider using a teen account, we may contact your parent/legal guardian in the event of an issue or incident.
  4. If you feel it’s important, you can exchange information or document the collision. Our on site support will also document it to ensure insurance details are captured. Our main focus is getting you to your destination safely, and we will help you hail another vehicle to get there.
  5. We provide limited medical coverage for our riders, regardless of who’s at fault or whether you have additional coverage.
For witnesses or other involved parties

If you’re involved in a collision with a Waymo vehicle:

  1. If anyone is injured, call 911 immediately.
  2. Follow the collision guidance outlined by your local Department of Transportation, including moving to a safe location to exchange information.
    • The Waymo vehicle may continue driving to reach a safe spot to stop, but look for our dome light signal, which indicates that the Waymo Driver is aware an event has occurred.
  3. You can easily exchange collision information with Waymo using one of the following options:
    • Call us at 1-844-263-9885 to notify us of a collision
    • Fill out a Collision form online
    • Scan the QR code on either front door of the vehicle
    • Contact insurance@waymo.com and/or download our insurance cards
    • Wait for a Waymo roadside assistance representative to arrive on the scene
  4. For your records and potential claims, take photos of the collision scene once it’s safe.
  5. Once you’ve exchanged information with us, you don’t need to wait for a Waymo representative to arrive. Our team will follow up with you if needed.
What happens next?

A member of Waymo’s risk and insurance team reviews every collision to determine the necessary actions. Regardless of the event, we want to know what happened so we can help resolve claims quickly.

If you have any concerns or questions after the incident, contact Waymo support through our app or get in touch with us at waymo.com/contact. You can also reach out to our Risk & Insurance team at insurance@waymo.com with questions and next steps related to potential claims.

The types of information we collect include:

  • Billing information (including payment method and billing address)
  • Preferred trip locations (including names you provide for those locations)
  • Information provided to our Rider Support (including via voice, email, or chat)

For creating an account and fulfilling your trips the provision of your name, contact and account details, and billing information is mandatory. If this information is not provided we will not be able to open an account for you or fulfill trips. The provision of other information is optional.

Information we gather from your use of our products and services

In addition to the information you give us, we collect information about you when you use or interact with our products and services, including:

  • Usage data: We collect usage data that includes trip history, buttons or links you click on our mobile app, views, access times, and interactions with content and our apps, referrer URL, in-vehicle interfaces, wait times for our vehicles, and other actions you take with our products and services
  • Location data: We collect information about your location in a few different ways. When you take a trip, we collect the pickup and drop-off location and details about the vehicle’s route. With your permission, we also collect the precise location of your mobile device. Device location information is always collected during a trip or whenever you use the app with location services enabled (even when you are not on a trip). Your location may be determined by GPS and other data on your device, depending in part on your device and app settings. We also collect your approximate location based on the IP address of the device you use to access our mobile app.
  • Cameras and microphones: We record video inside the vehicle during trips. We only record audio during active voice calls with Rider Support or when you actively choose to enable microphones inside the car. We also use external cameras to detect important things in the world around the vehicle to help operate our services. We also use external microphones to detect police and emergency vehicle sirens. We have implemented technical and procedural safeguards designed to limit the collection of any human voice data from these microphones. Learn more about our camera and microphone use.
  • Device information: We collect information about the device you use to access our app, including hardware model, operating system version, unique device identifiers, device event information (such as crashes, hardware settings, and the date and time of your request), and wireless and/or mobile network information (including phone number).
  • Log file data: We collect log files when you use our mobile app, which include the type of browser you use, access times, pages viewed, IP address, and referrer URL of your request.
  • If you participate in Waymo surveys or research panels: You can choose to provide demographic information, such as your age, gender, language, ethnicity (where allowed by local law), size of household, disability status, education details, and employment information. Providing us with this information is completely voluntary and not required to access our products or services.
  • Data collection technologies: We and our service providers use different technologies to collect information from our mobile apps and emails, including cookies and web beacons. Cookies are small data files stored on your hard drive or in device memory that help us improve our websites and your experience, see which areas and features of our websites are popular, and count visits. Web beacons (also known as “pixel tags” or “clear GIFs”) are electronic images that may be used in our products and services or emails and help deliver cookies, count visits, and understand usage and campaign effectiveness. For more information about cookies and how to disable them, see “Your Choices” above.
  • Vehicle sensor information: Our vehicles have various sensors designed to enable them to operate autonomously. As well as the external cameras and microphones mentioned above, the sensors also include LiDAR and radar sensors. For more information about the data our vehicle sensors collect, please visit our Help Center.
  • Inferences: We draw inferences from the above such as understanding frequently visited destinations, determining new or optimal drop-off and pickup locations, suggesting destinations, and personalizing your experience, including any ads or other information you see when you use our products and services.
