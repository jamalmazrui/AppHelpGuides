Guide help

This guide holds what the makers of Guide publish about it, gathered from guideinteraction.com on 14 November 2026. Guide is a Windows program for people who are blind or have low vision: you tell it, by voice or keyboard, what you want done on the screen, and it describes what is there and clicks, types or plays for you. It is meant to work beside a screen reader such as JAWS or NVDA, not to replace one. It is a paid subscription with a free trial.

The site is small, so this guide is too. The support pages are the help itself: settings, voices, keyboard shortcuts, the two assist modes and what to do when Guide cannot act in a program. The questions on Guide’s front page are articles of their own. The release posts are kept in their own category, because they are where Guide says what each version added.

This is a different product from the recruiting service also called Guide.

Questions people ask

Can I download Guide right now?

Yes, you can download Guide from this page. Additionally, we release regular updates to improve performance and refine Guide’s responses.

Can I still use my existing screen reader with Guide?

Absolutely. Guide is designed to complement, not replace, existing assistive tools. You can use it alongside JAWS, NVDA, or other screen readers.

Do I need special hardware or additional software?

Guide runs on Windows 10 or later. You do not need any special hardware beyond a standard computer.

Do I need to enter my credit card to start the free trial?

Yes — you’ll be asked to enter your credit card when starting the free trial. But you won’t be charged until the 7-day trial ends. You can cancel anytime before then and won’t be billed.

How do I cancel my subscription?

You can cancel your subscription at any time through the Guide app. Navigate to the Account tab in the tab control, and from there click Manage Subscription. This will open a secure Stripe web page where you can then click Cancel Subscription. Your access will continue until the end of the current billing or free trial cycle. If you hit the assist limit in the free trial, your plan has been canceled and you will not be charged. If all else fails, email support@guideinteraction.com from the email address you used to sign up, and we’ll help you out.

How do I report issues or request new features?

We love feedback! You can submit bug reports or feature suggestions by contacting our support team. We regularly incorporate user requests into our roadmap to ensure Guide evolves with the community’s needs.

How is Guide different from a traditional screen reader?

Guide is not a screen reader in the traditional sense. Guide uses AI to identify and describe elements contextually. It can take actions such as clicking buttons or playing a video with simple commands, making navigation of inaccessible content easier.

How much does Guide cost?

Guide is free to try for 7 days, with 30 assists in that timeframe. After your trial, you can choose from three plans: Guide Lite at $8/month for 15 assists (great for occasional use), Guide Standard at $15/month for 30 assists, or Guide Plus at $25/month for 100 assists. Standard and Plus also include Expert mode, a more powerful AI model for complex, multi-step tasks.

Is Guide safe to use? What does it have access to?

Yes, security and transparency are top priorities for Guide. Because Guide helps navigate inaccessible content, it uses AI and automation to take screenshots and simulate mouse and keyboard actions on your Windows computer.

Screenshots are securely sent to Anthropic’s Claude, an advanced AI model, to interpret what’s on your screen and suggest or perform actions. These screenshots are not stored or used for training, and Guide does not access your files, record audio, or monitor activity outside of what’s visually on screen.

What countries is Guide available in?

Guide works worldwide, but our services are based in the United States so users outside North America may experience higher latency or voice choppiness. Many people find it runs just fine for their needs.

What is a Guide assist?

An assist is what we call Guide performing a command or answering a question for you. For example, if you ask Guide to “Open Chrome and go to cnn.com,” that counts as one assist. If you then say, “Read the headlines,” that’s another assist. Each time Guide helps you with a task or provides information, it uses an assist from your monthly allowance.

What is Guide?

Guide is a desktop application designed for individuals with blindness or low vision. It leverages AI to describe on-screen content and execute commands through voice or keyboard shortcuts. Our goal is to simplify computer use and unlock access to digital tools for everyone.

What is Guide’s mission?

Guide exists to make computers more accessible and empower people with blindness or low vision to be more independent. We believe everyone deserves the same opportunities to work, learn, and connect in a digital world.

What kinds of use cases is Guide best suited for?

Guide can help with a variety of tasks. It is generally well suited to targetted actions in inaccessible content or software, rather than complete workflows. Some real world examples from our community include:

  • Switching language from Chinese to English in Steam when it somehow got changed
  • Reading a restaurant menu from an inaccessible PDF
  • Entering answers in inaccessible text fields on an online learning platform
  • Clicking on a non-labeled Play or Unmute button in a video player
  • Bypassing keyboard traps in inaccessible websites

What languages does Guide support?

The Guide app interface is in English, but Guide can respond to you in 32 languages. Language support varies by tier:

Best experience: English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Arabic, Chinese (Mandarin), Japanese, Korean, Hindi, and Indonesian.

Supported, but may vary: Dutch, Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, Finnish, Polish, Czech, Slovak, Romanian, Hungarian, Bulgarian, Croatian, Greek, Turkish, Russian, Ukrainian, Malay, Vietnamese, and Filipino.

Experimental: Tamil.

Guide can speak all listed languages. Understanding and computer control work best in the “Best experience” group.

What should I not use Guide for?

Guide is designed to help with accessibility challenges, not replace human judgment or handle sensitive tasks.

Avoid using Guide for:

  • Entering passwords or sensitive personal information
  • Submitting financial transactions or signing legal agreements
  • Browsing secure medical, banking, or government portals
  • Running unattended or automating workflows
  • While using any software or website you do not trust Treat Guide as a helpful assistant, not a fully autonomous system.

Guide Now Supports Windows Speech

February 22, 2026

Guide Now Supports Windows Speech

Guide 2.4.0 introduces support for Windows Speech (SAPI), letting you use your own Windows voice settings with Guide. We’re also improving our NVDA integration.

One of the most common requests we’ve heard from our accessibility community: “Can Guide use my Windows voice instead of its own?” Starting with version 2.4.0, the answer is yes.

Windows Speech (SAPI) Support

Guide now lets you choose Windows Speech as your TTS engine, using whatever voice and speed you’ve already configured in Windows Settings. This means:

  • Your voice, your settings. If you’ve spent time tuning a Windows voice to your preference, Guide now respects that without needing to reconfigure anything in Guide itself.
  • More voice variety. Access any voice installed on your Windows system, including voices you’ve downloaded from Microsoft.
  • Consistent experience. For users who rely on a specific Windows voice across all their apps, Guide can now be part of that consistent setup. To switch, open Guide’s Settings tab and choose Windows Speech (SAPI) from the new voice selector. You’ll see a dropdown of all voices available on your system.
Redesigned Voice Settings

We’ve cleaned up the voice settings panel with a new radio button layout that makes it easier to switch between Guide’s three voice modes:

  • Guide built-in voices (Amelia, Rachel, Brian, Michelle)
  • Windows Speech (SAPI) using your own Windows-configured voice
  • NVDA for screen reader users who prefer NVDA to handle all speech No more separate checkboxes to juggle. Just pick the mode that works best for you.
NVDA Improvements Coming

We heard your feedback. NVDA speech in Guide was too fast and didn’t respect your custom NVDA voice setup. We’ve made improvements to address both of those issues in 2.4.0, and we’re continuing to invest in the NVDA experience.

That said, there are real limitations to what’s possible with NVDA today. Because Guide can’t reliably detect when NVDA has finished speaking, the completion sound and window re-maximize aren’t available in NVDA mode. Guide also can’t guarantee it will always get priority in NVDA’s speech queue, so other things NVDA is reading may cut Guide off mid-response. We know these things matter for some workflows and we’re actively working on them. More improvements are in the pipeline.

How to Update

Guide updates automatically in the background. You’ll be prompted to restart when version 2.4.0 is ready. Or download fresh from our website.

If you have feedback on the new voice settings, especially if you use NVDA or SAPI day to day, we’d love to hear from you at support@guideinteraction.com.

Assist Modes in Guide: Smart and Expert

September 7, 2026

Assist Modes in Guide: Smart and Expert

Guide 2.5.0 lets you choose between Smart mode, the default for everyday tasks, and Expert mode, a more powerful model for complex work on Standard and Plus plans. Here’s how to switch and what to expect.

Since version 2.5.0, Guide has an Assist mode setting that decides which AI model handles your requests. There are two options.

Smart (default)

Smart is Guide’s everyday mode and the right choice for almost everything: reading what’s on screen, opening apps, filling in forms, sending messages, playing music. It runs on a newer, more capable AI model than earlier versions of Guide, and it’s what Guide uses unless you change it.

Expert

Expert puts a bigger, more capable model behind Guide, one built for long, multi-step work. Use it for tasks with many steps or unfamiliar layouts: a booking with dates and options, a long form across several pages, comparing things across tabs.

Availability: Expert is included with Guide Standard and Guide Plus. On the free trial and Guide Lite, Expert appears in the Assist mode list but can’t be selected; upgrading to Standard or Plus unlocks it straight away.

How to switch
  • Bring Guide into focus (Ctrl + Alt + G).
  • Go to the Settings tab.
  • Under Assistant Behavior, find Assist mode and choose Smart or Expert. The setting is saved immediately and applies to your next request. There is no need to restart Guide.

With a screen reader, the Assist mode control is a standard combo box: arrow through the two options and the description of each is read out.

Assists and actions

An assist is one request from you. Guide may take many actions (clicks, keystrokes, screenshots) to complete it. Since 2.5.0, Guide can take up to 40 actions per assist (up from 30), so longer tasks are less likely to stop before they finish. Your plan’s monthly assist count is unchanged.

Which mode should I use?
  • Start with Smart. It is fast, capable, and included with every plan.
  • Switch to Expert if Guide keeps getting stuck on a particular task, or the task has a lot of steps. Switch back afterwards if you like; the choice is yours to keep either way.

Guide not working in a specific app? Try running as administrator

If Guide can’t click or type in a particular program, running Guide as administrator usually fixes it.

If Guide works fine in most programs but struggles with a specific app, such as clicks not landing or keyboard input being ignored, it is most likely a Windows permissions issue.

Why this happens

Windows protects apps that run with elevated privileges from being controlled by other software. If the program you are trying to use is running as administrator and Guide is not, Windows will block Guide’s clicks and keystrokes from reaching it.

Running Guide as administrator puts it at the same privilege level, which removes that block.

How to run Guide as administrator
  • Close Guide if it is already open
  • Find the Guide shortcut on your desktop or in the Start menu
  • Right-click the shortcut and select Run as administrator
  • Accept the User Account Control prompt if one appears
  • Try your task again
Making it permanent

If you regularly use apps that require this, you can set Guide to always launch as administrator.

  • Open File Explorer and navigate to the folder where Guide is installed, typically C:\Program Files\Guide
  • Right-click Guide.exe and select Properties
  • Open the Compatibility tab
  • Check Run this program as an administrator
  • Click Apply Guide will now always start with administrator privileges.

Voice Options in Guide

Guide supports three TTS engines: built-in voices, Windows Speech (SAPI), and NVDA. Learn what’s available and how to switch.

Guide gives you three ways to hear responses. You can switch between them any time in the Settings tab.

Built-in voices

Guide’s default option. These are low-latency cloud voices that work out of the box with no extra setup.

Available voices:

  • Amelia
  • Rachel
  • Brian
  • Michelle (default) To sample a voice before committing to it, select it from the dropdown and a short audio preview will play automatically.
Windows Speech (SAPI)

Guide can use your Windows built-in voices instead of its own. This is useful if you’ve already configured a preferred voice in Windows Settings, or if you want access to voices you’ve downloaded from Microsoft.

Availability: Windows Speech is enabled automatically if your system supports it. If the option is greyed out, SAPI voices aren’t available on your device.

How to enable:

  • Open Guide and go to the Settings tab
  • Under Voice, select Windows Speech (SAPI)
  • A dropdown will appear listing all voices installed on your system. Pick the one you want. Guide will use whatever voice you select at the speed and pitch configured in your Windows Settings.
NVDA

If you use NVDA as your primary screen reader, Guide can hand off all speech to NVDA instead of speaking on its own. This keeps your audio experience consistent across everything you use.

Availability: The NVDA option is only enabled if NVDA is already running when Guide starts. If NVDA isn’t detected at launch, the option will be greyed out. Start NVDA first, then open Guide to enable it.

Things to know in NVDA mode:

  • Guide uses your NVDA voice, rate, and pitch settings
  • The completion sound and window re-maximize are not available in NVDA mode, since Guide can’t reliably detect when NVDA has finished speaking
  • Guide can’t guarantee speech priority in NVDA’s queue, so other things NVDA is reading may cut Guide off mid-response
  • To re-enable the completion sound and re-maximize, switch back to a built-in voice or Windows Speech
Switching between voice modes

All three options are available as radio buttons in Settings > Voice. Select a mode and Guide will switch immediately with no restart needed.
