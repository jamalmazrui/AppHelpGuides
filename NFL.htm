Can I still log in to my NFL account if I click “Reject” on the privacy banner at the bottom of the page?

Fans trying to sign in to their NFL account may encounter the privacy banner at the bottom of the sign in page. Fans have the choice to reject cookies and continue logging in to their NFL account.

How do I find my Apple Private Relay email address?

Fans trying to sign in to their NFL account after the removal of social authentication may have trouble locating their email address if they are a user of Apple Private Relay. Troubleshooting recommendations for this issue: On your iPhone or iPad: 1. Open the Settings app, then tap your name to view your Apple ID settings 2. Tap iCloud 3. Tap Hide My Email 4. Tap on NFL 5. The Private Relay email address will be displayed under This App Received 6. If you continue to encounter issues, please contact us using this website’s chat tool.

On the web: 1. Sign in to appleid.apple.com 2. Go to the Sign-In and Security section 3. Click Sign in with Apple 4. Click NFL 5. The Private Relay email address will be displayed under Hide My Email 6. If you continue to encounter issues, please contact us using this website’s chat tool.

For help finding your Apple Private Relay email address, check out this link.

Using biometric authentication as an alternative to a password (i.e. Use Touch ID, Face ID, etc).

Fans who come to the NFL sign in page may want to set up a biometrics passkey to authenticate their NFL account. Biometric passkeys are a new form of authentication that allows fans to use the unlocking mechanisms of their devices. The device biometric (fingerprint, face recognition, etc.) serves as a secure way to validate a fan’s identity in place of a traditional password. Biometric passkey set up instructions: Sign In Password Page 1. Go the NFL account sign in page 2. Enter your email address and click ‘Continue’ 3. Click the device biometric icon on the NFL account password page 4. Follow your device’s instructions to confirm your biometric (fingerprint, face recognition, etc.) 5. Check your email address inbox for a four digit code to validate your email address 6. Enter the four digit code to confirm your email address 7. The NFL site/application you were signing in to will load and you will be able to leverage your biometric for future sign in

The Basics

All fantasy seasons begin with the Draft, when team managers take turns selecting players for their teams. How Bonuses Work

For bonuses involving TD yardage, points are cumulative. For example, if bonuses for a 40+ yard touchdown and a 50+ yard touchdown are both turned on and a player scores a 55+ yard touchdown, the player will receive both bonuses. Please edit your settings accordingly. In contrast, bonuses for total yardage are not cumulative. For example, if your league has a bonus for 100-199 yards receiving and a bonus for 200+ yards receiving and a player finishes a game with 250 yards receiving, they will only receive the bonus for 200+ yards as their total does not fall between 100-199. #### Ties NFL-Managed leagues and leagues with default settings allow ties to be counted in team records should two opponents end a matchup with the same score. Standings, Seeding, & Rankings

By default, tie breakers for season standings in the case of an overall W-L-T record tie are as follows: - Points For - Head -to-Head Record

Head-to-Head Record: Breaking Standings Ties The second tie breaker for the same record is Head-to-head record. Teams are ranked in order of win-loss-tie percentage in matchups played against all the other tied teams together/combined. Individual matchups are irrelevant. EXAMPLE: Say A, B, C, and D are 3-1, and that A beat B and C, B beat C, and D didn’t play anyone in the group. - A is 2-0 against teams in the tied group. - B is 1-1 against teams in the tied group. - C is 0-2 against teams in the tied group. - D is 0-0 against teams in the tied group.

So A is first, B is second, and C and D are tied. Note: this is only the default in NFL-managed leagues. In custom leagues (including those with divisions), Points For is the default. You can also choose to use head-to-head or you can choose Points Against or Division Record (only available for leagues with divisions). What is a money line?

While a point spread still allows a bettor to wager on who will be the winner of a game/match, it also includes odds for the margin of victory. Point spread is a way for oddsmakers to make a matchup between two unbalanced teams more even by giving points to or taking points away from each team. Similar to the money line, a negative point spread indicates which team is favored to win and a positive spread indicates the underdog. However instead of the payout, it shows the number of points a team must win or lose by in order to cover your bet. Most often, the betting odds for point spread are -110. Example: - Winning Team: Point Spread: Odd: Explanation - Team A: -10.5: -110: Team A is favored to win. The bettor must wage $110 and Team A must win by 11 points in order for the bettor to win $100 - Team B: +10.5: -110: Team B is the underdog. The bettor must wage $110 and Team B must win or lose by less than 10 points in order for the bettor to win $100

In this scenario, there would be no tie/push since the point spread includes a .5 and there is no way for a team to win by half a point. However, if the point spread was +10/-10 and the favored team wins by exactly 10 or the underdog loses by exactly 10, you would push and get your initial wager returned. What is the preseason blackout policy for live games on NFL+?

PRESEASON Live Games

Live games on NFL+ are subject to blackouts depending on your current location. With NFL+ during the preseason, in-market games can be streamed on your respective club’s website and app. Preseason out-of-market and national games are available to watch on NFL+. Below are the types of blackouts you can anticipate with NFL+. What is an “in-market” blackout? - Preseason games airing locally within your market will not be available to watch live within NFL+ due to local blackout restrictions. For instance, if you live in Kansas City you can’t watch a Chief’s game live because it would be “in market” for you.

What is a “national” blackout? - A national blackout applies to a broadcast game airing live on FOX, ESPN, NBC, or CBS. The regular season national games and local market games are available to watch. Including all post season games.

Why can’t I see my team’s matchup in Live Preseason Games? - Games that are subject to local and national blackout restrictions will be blocked from the Live Preseason Games page.

Are there any restrictions on Live Audio? - No. Blackout restrictions do not apply to live game audio, so you are able to listen to every game of the season and you can also choose between local or national radio calls.
