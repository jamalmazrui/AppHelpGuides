Use on-demand services with Transit

On-demand services or microtransit is a form of public transportation that is used as a convenient connection to frequent fixed-route services, or on it’s own. New to microtransit? Don’t worry! While a new mode of transportation can be overwhelming, Transit makes it simple. Just follow these steps and you’ll be on your way to better mobility! #### Here’s what this article covers: - Using on-demand services - How to plan a trip using on-demand services - Troubleshoot on-demand issues

Using on-demand services

  • From the main screen, look for on-demand service in the list. Note that the on-demand card is separate from the ridehail card.
  • Tap “Request ride”.
  • Fill in your destination and tap “Request [On-Demand Service]” to open your area’s on-demand services application and book your ride. If it’s not already downloaded, you’ll be redirected to the App Store or Google Play Store to download it.

How to plan a trip using on-demand services

Warning: Before planning your first trip, make sure you have your agency’s on-demand app installed and ready to go! - Plan your trip as you usually would. If your trip is eligible for on-demand, you will see it in your trip results. - Tap on the trip result offering on-demand service. - Tap “Request [On-Demand Service]”. This will redirect you to your agency’s on-demand app where you can book the ride.

Pro tip: If the on-demand portion of your trip comes after a fixed-route bus, make sure to order your ride a couple of minutes before you get to your stop. #### Troubleshoot on-demand issues ##### My trip doesn’t give me an on-demand option This option may not be available for your trip. There are many possible reasons for this: maybe the trip is too far, or maybe the fixed routes in your area serve you better. On-demand service operates based on zones – if you’re not getting results, it could be because your origin and/or destination are not in compatible zones. ##### Fare issues Fares are managed by your local agency, so it’s best to contact them with any specific questions.

Locate bikeshare and scooter stations

Pin icons on the map indicate the locations of nearby bikeshare docks. The level within each pin indicates how many bikes are available at the station, and a lightning bolt badge means that the station has e-bikes. Scooter rental locations are also shown as pin icons on the map. To see the number of bikes and docks available at a station, tap the pin on the map. Once you’ve pulled up the station’s details, you can compare the availability of bikes and walking distance for other stations by tapping on other pins. Pro tip: if the bikeshare service you want to use supports unlocking directly in Transit, you can also tap the pin icon to keep that station at the top of the list on the main screen when you’re nearby. #### Purchase a pass or ride If you’ve never purchased a pass for your local bikeshare system, you can read about how it works here: Purchase a bikeshare pass. #### Unlock a bike Notes: - Some bikeshare systems require specific type of passes in order for you to quickly unlock a bike in the app. - Some bikeshare systems require tapping a button on the dock before users can unlock a bike. - Not all bikeshare systems support unlocking bikes directly in Transit. For some systems, you will be prompted to download an external app to unlock a bike.

Here’s how to unlock and use a bikesharing bike in Transit: 1. Select the bikeshare station you want to use on the map, or find the bikeshare card in the list of nearby routes and tap “Unlock a bike”.

Once you are set up with your account, you will be able to access the unlock code. 5. Then select a bike at the station and enter the 5-digit ride code provided in the app in the keypad on the dock. 6. When the green light displays on the bike dock, pull the bike back firmly from the dock. 7. Adjust the seat to your preference, strap on a helmet and pedal away!

Notes: - Your ride code will expire after a set amount of time. You’ll need to request a new one when the time’s up. - Ride codes are good for only one trip. You’ll be able to request a new code free of charge within the period of your pass. - Bikeshare networks have a maximum duration of free trips included in your pass. In most cases, this is 30 minutes. Additional usage fees will apply for longer trips.

If we don’t support bikeshare plans and fares in the app, tap “Unlock in [app]” to be redirected to the native app for that system. You can access your account and unlock a bike there. #### Unlock a scooter Here’s how to unlock and use a scooter in Transit: 1. Locate the scooter you want to use on the map and tap on the pin. 2. About accessibility information

When accessibility information is available, Transit displays whether vehicles and stops are wheelchair accessible. #### Enable accessibility information - Tap the gear icon at the top left of the main screen. - Scroll down to “Getting around” and tap “On the sidewalk”. - Scroll down to “Step-free accessibility” and select “Show accessibility info” to display whether vehicles and stops are wheelchair accessible. To surface results in the trip planner that are exclusively accessible, select “Show accessible trips first”. - You can also manage this and additional accessibility features by scrolling down to “Preferences” and tapping “Settings and accessibility”. - Scroll down to “Accessibility”. You’ll see additional customization options like “Minimize walking” and “Show line colors”.

Identify wheelchair accessible departures and stops

For each route, the main screen displays both whether (A) the vehicle for the next upcoming departure and (B) the closest stop are wheelchair accessible. Tap on a route for more information. Accessibility icons at the bottom of each ETA card show which upcoming departures are wheelchair accessible. Scroll down the stop list to check which stops along the route are wheelchair accessible. To check more departures, swipe left through the ETA cards and tap “More departures”. The full schedule screen indicates whether the upcoming departures from your selected stop are wheelchair accessible. #### Navigate your trip with VoiceOver and TalkBack For blind or visually impaired users, Transit is compatible with screen readers like VoiceOver (iOS) and TalkBack (Android). To navigate your screen, select an element, wait for it to be read aloud, then double-tap to select it. Information such as route names, stops, and trip duration are condensed into clear sentences to optimize your experience. You can also tap GO to navigate with step-by-step audio instructions throughout your journey, including when your stop is approaching. #### Report missing or inaccurate accessibility information Most of the time, it’s your local transit agency’s responsibility to add or update the accessibility information for their routes and stops. That’s because the accessibility information displayed in Transit is pulled directly from your local transit agency’s publicly available data. Unfortunately, not all public transit agencies provide accessibility information, and it’s not always updated in a timely manner. If you notice missing or inaccurate accessibility information, we recommend contacting your local transit agency directly. - You can search for your local transit agency’s contact information in our directory here: https://transitapp.com/support/search-agencies?lang=en

Otherwise, you can also contact Transit. Tap or click here to find out how.

Any previously purchased tickets in your Transit account will be not be transferred to over once you link your myID account - you will see a pop-up message about this before continuing.

How to track departures on your transit line

Public transit can sometimes be a guessing game. Can you trust that departure time? Will the bus show up? That’s why we’re here: open the app and you’ll see when the departure is in real-time, when you have to rely on the schedule, or even if your trip was cancelled. #### Here’s what this article covers: - Check real-time information on the main screen - Identify real-time, scheduled, and cancelled trips

Real-time - Scheduled - Skipped - Cancelled - Likely cancelled

Check real-time information on the main screen

You can check if a departure time in the app is real-time by looking for two pulsating waves next to the countdown. If these waves aren’t present, the times in the app will be greyed out to show the posted schedule, and won’t reflect any delays or service changes. To see additional upcoming departures, tap the line you’re interested in and swipe left through the ETA cards. You can also tap “More departures” to check the line’s complete schedule. Transit also tracks the live locations of transit vehicles on the map. The top right corner of the vehicle icon will tell you if there are GO riders on board, or if not, the last time we got an update on that vehicle’s location. #### Identify real-time, scheduled, and cancelled trips ##### Real-time When you see real-time waves next to a departure and a bus or train icon on the map, that indicates that you’re looking at real-time information! ##### Scheduled When the departure time is greyed out, that means we’re showing you the scheduled departure time. We know scheduled arrivals aren’t always accurate, which is why we display real-time information whenever it’s available. ##### Skipped If the departure time is crossed through and marked “Skips this stop”, it means the vehicle will not be stopping at your selected stop. You’ll have to go to a different stop to be picked up. ##### Cancelled Sometimes trips are cancelled: a vehicle breaks down, or there isn’t a driver available. To let riders know about a cancellation, transit agencies can add this information to the app in real time. You’ll know a trip has been cancelled by your agency when the departure is crossed out and marked “Cancelled”. ##### Likely cancelled If a trip on your transit line is missing real-time information, we’ll analyze the data and determine if we think this scheduled trip has been cancelled. The trip will be crossed out and marked “Likely cancelled” to let you know we don’t think it’s coming. #### Crowding information In supported regions, Transit also displays how crowded upcoming departures are in real-time. Here’s how to check: - On the main screen, select the line you’re interested in. The ETA cards will display the crowding level below the expected arrival time. Vehicle icons will also display crowding levels. - You can tap the vehicle icon on the map for more information.

Some agencies provide crowding information using sensors on their vehicles, but you can also let fellow Transit users know how crowded the bus is by answering questions in Rate-My-Ride. To do so, start GO and select the appropriate answer when prompted about how many seats are available on the vehicle. We’ll update the crowding level for other users to see. If you’ve minimized GO, you won’t be prompted to answer questions in Rate-My-Ride. Otherwise, just dismiss the prompt and we won’t ask again for that trip. #### Where real-time information comes from Here’s how real-time works: - Transit agencies use GPS trackers in their buses and trains to monitor the position of the vehicle. - Then, they use the vehicle location to calculate a more precise arrival time for your stop. - They send this new arrival time to us and we display it in the app!

Since real-time arrivals are based on the actual location of the vehicle, they’ll change depending on whether it’s running early or late. While we would love to provide perfect real-time information for all transit services, we don’t actually operate the transit system. But there are things that can help improve real-time information available in the app: - If your transit agency doesn’t have real-time information in the app, help out your fellow riders by tapping GO to crowdsource the real-time location of your vehicle. - We’re also working in dozens of cities to come up with our own real-time predictions that are better than what you’ll find in any other app. You’ll see a notice in the app if they’ve rolled out to your city!

Detours

If there’s a detour we haven’t detected or if we’ve routed you the wrong way, let us know. Keep in mind that automatic detour detection is not available in all supported regions. You can help make this feature better by tapping the GO button every time you ride. GO crowdsources up-to-the-second bus locations for other riders, and helps our detour detector by providing even more precise off-track tracking. #### Contact us about inaccurate information If you’ve encountered inaccurate real-time or cancellation information in the app, please contact us to report the problem. What is GO ?

Transit’s GO feature helps you navigate your local transit system by sending notifications to guide you through a trip step-by-step. - Go in … minutes, so you know when to start heading to your stop. - Hurry up!, if you might miss your ride. - 5…4…3…2..more stops, as you get closer to your destination stop. - Your stop is next!, when your stop is next. - This is your stop!, when you’re at your destination.

Once launched, GO runs in the background and works with voice notifications, so you can get lost in your music or take a nap without worrying about missing your stop. GO then ends automatically when you’ve reached your destination. Plus, when you ride with GO, you provide other riders on your line with more accurate real-time tracking information.
