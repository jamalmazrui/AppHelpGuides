Accessing The Atlantic

The Atlantic’s accessibility practices

The Atlantic strives to be inclusive of all the ways our readers experience our journalism. This includes building accessibility into the design and development process for our website and app. Readers can expect that our form fields have clear and accurate labels, our colors and fonts have been accessibility tested, and that we programmatically adjust focus where possible. Our developers also test with voiceover while building.

If you have any additional questions about our accessibility practices, have any requests, or have identified an issue with an accessibility feature, please contact our Customer Care team.

How to Share Your Premium Plus Subscription (Accessing The Atlantic)

Your Premium Plus subscription includes digital access for up to three additional people. To share your subscription with friends or family, please follow the instructions below.

Send Invitations

  • Sign in to your Atlantic account.
  • Go to your Account page.
  • Select “Share Access”.
  • Click “Add a friend”
  • Enter the full name  and email address of the person you’d like to invite.
  • Add an optional message to your invitation.
  • Click “Confirm”. Your invitee will receive an email from The Atlantic with a link to activate their access.

What Your Invitees Receive

Once an invite is redeemed, your member will have:

  • Digital access to TheAtlantic.com
  • Access in The Atlantic app for iOS and Android Please note that shared members receive digital access only. Premium-level physical benefits—including print delivery, ad-free browsing, the coffee table book, and merchandise—are reserved for the primary subscriber.

Still Have Questions?

If you’re unable to send invitations, your invitees did not receive their email, or you need assistance managing your shared access, please contact our Customer Care team.

Manage Your Shared Members on a Premium Plus Plan (Accessing The Atlantic)

Your Premium Plus subscription includes digital access for up to three additional people. You can view, add, or remove shared members at any time from your account.

View Your Shared Members

  • Sign in to your Atlantic account.

  • Go to your Account page.

  • Select Share Access. On this page, you’ll see:

  • Pending invitations (invites sent but not yet redeemed)

  • Active members (people who have redeemed their access)

Manage Invitations and Members

Under Share Access, you’ll see the following available actions:

Create Invite
  • Click “Add a friend”
  • Enter the full name  and email address of the person you’d like to invite.
  • Add an optional message to your invitation.
  • Click “Confirm”. The person you invited will receive an email from The Atlantic with instructions to activate their access.
Delete Unredeemed Invite

If you have sent an invite but it has not yet been redeemed, you can remove it at any time.

  • Find unredeemed member
  • Select “Cancel Invite” next to their name.
  • Select “Yes, remove them” to confirm. Once the invitation is deleted, your available seats will update and you can send a new invite.
Delete Redeemed Invite

Revoke access from someone who has already redeemed their invitation.

  • Find active  member
  • Select Remove Access next to their name.
  • Select Yes, remove them to confirm. Their digital access will end immediately. Your available seats will update, and you can send a new invitation.

Notifications About Removed Access

When a member is removed from your subscription:

  • You will receive an email confirming that their access has been revoked.
  • The removed member will receive an email letting them know their access has ended. You can invite a new member at any time as long as you have an available seat.

Still Have Questions?

If you’re unable to manage your shared members or if an invitation isn’t working as expected, please contact our Customer Care team.

To adjust your screen resolution, you’ll need to access your computer’s machine settings, outside of your web browser. We recommend setting your display to a resolution of 1024x768 or higher.

    • Select a resolution greater than 1024x768

All billing inquiries

Cancel your gift subscription(s)

This article explains how to manage gift subscriptions to The Atlantic that you have given to other people. Gift subscriptions are separate from regular subscriptions and include auto-renewal options for uninterrupted delivery. If you purchased a gift, you can update or cancel its automatic renewal anytime in your account.

For Gift Givers

Here’s how to view and manage your gift subscriptions:
  • Create an online account or sign in to your existing account. If you do not see your subscription listed, please read this quick overview about how to connect your subscription to your account.
  • Once you’re signed in, go to your account homepage and select “Subscription” in the menu to the left of the screen.
  • Select “Gift Subscriptions” in the dropdown menu.
  • To turn off auto-renewal, toggle “Automatically renew your gift” to the off position. When auto-renewal is off, the gift subscription will end after the current term and you will not be charged again. Gift subscriptions are set to auto-renew by default once they are sent. For instructions on how recipients can redeem their gift subscription, please see this article.

If you would like to purchase a new gift subscription for the same recipient and the gift subscription has expired, then you will need to purchase a new gift subscription for them here.

If you are having any trouble managing your gift subscription(s), please contact our Customer Care team.

For Gift Recipients

If you received an Atlantic subscription as a gift and you would like to cancel it, please contact our Customer Care team.

Inquire about a credit card charge

Except for select locations, subscriptions purchased from The Atlantic are enrolled in our automatic renewal program, which guarantees uninterrupted service and paperless billing. Cancel before the end of your subscription to avoid being charged for an additional term. You will always be notified before the card on file is charged for another one-year term.

If you are unsure about a charge made to your credit card, please contact our Customer Care team.

Update the payment method for your subscription

If you would like to change or update your credit card information, you can do so in your account. Here’s how to do so:

  • Create an online account or sign in to your existing account. If you do not see your subscription listed, please read this quick overview about how to connect your subscription to your account.
  • Once you’re signed in, go to your account homepage and select “Subscription” in the menu located to the left of your screen.
  • On the Subscription page, scroll down to “Billing Information” and select “Add a New Payment Method”.
  • Enter your new credit card information and select “Save.” All payments to The Atlantic will be charged to your new card. If you are experiencing any trouble changing the payment information associated with your subscription, please contact our Customer Care team.

The Atlantic’s archive

Explore past issues online

Subscribers have unlimited access to the fully digitalized Atlantic archive, which includes the complete print-magazine collection from November 1857 to the present day. The archive consists of nearly 30,000 articles, essays, original fiction, and poetry as well as every cover in the magazine’s history. On theatlantic.com/archive, readers can search for individual articles by keyword and author, or explore back issues in their entirety. Please note that certain printed pieces may be excluded from this collection where the digital rights are held exclusively by the authors. For reasons of transparency and historical accuracy, the text of the articles that appear in The Atlantic’s archive have not been retroactively edited or otherwise modified in any way.

Important: Unauthorized Emails Impersonating The Atlantic

We are aware of multiple types of fraudulent emails that misuse The Atlantic’s name or branding. Below are the scams we are currently tracking and guidance on how to identify and report them.

Fraudulent Emails Impersonating The Atlantic

Over the past few weeks, we have received numerous reports of fraudulent emails sent from theatlanticm@gmail.com and other similar @gmail.com accounts. These emails purport to come from The Atlantic’s staff and are being sent to authors of recently published books. The emails ask the recipient to respond to discuss potential coverage of their book. We believe these emails are the first step in a phishing scheme.

Any real communications regarding The Atlantic’s journalism and/or official business will always come from an account using the @theatlantic.com domain.

Unfortunately, scams like this one are very common in today’s landscape. We have taken steps to report the account in order to prevent further fraudulent activity.

If you receive this sort of scam email, you should not respond. We encourage recipients to report the email to Google using this form. We also encourage recipients to send any suspicious emails that reference The Atlantic or its journalists to fraudalert@theatlantic.com, so that we can continue to monitor and report these sorts of scams. (If you are not sure if an email purporting to come from The Atlantic is genuine, you can also forward it to this email address for verification.)

Emails Spoofing Atlantic Addresses

We’re aware that some readers are receiving unwanted messages that appear to come from “onestory@theatlantic.com” or other Atlantic-branded addresses. These emails are not from The Atlantic.

Our systems remain secure, and our email provider has confirmed that these messages were not sent by us, nor did we provide your email to these spammers. Rather, spammers are using a common technique called email spoofing — forging our address to make their mail look legitimate.

We’re working with our partners to mitigate the issue and report abuse to the relevant providers.

If you receive one of these messages do not engage with it other than to mark it as spam or junk to your email provider, or to delete it.

We’re very sorry for the inconvenience and appreciate your patience as we continue working to stop these fraudulent emails.

Play The Atlantic Games

The Atlantic has 6 games, all accessible from the games hub on desktop computers and the Games tab on the updated version of The Atlantic app. To play a game, scroll to the icon of the game you want to play and click Play.  The archives to past games are also accessible via that same hub, under the “Play” button.

Rabbithole

Rabbithole is The Atlantic’s daily trivia game, where every answer leads you deeper into the game by becoming part of the next question. Work your way through seven increasingly challenging questions, earning up to three points per question depending on whether you answer on your own, use a hint, or choose from multiple-choice options.

Lemony Snicket’s Suspicious Incident in Dubious Park

Step into Lemony Snicket’s Suspicious Incident in Dubious Park, an interactive mystery where you must uncover a killer hiding somewhere in the park. Explore an illustrated world, question suspicious characters, gather clues, and piece together the truth before making your accusation.

How to Play:

  • Explore the park: Navigate the illustrated map and discover characters, locations, and objects to investigate.
  • Question characters: Talk to the people you meet to gather information, compare stories, and uncover new leads.
  • Examine clues: Inspect objects throughout the park for details that may help solve the case.
  • Follow the evidence: As you make discoveries, new conversations and clues may become available.
  • Use hints if needed: If you’re unsure where to go next, your detective partner can provide guidance.
  • Make your accusation: Once you’ve gathered enough evidence, identify who you believe committed the crime and see if you’ve solved the mystery. After completing the mystery, you can continue exploring the park to uncover details and conversations you may have missed.

Crossword

The Atlantic crossword is a mini puzzle that gets bigger and more difficult every day, with a full-size themeless puzzle every Sunday. The puzzle goes live at midnight ET.

Each crossword includes the ability to solve alongside a friend (Social Play) by clicking the icon to the right of the timer on the top left corner above the puzzle.

You can reveal or check your answers by clicking the Assist drop-down menu on the top right corner above the grid.

You can print your puzzle by clicking the print button on the top right corner above the grid.

You can also customize your solving experience by clicking on the settings button on the top right corner above the grid.

Bracket City

Bracket City is a daily word game where nested clues resolve into other clues, eventually revealing a fun fact from today in history. New puzzles go live every day  at midnight ET.

You can start by typing an answer to any bracketed clue highlighted in light blue into the input bar on the bottom. If it’s right, the clue will disappear and the answer will become part of the next clue. If you’re wrong, it will say “incorrect.”

If you’re stuck, you can tap on any light-blue clue to peek at the first letter of the answer or reveal it entirely.

To play the tutorial, click on the question mark on the top right corner of the puzzle.

To change settings, including dark mode and hard mode, click on the exclamation mark on the top left corner of the puzzle.

To access the archives, click on the date in the center above the puzzle.

Stacks

Stacks is a daily word puzzle where the goal is to drop words on top of each other in an order and position that creates new words going across. New puzzles go live every day  at midnight ET.

Click on a word in the bank at the top of the puzzle to get it ready to drop. Click the arrows on either side of the selected word to move it left or right to test out different positions.

Click the drop button below the selected word or the down arrow on your desktop keyboard to make a drop. Undo a drop by clicking the “Undo Drop” button at the bottom of the stack.

When a word has been dropped in the right stack, it will turn green.

To play the tutorial, click the question mark on the top right corner of the puzzle.

To play on hard mode, with no silhouette and limited Undo Drops, click the gear icon on the top right corner of the puzzle.

Caleb’s Inferno

Caleb’s Inferno is no longer available in the Games Hub, but previously published puzzles can still be accessed through their direct links and the puzzle archive page.

Caleb’s Inferno is a variation of the crossword that gets more difficult as you solve downward. This puzzle was published monthly on the back page of the print magazine. Online, it was published the Sunday before the print magazine was released.

Save Atlantic articles

You can save stories you see on The Atlantic’s website or app.

To save an article on the website, click on the title of the article to open it. Below the title and introduction, on the right side of the page, you will see a small bookmark icon and the word “Save”. Click the icon to save the article. To save an article in the mobile app, tap the three dots at the top of an article or wherever you see the story previewed in your feed. Select “Save Story.” You will need to have an account to save a story. Go to the “Settings” page of the app or click here to create an account.

Sending Gift Articles

What is a gift article?

All subscribers of The Atlantic can share unlimited gift articles to anyone who isn’t currently an Atlantic subscriber. Recipients don’t need to sign in or register—they’ll have free access for 14 days.

How to gift an article?

To gift an article, make sure you are an active subscriber and are logged in on TheAtlantic.com. When viewing an article on site, you should see the option to “Share as gift.”

Upon selecting “Share as gift” you will see options for ways you can gift the article.

If you are signed into the mobile app, tap the three dots on an article page or article card. Select the “Share as gift” and you will see the options for ways you can gift the article.

How many articles can I gift?

Subscribers to The Atlantic can gift unlimited articles.

Does a recipient need to register or subscribe to view the gifted article?

No, a recipient does not need to register or subscribe in order to view the gifted article, unless their access expires.

How long will a recipient be able to view the gifted article?

Gift article recipients will have access to view the gifted article for 14 days. Once their access expires, they will need to subscribe in order to view the article again.

How will a recipient know they’ve received a gifted article?

Anyone who’s not currently a subscriber to The Atlantic who views a gifted article, will see a note that tells them the article has been gifted to them and how much longer they’ll have access to view it.

Can I gift articles on The Atlantic’s mobile app?

Yes, this feature is available in The Atlantic’s mobile app for iOS and Android.

Digital subscribers to The Atlantic can also access their Digital Libraries from the web browser on any device that is connected to the internet. More information about accessing your Digital Library can be found here.

iOS Devices

To read The Atlantic on iOS or Apple devices:

  • Open theatlantic.com in your preferred web browser. Atlantic subscribers can read unlimited articles or download digital magazine issues to Apple devices.
  • Or you can use The Atlantic’s iOS app, available to download in the App Store. All subscribers have unlimited access to the app when they sign in with their Atlantic account details. If you’d like to use this option, please find further instructions here. Please note: The Atlantic app requires iOS 16 or newer.

Android Devices

To read The Atlantic on Android devices:

  • Open theatlantic.com in your preferred web browser. Atlantic subscribers can read unlimited articles or download digital magazine issues to their Android devices.
  • Or you can use The Atlantic’s Android app, available to download for free in the Google Play Store. All subscribers have unlimited access to the app when they sign in with their Atlantic account details. If you’re not yet a subscriber, please purchase a subscription on theatlantic.com. Please note: The Atlantic app requires Android 6 or newer.

Kindle By Amazon

To read The Atlantic on a Kindle e-reader:

  • Open www.theatlantic.com on Kindles with web browser capabilities
  • Digital subscribers can download digital magazine issues to Kindles with web browser capabilities. If you have a subscription through Amazon, it includes all print editions of The Atlantic magazine in Kindle format, but not unlimited access to The Atlantic’s website. To enjoy full digital benefits, please explore our subscription options.

Nook By Barnes And Noble

To read The Atlantic on Nook by Barnes and Noble:

  • Open www.theatlantic.com on Nooks with web browser capabilities
  • Digital subscribers can download digital magazine issues to Nooks with web browser capabilities.
  • Purchase a specific Nook subscription to The Atlantic through Barnes and Noble.

Apple News+

Unfortunately, you currently cannot access The Atlantic using your Apple News+ subscription. You also cannot access Apple News + using your Atlantic subscription. Please contact our Customer Care team if you have any other questions.
