Muse help

This guide holds Meta’s own help for Muse, its personal AI agent, gathered on 17 September 2026 — nine days after Muse was released, so expect it to age quickly.

It covers what Muse does with your guidance and approval, how connectors work, how it handles privacy, safety and security, and how to manage the data it keeps. Muse can send mail and make purchases on your behalf, so read the approval and connector pages before you connect anything; nothing here is advice, and anything about money should be checked against Meta’s own pages on the day.

Meta refuses ordinary requests for these pages, so they were gathered with a web browser instead. That is why this guide is short: only the pages a browser could reach by following links are here.

How to get started with Muse

Updated:2 weeks ago

Muse is your personal AI agent that can take actions and get things done with your direction, and can be connected with other apps and services. You can ask your Muse questions, get help with tasks, set reminders, and have ongoing conversations that build on what you’ve discussed before.

Before you start

Whether you’re using Muse from the app or the web, you can connect using your Meta Account. If you don’t have a Meta Account or don’t want to connect with an existing account, you can create one using an existing Facebook or Instagram account or by providing an email address or phone number.

How to use Muse
Start a conversation
  • Open Muse, which will begin on the Chat tab.
  • Type a message or question.
    • You don’t have to wait for Muse to respond before asking your next question. Unlike other AI apps, Muse is not turn-by-turn. You can follow up naturally, and you don’t need to repeat the context.
Create a side chat

Side chats are separate conversations that help you organize different topics without cluttering the main chat. You can also delete side chats.

  • Open the Chat tab on Muse.
  • Tap the menu icon in the top left corner, then the pencil and note icon to create a side chat.
  • Type a message or question.
  • Your Muse will respond, and stays aware across the main chat and side chats.
Ask for help with a task
  • Describe what you need. Be as clear and specific as possible.
  • Your Muse may ask clarifying questions, then get to work.
  • Review what your Muse does or provides. You can ask for changes or adjustments.
Set a reminder
  • Tell your Muse what you want to be reminded about and when.
  • Your Muse will confirm the reminder details.
  • Your Muse will aim to send you a reminder at the scheduled time. You can view reminders by tapping the Assistant icon, then navigating to Upcoming.
Work with files and artifacts
  • Upload a file or ask your Muse to create one.
  • Your Muse can read, summarize, edit, or generate artifacts including PDFs, spreadsheets, documents, images, and podcasts.
  • Finished artifacts are saved to your workspace in the Library tab, and shared in the conversation. Learn more.
Tips
  • Give context: The more relevant detail you share, the better the response.
  • Ask for changes: If something isn’t quite right, say so. Your Muse can adjust.
  • Use follow-ups: You don’t need to start over. Build on the conversation.
  • Say what format you want: If you need a table, a list, a file, or a specific tone, just say so.
  • Define boundaries: Clearly state what you want your Muse to do and state limitations, for example: “Only search my email, not my text messages.”
  • Provide attribution: When you use Muse to send emails, texts, artifacts, or other information to others, you can ask your Muse to add a note that it was generated by your AI agent to keep recipients informed about how the content was created.
Usage

Muse is available for free with a usage limit. If you reach your free usage limit and want more usage, you can upgrade to a paid subscription, or wait until your free usage limit refreshes. Learn more.

Things to keep in mind
  • Your Muse is designed to remember context from your conversations, so you can refer back to things you’ve discussed before.
  • You can customize your Muse’s name, personality, and preferences at any time by telling your Muse the changes you’d like to see. Learn more.
  • Your Muse may be inaccurate or take unexpected actions. Monitor its actions carefully and intervene as needed. Learn more. Share

How Muse handles your privacy, safety and security

Updated:2 weeks ago

We’ve designed Muse to have multiple layers of privacy and security protections. No automated system is perfect, so review Muse’s activity and only grant the access you’re comfortable with.

Your data in Muse

Muse runs on an environment in the cloud called a virtual machine (“VM”). This is the core environment where Muse keeps information you share and things you create together. Muse also maintains backups to help protect your agent’s history from data loss. Every Muse user’s VM is isolated so no one else’s agent can access it.

In the future, Muse users will also have the choice to use a Muse Confidential VM: an environment we’re designing to keep people’s information private, even from Meta.

Muse and Meta ads

Muse doesn’t share your conversations or the data in your virtual machine with Meta ad systems. This applies even if your Accounts Center includes other Meta Products.

How Muse keeps logins safe

When you connect an app or service, credentials like usernames and passwords are stored in a Secure Credentials Store. Muse can use the Secure Credentials Store to complete an authorized action without the AI model seeing your password. Before Muse uses your credentials, the request goes through checks designed to confirm that:

  • Muse is connecting to the intended service
  • You connected or authorized the service
  • You gave Muse permission to perform that type of action
Protection against malicious instructions

Websites, emails, files and connected services can contain instructions intended to manipulate an AI agent. This is sometimes called a prompt injection attack. Muse uses multiple protections to help detect and resist these attempts. These include model training, automated detection systems, and limits on the information and services Muse can access during a task. Important permission and security checks operate separately from the AI model, meaning that they don’t depend only on Muse recognizing that an instruction may be malicious.

Improving our AI models

You decide whether we can use your interactions with Muse to develop and improve our AI models. This setting is on when you first use Muse. You can change it at any time using the toggle in your Settings under Data controls. When the setting is off, your interactions with Muse will not be used to develop and improve AI at Meta. Changes to this setting also apply to previous interactions.

Allowing us to use your interactions in training helps us make smarter, more capable models. If you do allow this, we still remove certain categories of personally identifiable information like names, email addresses, phone numbers and Social Security Numbers. We also disassociate the interactions from your account.

You control connected apps and services

You can choose to connect Muse to apps and services provided by Meta or third-parties. We call these Connectors. When you set up a Connector, Muse gets new abilities, like the ability to see your calendar or write an email.

We designed Muse to exchange with Connectors only the data that’s needed for them to work for you. For example, connecting your email allows Muse to search your email, but it won’t download your whole inbox (unless you tell it to). Some Connectors can also share information proactively with Muse. For example, calendar Connectors can update Muse automatically when there are changes in your schedule. We identify Connectors that do this before you connect them.

Learn more about Connectors and how to manage them.

Permissions for important actions

Muse is designed to ask you to confirm before it takes certain important actions like sending an email or making a purchase. When Muse requests permission, you can review the proposed action before allowing or denying it. Permission requirements may vary depending on the action, the Connector and the default permissions you selected.

Many of these approval checks are enforced outside the AI model, so they don’t rely only on Muse deciding whether it should ask. You can also review Muse’s activity at any time. Learn more about how Muse works with your guidance and approval.

Safety standards

Muse is built to a set of safety standards that govern both what your Muse says and what it does. For example, these standards are designed to prevent your Muse from:

  • Producing harmful or illegal content
  • Assisting with unlawful activity, such as fraud, scams, or obtaining illegal goods
  • Taking actions that could harm you or other people
  • Sexualizing minors or facilitating harm to them
Deleting and forgetting Muse data

You can delete any message you send to Muse, Side Chats with Muse, and files in your virtual machine. If you want to start from scratch, you can reset Muse, which deletes all your Muse data.

After you delete something from Muse, Muse may still remember information it learned from what you deleted. You can also use the forget skill: simply ask Muse to forget a topic or set of interactions. Forgetting works by asking Muse to look for information about a specific person, topic or matter in its memories and supporting files and remove it to the best of its ability. You can find out what Muse remembers about you at any time by asking directly, or by viewing files like your MEMORY.md.

Report a problem

If your Muse does something unexpected or concerning, you can report it so our team can investigate and improve safety. To report a bug:

  • From the web:
    • Click on Settings in the lower left hand corner and select Report an issue.
  • From the app:
    • Shake your phone to open the reporting sheet, select Submit a report and complete the form, then select Submit.
    • You can also open the Chat tab, tap the menu icon in the top left, tap Settings, and select Report an issue. Reporting helps us identify and address bugs and concerning behavior.

Information for people who don’t use Muse

Updated:a week ago

When others use Muse, information about you may be provided to Muse, even if you don’t use Muse directly.

This can happen in a few ways, including:

  • When a Muse user shares your information: A person using Muse may connect other apps and services or share information (such as text, images, videos and documents) that contain information about you and ask your Muse to use that information. For example, if a Muse user connects their calendar app and asks their Muse what meetings they have scheduled for the week, the names of the attendees and event details could be included in the request and response.
  • Publicly available information: To carry out user tasks, Muse might also access information that is publicly available on the internet, which may incidentally include information about you. Since the person using Muse chooses what they share with us and what they request through Muse, we can’t predict exactly what information about you may be included.
How do we use and share information about others shared by Muse users?

When we process information from external sources about people who don’t use Muse, we don’t link that information to any Meta account. This information is not stored in a way that allows Meta to reasonably identify which information belongs to a particular person.

We don’t sell your information, and we never will. We may use and share information provided to us through Muse as described in the Muse Privacy Policy.

How can you exercise your rights?

You may have rights under applicable laws, including the right to object to how your information is processed. Keep in mind that Meta can only honor data subject rights in relation to information that is linked to a Meta account or stored in a way that allows Meta to identify which information relates to a particular person.

You can contact us through Privacy rights request. Read the Meta Privacy Policy for more information.

Developing and improving AI at Meta

If you use Meta Company Products, in certain countries you may be able to submit an objection request. This may include the right to object to your interactions with features that are part of AI at Meta and the public information you’ve shared on Meta Products being used to develop and improve AI at Meta in the future, which can be submitted through your Facebook, Instagram, Meta or WhatsApp account. Your objection will be applied to information associated with the account you’re logged into and any accounts that you’ve added to the same Accounts Center.

If you’re concerned that your personal information from third parties appeared in a response from an AI at Meta model, you can tell us using a dedicated form whether or not you use Meta Products:

  • Australia, Brazil, Ecuador, the European Union, Indonesia, Nigeria, the Philippines, Serbia, South Africa, South Korea, Thailand, Turkey, and the UK: use this form.
  • All other countries: use this form.
Opt out of AI phone calls

We’re also experimenting with the ability for Muse to make phone calls on your behalf to businesses whose phone numbers are publicly available. For example, users may use AI phone calls to ask about your business’ opening hours, or to make a booking. If you’d prefer that your business not receive these AI phone calls, you can opt out using this form.

What Meta says about Muse

Meta AI Doesn’t Just Think, It Acts

Meta’s newsroom, July 2026. This is an announcement rather than help: it describes Muse Spark, the model that plans and acts on your behalf, and what Meta says it can do.

Earlier this month, we announced our new model, Muse Spark 1.1, which powers the Meta AI app and meta.ai. The model is built to plan, work with your apps, and follow through from start to finish, and those capabilities are what make the new features possible in Meta AI. With it driving the experience, Meta AI is starting to take action on your behalf.

This is our next step toward personal superintelligence: an AI that knows your context, is there for you whenever you need it, and handles things so you don’t have to.

An AI Assistant That Plans and Follows Through

You’ve been using Meta AI to find answers, think through ideas, and create content. Now it can go further and actually get things done for you. Meta AI can now make plans, follow through on next steps, and keep you on track without needing to be reminded or re-prompted.

Tell Meta AI you’re renovating your kitchen and it will learn your style, scout Marketplace for furniture and fixtures that fit your budget, and send you a mood board so you can see how it all comes together. Ask it to help you build a running plan for your first half marathon and it will create a week-by-week training schedule, adjust for your availability, and share your plan for the week every Monday morning. Or ask it to plan a birthday dinner and it will find restaurants, check your calendar for a night that works, and suggest options.

Meta AI can now give you a daily briefing, pulling what’s on your calendar, finding relevant updates like realizing when you’re double booked or when plans have changed, and giving you a quick summary at your preferred time. You only have to set up tasks with Meta AI once, then you can leave Meta AI to handle them, whether that’s a weekly meal plan, a heads-up on the latest sneaker drops, or an afternoon update on trends you care about. Tell Meta AI what you want and when, and it’ll keep delivering.

When you want to dig into a topic, Meta AI can do the research in minutes, synthesizing information from across the web, from research papers to what creators and communities share on our apps, so you can understand a subject or weigh your options without the manual work. And if you want to turn what you’ve learned into something visual, ask Meta AI to generate slides.

You don’t have to wait for Meta AI to finish before you weigh in. While it’s putting together a report, a presentation, or a plan for you, you can steer it in real time. Tell it to shift focus, change the tone, or cut a section, and it’ll course-correct on the spot so the end result is actually what you wanted. And everything Meta AI creates for you, from training schedules to slide decks to mood boards, now lives in one place, so you can revisit it, build on it, and share it with friends.

How to Get Started

These features will start rolling out today in select markets in the Meta AI app and meta.ai. We’ll bring them to more countries and surfaces, including WhatsApp, in the coming weeks.

It’s your choice how you use Meta AI in the app and meta.ai: find quick answers; get thoughtful responses that reflect your world; or shop with real inspiration from posts, Reels, and products that people are sharing across our apps. And when you want a fully private conversation, Incognito chats are always there.

This is just the start of what we’re envisioning for Meta AI, and we’ll keep building to make AI more fun, relevant, and useful for you.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What can Muse do?
  • Muse can answer questions, complete tasks, browse the web, make purchases, generate images, create documents, and connect with your favorite apps and services via Connectors. It can also set reminders, track goals, and monitor things you care about, all working in the background on your behalf.
How does Muse work?

Because it’s agentic AI, when you give Muse a task, it figures out which built-in skills it needs and gets to work. It can browse the web, pull information from connected apps, and take action across multiple steps. You stay in control by setting permissions, reviewing and approving what it does along the way.

Is Muse free?

Muse is available for free with a usage limit. If you reach your free limit and want more, you can upgrade to a paid subscription, or wait until your free usage limit refreshes.

Is Muse safe?

Yes. Muse is built with automated protections, agentic AI security standards, and user controls designed to prevent harmful content and unauthorized actions. It also uses a protective layer to stop your information from being shared without your approval. You can review your agent’s activity, manage permissions, and report issues at any time.

Does Muse keep working after I close the app?

Yes. When you ask Muse to do something, it keeps working in the background whether you’re actively using the app or not. For example, if you ask it to monitor daily weather reports and alert you about rain, it will do that on its own without you needing to check in.

Does Muse ask before it acts on my behalf?

Yes. Muse is designed to ask your permission before taking certain actions, like sending messages, making purchases, or sharing information with a connected app. You can choose to allow an action once, always allow it, or deny it. You can manage your default permission settings for every connection at any time.

What is a personal AI agent?

A personal AI agent is an AI that can take actions and get things done on your behalf, not just answer questions. Muse, for example, can browse the web, connect to your apps, complete multi-step tasks, and keep working in the background with your direction and oversight.

How is a personal agent different from a chatbot or AI assistant?

A chatbot responds to one question at a time. An AI assistant can handle more complex conversations and generate content. A personal AI agent like Muse goes further: it can take real actions across the web and connected apps, work through multi-step tasks on its own, and keep running in the background even after you close the app.

What can a personal AI agent help with?

A personal AI agent can help with everyday tasks like booking reservations, monitoring prices, managing reminders, creating documents, generating images, and researching topics. It can also connect to third-party apps and services to take actions on your behalf, like sending emails or completing online purchases. See more agentic AI examples.

To start a chat with Meta AI:
  • Open the Meta AI app.
  • At the bottom, tap Ask Meta AI… and type in your prompt.
  • Choose Meta AI’s mode by tapping the current mode in the bottom right, then tapping the mode you’d like to use. Some modes include:
    • Instant: Generates quicker responses, best used for everyday questions.
    • Thinking: Generates in-depth responses for more complex questions or reasoning.
    • Shopping: A dedicated shopping concierge for you based on your interests and prompt.
  • Tap . Meta AI will generate a response for you. You can have a voice chat with Meta AI by tapping before sending a prompt. You can say something like “Hang up.” or “I’m done.” to end the chat. You’ll need to allow the Meta AI app access to your microphone to speak with Meta AI.

If you’re having a voice conversation with Meta AI, you can continue talking to Meta AI in the background while you use other apps on your device. When the Meta AI app is in the background, your device will show that the microphone is in use. Tap X or End on the voice conversation to end it. How Muse works with Connectors

Updated:2 weeks ago

You can choose to connect Muse to apps and services provided by Meta or third-parties. We call these Connectors. When you set up a Connector, Muse gets new abilities, like the ability to see your calendar or write an email.

To connect a Connector

You can connect a Connector by asking Muse (example: “Connect my Gmail”), or following these steps:

  • Go to Settings, then tap Connectors.
  • Tap Connect next to the Connector you want and read the information.
  • Tap Continue to authorize the connection.
  • Follow any additional login or authorization steps. While connected, your Muse will exchange information with a Connector relevant to the tasks you give it. Connectors have their own terms and privacy policies that govern how they use the information they receive.

We designed Muse to exchange with Connectors only the data that’s needed for them to work for you. For example, connecting your email allows Muse to search your email, but it won’t download your whole inbox (unless you tell it to). Some Connectors can also share information proactively with Muse. For example, calendar Connectors can update Muse automatically when there are changes in your schedule. We identify Connectors that do this before you connect them.

To disconnect a Connector

You can disconnect a Connector at any time in Settings under Connectors. Disconnecting stops Muse from exchanging data with the Connector. Information that Muse previously used to perform tasks with that Connector might still remain in Muse’s memories and your conversation history.

Note: You can also ask Muse to forget interactions.

Approving Connector actions

By default, Muse will not take many important actions, like sending an email, without your approval. Many Connectors can also be set up so that Muse is only able to retrieve data, but not take actions (like sending a calendar invite). You can change when Muse asks for your approval in your Settings. Learn more about setting up approvals.

Meta Connectors

Some Connectors connect to Meta products. Three of these – Facebook, Instagram and Threads – are connected automatically if you have your accounts in the same Accounts Center. You don’t need to do anything to set these up. You can manage your accounts at any time in Accounts Center. Learn more about how and when we use information across accounts in the Muse Privacy Policy.

Custom connectors

If you want to connect to a service not yet available in the Connector list, you can ask Muse to create a Custom Connector. Muse will guide you through the process, which can involve retrieving API information from the service. Muse stores these in its Secure Credentials Store. Meta doesn’t review custom connectors or how they use your information, so grant access with caution and review the provider’s privacy policies.

Notes:
  • After setup, you manage Muse’s access to Connectors like Apple Health or Android SMS in your device settings.
  • When connecting to Google, the use of information received from Workspace APIs will adhere to the Google User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Share

How your agent can take actions on local files and some apps through the Mac app

When you use the Muse app on your Mac, your agent can work across your computer to find, organize, and manage the files you ask it to work with (Example: “Pull the three invoices from last month into one folder”). Your agent can also read info from the apps on your computer like iMessage, Notes, Reminders, Email, Calendar (examples: “Summarize my messages from the week.”)

How it works

To let your agent use your computer, you’ll be asked to grant Muse permissions that are set and controlled by macOS, not by Meta:

  • Full Disk Access, so your agent can find, read or update files. This permission covers all the files on your Mac, but your agent only uses the files and app information you ask it to work with.
  • Automation, so your agent can perform actions on apps that store information locally, like send messages or delete notes.
  • Notifications, so your agent can tell you when a task it was working on is done.
You stay in control while it works

Before your agent takes an important action like sending an email or making a purchase, it asks you to confirm first. When your agent deletes a file, it moves it to your Trash, where you can restore it. Learn more.

Apps that keep your information on your Mac

Some apps store your information on your Mac locally rather than only online, for example your messages, notes and mail. Your agent reads these from your Mac directly.

You’ll see some of these apps that store information locally listed during setup. For each one, you choose:

  • Off: your agent can’t use the information that app keeps on your Mac.
  • Read only: your agent can read that information when you ask it something, but can’t do anything in the app.
  • Read and interact: your agent can read that information and take actions in the app, like sending a message. You can also choose to turn off Muse access to these apps later in Settings.

Apps you have installed aren’t controlled by Meta. Meta doesn’t review these apps or how they use your information. They’ll use any information shared with them in accordance with their own terms and privacy policies. Review the apps Muse can use carefully, as well as each privacy policy.

How we use info we collect

Your interactions with Muse can also include information from the apps and services Muse uses to perform tasks for you, including screenshots of your screen. You decide whether we can use your interactions with Muse to develop and improve our AI models, as described in the Muse Privacy Policy. You can change it at any time using the toggle in Settings under Data controls.

How to manage access

  • Open Settings, then click File System Access to turn off some of the apps that keep your information on your Mac.
  • To turn off a single capability, access to your files, or notifications, open your Mac’s System Settings, go to Privacy & Security, and remove Muse’s permission for Full Disk Access, Automation, or Notifications.
  • To turn off computer use entirely, remove these permissions in System Settings.

Things to keep in mind

  • Your agent may be inaccurate or take unexpected actions. Monitor its actions carefully and intervene as needed.
  • Computer use gives your agent broad reach across your Mac, so only grant access you’re comfortable with. Share

What are skills?

Skills are built-in capabilities that allow your Muse to perform specific tasks. Think of them as specialized tools your Muse can use. Things like creating podcasts, generating images, compiling research, drafting documents, creating dashboards or browsing the web.

Skills are developed by Meta and are already part of Muse. They let your Muse go beyond answering questions and actually take actions like creating a morning news digest, generating a document, or scheduling a task.

How do skills work?

When you give your Muse a request, it determines which skills are needed to complete the task. For example:

  • Asking “Create a podcast about World War I” uses the audio generation skill and web browsing skill.
  • Asking “Create a PDF comparing coffee beans” uses the document creation skill.
  • Asking “Generate an image of a cat playing piano” uses the image generation skill.
What information do skills require?

Skills may require access to specific information depending on the task. For example:

  • Location-based skills may need access to your location to provide relevant results (example: “Remind me to buy eggs when I’m next at the store.”).
  • File-based skills need access to files you upload or store in Muse.
  • Web browsing skills access the internet to find information on your behalf.
How to discover and use skills

Skills are automatically available within Muse and you don’t need to install them separately. Simply ask your Muse to do something, and it will use the appropriate skill. You can explore what your Muse can do by:

  • Visiting the Ideas tab for personalized task suggestions.
  • Asking your Muse “What can you help me with?”
  • Trying requests like creating digests, podcasts, images, PDFs, or research.
Safely managing skills
  • Skills activate when you make a request that requires them.
  • For certain actions like sending emails or making purchases, your Muse is designed to ask for your permission before proceeding. Learn more about permissions. You can review what skills have been used in your Activity log (accessible when you tap your Assistant icon).

How Muse works with your guidance and approval

Updated:2 weeks ago

We’ve designed Muse with multiple layers of privacy and security protections, including checking with you before sensitive actions like payments or purchases. But no automated system is perfect. Because Muse acts on your behalf, you’re responsible for guiding it carefully and approving its actions. You should review its activity, and only grant the access you’re comfortable with.

Muse works with your oversight

Your Muse can make mistakes or take unexpected actions. To use Muse safely, we recommend you:

  • Control what it accesses.
    • You choose what information or Connectors your Muse has access to and what actions your Muse can take on your behalf. As you’re learning, start with low risk tasks until you become more familiar with how your Muse works.
  • Oversee its actions regularly to make sure they match your intentions.
    • You can also ask your Muse to provide the sources it based its response on.
  • Correct any mistakes promptly.
    • You’re responsible for fixing errors, including with third party services or recipients.
  • Review your permissions periodically.
    • Check which Connectors Muse can access and your default permissions settings for those Connectors and adjust as needed.
  • Comply with the Muse Terms.
    • These contain important information about your responsibilities and what you must not use Muse for.
How to set boundaries on what Muse can do

You can set boundaries for your Muse and adjust them at any time to match what you’re comfortable with. It’s worth understanding the concepts of read and write actions. Read actions let Muse view your information (example: checking your calendar or reading an email), while write actions let Muse make changes and manage information on your behalf (example: sending a message or making a purchase). Your Muse learns over time which decisions need your sign off.

Setting permissions

To manage your Muse permissions, navigate to Settings, then tap Permissions. Here you can manage:

  • Connectors
    • If you select Ask for some actions, your Muse will ask for permission before every write action and important read actions.
    • If you select Always ask, your Muse will ask for permission before any action.
  • Web access defaults
    • If you select Ask for some actions, your Muse will ask for permission when your information may be shared or it visits a website that is unfamiliar.
    • If you select Always ask, your Muse will ask for permission before accessing any website.
  • Manage permissions
    • If you select Connectors, you can manage your permissions per Connector.
    • If you select Artifacts and scheduled tasks, you can manage your approvals for each scheduled task and artifacts.
    • If you select Allowed websites, you can view which websites you’ve allowed your Muse to access and revoke permission.
Approving actions

When Muse takes an action, it will ask you for approval in line with the permissions settings you’ve set up. Approval options for actions can include:

  • Allow once: Muse proceeds this one time
  • Allow for this task: Muse can take this type of action for the entire task
  • Allow for this site: Muse can take this type of action for this website in the future without asking again
  • Always allow: Muse can take this type of action for this Connector in the future without asking again
  • Deny: Muse won’t proceed this one time You can tap See task details to see additional details about the task before deciding what permission to grant.
Monitoring Muse’s actions

Your Muse is designed to provide transparency into what it’s doing:

  • Your assistant avatar at the top of your screen shows live status updates (examples: “Is working,” “Making something”, “Is updating memory”).
  • Visit your Activity log by tapping your assistant icon to see a chronological record of actions your Muse has taken and permissions you’ve given it.
  • Check Upcoming by tapping your assistant icon to see scheduled tasks and reminders.
  • Tap See task details on any permission request to see additional task details before deciding what permission to grant.
  • Browse your System Files for a deeper look into your Muse’s activities.
Controls you have over your Muse’s actions

In addition to the controls mentioned above, you can also:

  • Undo or stop
    • You can ask your Muse to undo or stop some actions, like reading a website or comparing prices for a purchase, in chat. Some other actions, like sending an email, cannot be reversed, just like if you were doing them yourself. Keep your default permissions under review to stay in control.
  • Disconnect Connectors
    • You can disconnect Connectors at any time to prevent future actions.
  • Reset
    • Resetting Muse permanently deletes all information, chat history, files, and active tasks. This cannot be undone. Learn more about resetting Muse.
What Muse is not built to do

See the Meta AI Terms of Service and Muse Terms of Service for information about what you shouldn’t use Muse for.
