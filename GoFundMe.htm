Adyen Accounts

Adding personal and bank information

Important reminders for adding personal and bank information

  • The name entered for personal information must match the name entered for bank information. Do not use a nickname or someone else’s name.
  • If funds are going to someone else’s bank account, please click “Back to GoFundMe” and select “Someone else’s bank account” when asked “Whose account will the funds be transferred to?”
  • When entering your bank account number, include any 0’s at the beginning of the number.
  • Use the routing number for ACH, EFT, or direct deposit. Do not use the wire transfer number.
  • For European bank accounts, the IBAN will include capital letters without spaces.

Uploading documents

We may ask for additional information in the form of documents to verify your identity. This could be a government-issued photo ID (license or passport), and/or a bank document (check, bank statement, or signed and stamped bank letter). You’ll be notified with a red banner on your “Transfers” page if documents are needed.

If you’re having trouble verifying your documents, please review this article for information about document requirements and troubleshooting tips.

Using a business bank account to receive funds

To transfer funds to a business or organization’s bank account, you’ll want to select “A business bank account” when asked “Whose account will the funds be transferred to?”. Transfers will need to be set up by a Decision-Maker (this would be someone who is authorized to handle funds on behalf of the organization), and they will need to enter their personal information along with the organization’s bank info.

You can review more information about business bank transfers in this article.

Invite someone else to receive funds

When inviting someone else to receive GoFundMe funds, please ensure they meet all requirements to receive funds. The beneficiary must have a bank account in the same currency that the fundraiser is set up with. Once a beneficiary is added, their name will appear on the GoFundMe along with the organizer’s.

Please ensure your beneficiary has the following documents:

  • Official government-issued identification (Over 18 years of age). This can be a driver’s license, government-issued photo ID, or a passport.
  • Official bank statements including the following information:
    • The bank logo, or bank letterhead (must be issued by the bank).
    • Their name as the account holder.
    • The bank account number they will use to receive the funds.
    • The sort code if in the UK or Ireland.

Our payment processor may require this documentation to verify account details.

Once you’ve confirmed they meet the requirements, please follow the instructions below to add a beneficiary:

  • Log into your GoFundMe account and choose “Your fundraisers” from the top right menu
  • Choose the fundraiser where you’d like to add a beneficiary
    • US fundraisers - Select “Fundraiser” > “Transfers”
    • All other countries - Choose “Transfers”
  • Select “Someone else’s bank account” under “Whose account will the funds be transferred to?”
  • Make sure to confirm your own email, and then click “Get started” under “Invite a beneficiary”
  • Enter the beneficiary’s first and last name (this will show publicly on the fundraiser page) and their email address, then click next
  • Confirm the information you’ve entered is correct, and then select “Send invite”

If you receive an error message, please try sending the invitation to another email address. Your beneficiary may have an existing account that’s causing the error.

After the invite has been sent successfully, your beneficiary will receive an email to accept the funds.

Note: Please keep watch in your email inbox (and spam folder) for an email from our team titled, “Important Message from GoFundMe”. If you receive that, please respond there as soon as possible. Our team will need to work with you to complete further verification before funds can be sent.

My information has been sent for extra verification

We want to make sure your bank transfers are both safe and successful. For this reason, your outgoing transfers may be paused while we review your personal and bank details. This is a security measure to continue keeping your funds safe, and it may happen after some funds have already been transferred. We won’t be able to initiate anymore fund transfers until you’ve verified your account.

How do I verify my account?

To verify your account, you’ll need to upload documents, such as an ID or bank statement, through your GoFundMe account. Please review this article for more information or if you’re having trouble uploading documents.

How long will it take?

After you upload documents, please allow 1-2 business days for verification. Once your documents have been successfully uploaded, it may take an additional 2 or more business days for your funds to be transferred. You’ll receive an email when a new transfer has been initiated, and you can also double check for this information within your GoFundMe account.

Transferring money to your personal bank account

To transfer funds, please ensure you are over 18 years old and have all of the following documents:

  • Official government-issued identification. This can be a driver’s license or photo ID from your country or a passport from any country. It must not be expired.
  • Official bank statements including the following information:
    • Your bank logo, or bank letterhead (must be issued by the bank).
    • Your name as the account holder.
    • The bank account number you will use to transfer the funds.
    • Your sort code if in the UK or Ireland.

You should have verified your email when you started the fundraiser, but if you haven’t verified it yet, you’ll be prompted to do so before setting up transfers. Simply follow these instructions to have the donations sent to your personal bank account:

  • Log into your GoFundMe account and select “Your fundraisers” from the top right menu
  • Choose the fundraiser you want to set up transfers for
    • US fundraisers - Choose “Fundraiser” > “Transfers”
    • All other countries - Choose “Transfers”
  • Select “Your bank account” under “Whose account will the funds be transferred to?”
  • Select “Get started” and then “Continue to Adyen” or “Continue to Stripe”
  • Follow prompts to enter your personal and bank details in our payment partner’s secure portal

For personal details, you’ll be required to enter the following information:

  • Full first and last name (Ensure this name matches your identification document and the name on your bank account.)
  • Home address (No P.O. Box)
  • Phone number
  • Email address
  • Birthdate (must be over 18)
  • SSN or ITIN (US) or National Insurance Number (UK)
  • ID or Passport Number (Must not be expired)

For bank details, you can log in directly to your bank portal or you’ll be required to enter the following information and upload a bank statement to enter manually:

  • Account number or IBAN (European accounts)
  • Routing number (US) or Branch code (European accounts)
  • Provide a bank document

Important bank transfer expectations

After you add your banking information and set up transfers, new donations that come into your account will go through a verification process, which can take up to 5 business days (weekends and holidays do not count as business days).

Once they become available, all verified donations held within the account will be sent automatically at the frequency you choose (daily, weekly, monthly) and will be delivered to your bank account in 2-5 business days.

Note: Please keep watch in your email inbox (and spam folder) for an email from our team titled, “Important Message from GoFundMe”. If you receive that, please respond there as soon as possible. Our team will need to work with you to complete further verification, before funds can be sent.

ID Requirements

To ensure funds reach you safely, our payment partner may request you upload an image of your government-issued photo ID. The document must meet the following requirements:

  • Not expired, not a temporary ID (ID must belong to an individual 18+ years of age.)

  • Full legal name that matches bank statement and name entered for transfer set up

  • Clearly visible photo (Make sure there are no glare spots.)

  • Front and back of ID (as separate images) or full passport photo page

  • Show all information (Don’t cover or edit out any information.)

  • Include all four corners of the document

  • The file size must be at least 100 KB, but no larger than 10 MB

A few additional tips:
  • The easiest way to upload an ID document is to take an image with your smartphone camera. It doesn’t need to be a physical, scanned image.
  • Try to set the ID on a plain background.
  • JPGs work better than PDF formatting.

Please follow these steps to submit your ID for review:

Step 1: Navigate to your transfers:

  • Log into GoFundMe and choose “Your fundraisers” from the top right menu
  • Choose the fundraiser where you’re setting up transfers
    • US fundraisers - Choose “Fundraiser” from the left menu > then select “Transfers”
    • All other countries - Choose “Transfers” from the left menu
  • On your Transfers page click “Get started”

Step 2: Continue to our payment partner’s secure information portal and click on “Personal details”

Step 3: You’ll be prompted to start verification, click “Start verification”

Step 4: Choose your country and document type

Step 5: Upload your photo or take an image with your phone

After submitting, your document will be under review, and you can return to GoFundMe.

If these steps don’t match what you’re seeing in your account, please review this article instead.

Re-uploading a document

If you’ve already uploaded a document that wasn’t verified and need to upload a different one, follow these steps:

  • Initiate process by clicking the link provided in the email titled “Action required - withdrawal setup failed” OR click the red banner in the “Transfers” page of your GoFundMe dashboard
  • Then click “Continue to Adyen” or “Continue to Stripe”, depending on your payment partner
  • Click into the section, identity or bank, that has a red banner, and the button to upload will be in the bottom, right corner beside the “Edit” button

If you’re still having trouble with these steps, please submit this form.

Accessing your nonprofit’s data

Only nonprofits located in the US, Canada, the UK, Ireland, or Australia will be able to access more in depth donor data from fundraisers. Charities in other countries can still run a personal GoFundMe and withdraw funds to their organization’s bank account.

In order to access a nonprofit’s donor and fundraiser data, you’ll first need to submit a claim that verifies you work for the nonprofit.

If you work for a nonprofit based in the US, you’ll claim and manage your nonprofit account through GoFundMe Pro. Review this article for steps on how to claim your nonprofit account.

For nonprofits in Canada, the UK, Ireland, or Australia, please follow the steps below to complete the nonprofit claim process:

  • Sign up or log in to a GoFundMe account using your official nonprofit email address (e.g., myname@mynonprofit.org)).

  • Search for your nonprofit at this link and enter the BRN (CA), Charity Number (UK/IE), or ABN (AU).

  • Verify your identity by clicking the link in the email we send you and upload:

  • A government-issued photo ID

  • Document proving you are authorized to act on behalf of the nonprofit

We’ll review your connection within 2 business days and will follow up if additional documentation is required. Once verified, we’ll email you instructions to access your nonprofit’s donor and transaction data.

Claiming and managing your US nonprofit

Nonprofits based in the United States are supported by GoFundMe Pro. GoFundMe Pro is a best-in-class fundraising platform for nonprofits, and you’ll be able to continue managing your nonprofit as normal within GoFundMe Pro.

Follow these steps to claim your US-based nonprofit:

  • Claim your nonprofit, or go to gofundme.com, select “Sign up as a nonprofit” from the “Fundraise” menu
  • Select “Claim my Nonprofit Page”, and then search for your organization
  • Confirm you’ve selected the correct nonprofit by selecting, “Confirm and continue”
  • Create an account with your work email (If you already have an account, you’ll be prompted to sign in.)
  • Next you’ll verify your identity by sharing your full name, your role at the nonprofit you’re claiming, and your home address
  • If you’re automatically verified, you’ll be prompted to sign in to GoFundMe Pro and then brought to your dashboard
  • If we have trouble verifying your connection to the nonprofit, we’ll prompt you to upload documents (like a paystub or your ID)
  • Once your documents are confirmed, then you’ll be prompted to sign in to GoFundMe Pro and can access your nonprofit dashboard

If you’ve already claimed your US-based nonprofit with GoFundMe

You can now manage your nonprofit and access donor details within GoFundMe Pro. We’ve made it simple to gain access to your new Pro account.

You can log into your existing GoFundMe nonprofit account and there will be a banner letting you know about GoFundMe Pro. Choose “Manage page” to sign into your new account. You’ll also receive an email from us notifying you about the change.

The email will have the subject “Congrats! Your Nonprofit Page is verified”, and within the email, select “Get started” to access your GoFundMe Pro account. From that account you can manage your Nonprofit Page and review donor data.

Confirming your connection

To claim your nonprofit, you’ll need to sign up for a GoFundMe Nonprofit account. Use your official nonprofit email to sign up (eg. myname@mynonprofit.org).

Our team will review your submitted government-issued photo ID and the email address you’ve used to create your GoFundMe account.

If additional information is needed, we’ll contact you via email so you can submit this form to contact our team. The rest of this article will detail the information requested in this form.

Accepted documents

The following are examples of the types of documents you can attach to show you are authorized to act on behalf of the nonprofit:

  • A picture of your nonprofit’s government registration documents showing your name and role
  • A picture of your most recent pay stub from the nonprofit
  • A picture of the nonprofit’s bank statement, showing your name as an authorized signer

We’ll review the documents to confirm the photo ID submitted matches the name listed on the supporting documents or nonprofit website. Once our review of the documents is complete, we’ll respond within 1-2 business days to confirm your full access or request additional information.

While we recommend your Fundraising Manager claim your nonprofit, here are a list of roles we can approve as the nonprofit representative on GoFundMe:
  • Donor Relations
  • Fundraising Chair
  • Data Protection Officer
  • Individual giving manager
  • Community fundraisers/managers
  • Digital giving manager
  • Executive Director
  • Board Member
  • Director of Development
  • Treasurer/Assistant Treasurer

Could not confirm your connection to nonprofit

In the event we can’t confirm your connection to the nonprofit, we’ll ask for additional information. If we’re still unable to successfully verify your connection to the nonprofit, your GoFundMe account will be converted to an individual account. With an individual account, you can continue to fundraise on GoFundMe, but you won’t have access to donor data or the permissions to act on behalf of the nonprofit.

Understanding California AB488 and How It May Affect Your Nonprofit

A Guide for Charitable Organizations*

If your nonprofit organization or charity is fundraising through GoFundMe, GoFundMe Pro, or any other online platform, it’s essential to understand California’s AB 488 law. With the rapid growth of online fundraising, California introduced this law to ensure that donations are handled responsibly and transparently. Importantly, AB 488 applies to any nonprofit that solicits or receives donations from California-based donors—regardless of where the nonprofit is located. This guide will help you navigate AB 488 so that you can ensure that your nonprofit remains compliant and your fundraising efforts aren’t interrupted.

*This publication is for general information purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. You should consult your own legal advisor to understand how AB488 may impact your organization.

In this article:

  • What is California AB 488?
  • Good standing requirements under AB 488
  • How to remain in good standing under AB 488
  • FAQ
What is California AB 488?

California AB 488 is a law that regulates online charitable fundraising platforms, like GoFundMe. It took effect on January 1, 2023. Final regulations were approved in March 2024.

The goal of the law is to:

  • Increase transparency in online giving
  • Protect supporters and nonprofits
  • Ensure donations are delivered properly and on time

Under AB 488, online fundraising platforms must confirm that recipient nonprofits are in good standing with the California Attorney General, Franchise Tax Board, and IRS before letting them fundraise. Nonprofits must actively maintain their good standing to keep fundraising in California.

Tip: If you’re not sure how this law affects your organization, or how to stay in good standing, talk with a nonprofit attorney or compliance expert.

Good Standing Requirements Under AB 488

To fundraise on GoFundMe, or any other charitable fundraising platform operating in California, your nonprofit must be in good standing under California law. Under AB 488, good standing means:

  • The IRS hasn’t revoked your tax-exempt status.
  • The California Franchise Tax Board hasn’t revoked your tax-exempt status.
  • The California Attorney General hasn’t prohibited your organization from soliciting or operating in California.

If your nonprofit isn’t in good standing, GoFundMe Pro can’t process donations on your behalf.

You can check your status using these resources:

  • California Attorney General (AG): May not operate list
  • California Franchise Tax Board (FTB): Revoked exempt organizations list
  • IRS: Revocation list

If your organization appears on any of these lists, it’s considered out of good standing, and GoFundMe won’t be able to process donations to your organization.

How to Remain in Good Standing under AB 488

To keep fundraising without interruption, stay up to date with your required registrations and filings.

Tip: Talk with an attorney or compliance expert to find out whether your organization is up to date with its required registrations.

Register with the California Attorney General

If you receive donations from supporters in California, you may need to register and file annual reports with the Attorney General’s Registry of Charities and Fundraisers to confirm your nonprofit can fundraise in California.

  • Initial registration: File Form CT-1 with your Articles of Incorporation, bylaws, and IRS determination letter.
  • Annual renewal (Form RRF-1): Required for all registered nonprofits. If your revenue is more than $50,000, you may need to include financial statements or IRS Form 990.
  • Fundraising compliance: If you use commercial fundraisers or fundraising counsel, additional registration requirements may apply.

As of September 2026, the Registry has launched a new online filing service. Nonprofits can now submit required filings, upload supporting documents, make payments, and manage registrations electronically. Here are some steps you can take now:

  • Look for a letter from the Registry with your organization’s registration code, and keep it for your records.
  • Create an account through the Online Filing Service.
  • Link your organization’s registration record using your registration code and identifying information, like your FEIN.
  • Review the Registry’s program information and FAQs before you submit anything.
California Franchise Tax Board: State tax filings

To keep your California tax-exempt status, your nonprofit must file annual tax returns with the California Franchise Tax Board. Learn more on the Franchise Tax Board’s site for charities and nonprofits.

Annual filing requirements
  • Form 199N: For gross receipts of $50,000 or less.
  • Form 199: For gross receipts over $50,000.
Applying for California tax-exempt status
  • Form 3500: The standard application.
  • Form 3500A: For organizations the IRS has already recognized.
IRS: Federal tax filings and tax-exempt status

Your nonprofit must file annual returns with the IRS to keep its 501(c)(3) status active. If the IRS revokes your federal tax-exempt status, your organization may fall out of good standing under AB 488.

Annual IRS Form 990 filings

Which form you file depends on your organization’s gross receipts:

  • Form 990-N: For gross receipts of $50,000 or less.
  • Form 990-EZ: For gross receipts between $50,000 and $200,000.
  • Form 990: For gross receipts over $200,000.
Reinstating tax-exempt status

If your nonprofit has lost its federal tax-exempt status:

  • Check the IRS Revocation List.
  • Apply for reinstatement using Form 1023 or Form 1023-EZ.

Tip: Check your IRS status once a year to avoid surprise disruptions to your fundraising.

California Secretary of State: Business filings

If your nonprofit is registered and operates in California, you must file with the California Secretary of State every two years to stay active and in good standing.

Note: The Secretary of State doesn’t publish an operate list for AB 488 purposes. But if you fall out of good standing with the Secretary of State, your nonprofit could still end up on the Attorney General’s May not operate list.

Statement of Information

Your nonprofit will need to file a Statement of Information (SI-100 or SI-550). Here’s what to know:

  • File every two years.
  • Submit your first filing within 90 days of registration.
  • Pay a $20 filing fee.
Check your registration status

You can confirm your status by searching for your organization in the Bizfile Online portal.

FAQs About AB 488

Does AB 488 apply to all nonprofits, even those outside of California?

Yes. AB 488 impacts any nonprofit that receives online donations from California-based donors, including nonprofits that are located outside the state of California.

Why is my nonprofit still listed as out of compliance (i.e., on one of the three agency lists) after submitting all required filings?

Your nonprofit may still be listed as out of compliance due to backlog issues with the Attorney General’s Office, IRS, or California Franchise Tax Board. Even after submitting all required filings, processing delays can occur, preventing your compliance status from updating. To resolve this, regularly follow up with each agency to confirm receipt, keep records of your filings and any communication, and provide additional information if requested to speed up the process.

What happens if my nonprofit is non-compliant with AB 488?

Non-compliance may result in the suspension of your online fundraising activities and preventing your organization from receiving donations until you take steps to come back into compliance.

How can my nonprofit ensure compliance when fundraising online?

To the extent applicable, ensure proper registration with the Attorney General, Franchise Tax Board, and Federal Internal Revenue Services. Stay up to date with annual reporting and financial disclosure requirements to maintain good standing and continue fundraising without disruptions.

Where can I learn more about AB488?

For official information, the best resource is the California Attorney General’s website dedicated to charitable fundraising platforms. This site includes the full text of the law, the finalized regulations, and FAQs/instructions for organizations on how to comply​. This should be your first stop for understanding registration processes or definitions under AB 488.

If you have specific questions about how AB 488 applies to your nonprofit, consider reaching out to a professional. Nonprofit attorneys or compliance consultants familiar with fundraising law can give tailored guidance. AB 488 introduces some complex new obligations, so getting expert advice can be worthwhile – especially if your organization is heavily involved in online fundraising.

Stay Compliant and Keep Fundraising

Compliance with AB 488 ensures your nonprofit can continue fundraising without interruption and avoid penalties. By following the steps above and using available resources, your organization can stay in good standing and focus on making a difference.

If you made a donation and it’s not appearing on the GoFundMe, this is likely due to processing times.

Our payment partners and our Trusty & Safety team review donations before finalizing them. In rare cases, this means that once you complete a donation it can take up to 5 business days to process and become available on the GoFundMe.

Please refrain from making several donation attempts. If you don’t see your donation after 5 business days, please contact our Support team.

Why can’t I see my donation in my GoFundMe account?

If you made a donation and it isn’t appearing within your GoFundMe account, this is likely because you weren’t signed in when making the donation. Please contact our team if you’d like past donations made with the same email to be added to your account. For future donations, make sure you’re signed in, and your donation will automatically appear within your account.

Note: Donations can’t be moved between GoFundMe accounts, so even if you create a new account under the same email, we won’t be able to transfer donations made under an old, deactivated account.

Currency conversion

If you donate to a fundraiser in a currency different from your own, you’ll be charged in the fundraiser’s currency. This means the charge on your card could be higher or lower than what you might expect.

To see if this might be the case, you can confirm the currency used on the fundraiser page. If you donated more or less than you meant to in a different currency, please submit a refund request here.

Other pending charges

Credit card companies sometimes show attempted charges on statements as a security precaution to let their customers know how their cards are being used.

If you attempted to make your donation a few times, you may see several pending charges on your bank statement, even if you only successfully donated once. These additional pending charges will usually drop off within 24 - 48 hours of your payment attempt. If they don’t drop off within 48 hours, please contact us.

Trouble making a donation

Carefully enter your information

Please use your own card and email address to make a donation. Do not use someone else’s card or donate on their behalf. For the payment information, you need to enter your name exactly as it shows on the card or the digital payment account you’re using. During check out, you’ll be able to donate as “Anonymous” on the fundraiser page by checking the box next to “Don’t display my name publicly on the fundraiser,” but you’ll need to use your real name for payment details.

If you’re receiving an error message or your donation isn’t processing, please try the following:
  • Be sure the information and spelling you entered matches the card or the digital payment account you’re using. We’ve found this is the most common reason for an error during the donation process, so it’s important to double check.
  • Clear your internet’s history by clearing cache and cookies, using a private browser, or trying a different device.
  • Use a different payment method, or contact your bank to ensure there are sufficient funds and the bank isn’t blocking the transaction.
  • Make sure the fundraiser is accepting donations. If the fundraiser hasn’t fully been launched and is still in draft mode, or if the organizer has turned donations off, donating will not be an option.

Multiple donation attempts within a short amount of time may result in the donation failing for your protection. If you’ve tried all of the above steps and still can’t complete the donation, it’s best to wait 24 hours and then try making your donation again. If you’re still having trouble, you can click the “Contact Us” button to contact our team.

What are recurring donations?

A recurring donation enables donors to support fundraisers on a monthly basis. Donors can make their initial donation, choose the monthly option, (if available on the fundraiser) and for a 5% fee (detailed below), GoFundMe will automatically process their donation every month without additional effort from the donor. They’re easy to set up, always protected by the GoFundMe Giving Guarantee, and donors can change or cancel their recurring donation at any time.

*At this time, recurring monthly donations can only be made using credit or debit cards, PayPal, or by bank transfer.

How can I set up a recurring donation?

Donors will have the option to make their donations a monthly recurring donation at checkout if the GoFundMe organizer has allowed monthly donations.

When will I be charged for my recurring donation?

Donors will be charged each month on the same day as their original donation, or the next closest day. For example, if you make your original donation on June 20th, you’ll be charged again on July 20th. If you make a donation on the 31st, and the next month only has 30 days, you’ll be charged on the 30th.

Do recurring donations have a fee?

For donors, yes. For organizers, no—our pricing remains the same. It’s always free to start and manage your fundraiser on GoFundMe. As a donor, if you choose recurring donations, there’s a recurring fee of 5% per donation in addition to our transaction fee that’s applied to each donation. (Transaction fees vary by country, so check out our pricing page for additional information on fees and fee information for your country.) Fees help us make recurring donations safe and effective while ensuring GoFundMe remains free for people who start a fundraiser.

Where can I find my recurring donation receipts?

You’ll receive a receipt to your email each month when your recurring donation is made, but you can also access your receipts from within your GoFundMe account. Follow these steps to access your receipts for monthly donations:

  • Sign up/in with the email associated with your recurring donation. Don’t worry if you don’t have an account - you’ll still be able to see your donations once you verify your email.

  • Your recurring donations will be listed at the top, choose the donation you’d like receipts from
  • Click on “Past donations”
  • Your receipts will be listed by the dates they were made, and you can click into each of them

Of course! We know circumstances can change, so donors can cancel their recurring donation at any time. Donors can cancel their recurring donation from their monthly email receipt of the charge or from within the “Donations you’ve made” section of their GoFundMe account.

  • Sign up/in with the email associated with your recurring donation.
  • Log in to your GoFundMe account
  • Choose “Donations you’ve made” from the menu in the top right corner

Tax information for donors

Is my donation Tax-Deductible?

Donations made to a personal GoFundMe fundraiser, rather than a nonprofit fundraiser, are generally considered to be personal gifts and aren’t guaranteed to be tax-deductible. But, you can always check with a tax professional to be sure. You won’t be issued a tax receipt from GoFundMe.

Donations to these fundraisers are collected by PayPal Giving Fund, a non-profit organization, or GoFundMe Pro, and distributed to the designated nonprofit.

Donations made to personal GoFundMes are generally considered to be “personal gifts” which, for the most part, are not taxed as income in the United States. Additionally, these donations are not tax deductible for donors.

However, there may be particular case-specific instances where the income is taxable for organizers. For example, if the donations are considered income to the recipient.

The best way to ensure you’re in compliance with the tax laws is to maintain adequate records of donations received and consult with a tax professional. Unexpected refund from GoFundMe

If you’ve received an unexpected refund, we’re here to help. We think it’s wonderful that you’ve chosen to support someone through GoFundMe in their time of need. However, because every fundraiser has different circumstances, there can be times when unrequested refunds will be issued. You could receive an unexpected refund because:

  • The GoFundMe organizer decided to deactivate their fundraiser
  • The donation couldn’t be verified
  • The GoFundMe organizer didn’t verify their account or transfer funds within required deadlines
  • The donation was disputed with a card provider
  • The donation was made by the GoFundMe organizer

The GoFundMe organizer decided to deactivate their fundraiser

The GoFundMe organizer may have decided to deactivate their fundraiser and refund all donors if funds are no longer needed. If you’re a donor, it would be best to get in touch with the GoFundMe organizer for more information. If the fundraiser is still active, you can use the “Contact” or “Message” button to reach out to them. If it’s deactivated, we can assist you in contacting them if needed.

The donation couldn’t be verified

Our Trust & Safety team or our payment partners may proactively refund donations if we’re unable to verify a donor’s identity. These security measures are designed to protect GoFundMe organizers from fraudulent donations. If you’re a donor and would like to contribute again, we can work with our payment processor to verify your identity by reviewing your government-issued ID or a link to your social media, such as Facebook or LinkedIn. Please get in touch with us by clicking “Contact Us” to get that process started.

The GoFundMe organizer didn’t verify their account or transfer funds within required deadlines

Due to federal regulations, our payment processors can’t hold funds for extended periods of time. Our team sends several reminders to the GoFundMe organizer to assist them with the transfer process.

If a GoFundMe organizer doesn’t verify their account within the required time frame, their donations will be automatically refunded in full back to donors. Donors will receive funds back to the card used to make the donation within 3-10 business days.

The donation was disputed with a card provider

Donors have the right to dispute their donation with their card issuer. This usually happens if the donor doesn’t recognize the charge on their bank statement.

This results in the card issuer pulling the funds from the GoFundMe organizer’s payment account (not their personal bank account). If you’re a GoFundMe organizer, read this article to learn more about addressing a disputed charge.

The donation was made by the GoFundMe organizer

GoFundMe organizers aren’t allowed to donate to their own personal fundraisers, even if the donation was given to them by someone else by cash or check.

This can be seen as a cash advance in the banking world, and these donations will need to be refunded.

Unrecognized charge on bank statement

Our Trust & Safety team works around the clock to prevent unauthorized transactions on GoFundMe. If you’re seeing a charge from a donation you don’t remember making, we’re here to help you get to the bottom of it.

Here are a couple things to check if you see a charge from GoFundMe you don’t recognize:

Do you have a receipt from a recent donation?

Search your inbox for “Thank you for your donation to”, and any receipts for donations made to a GoFundMe should populate. Seeing the receipt might jog your memory about an intentional donation you made.

Did you make a donation with an online payment method, like PayPal or Google Pay?

Check your transactions for any online payment method you have set up through your card or bank account. Narrowing down the payment method could help you remember whether the charge was intentional or not.

Did a household member use your card to make a donation?

Unrecognized charges may have been placed by a family member, friend, or coworker who may have had your permission to use your payment method. We suggest reaching out to anyone who may have had access to your financial information or to your device.

If these don’t apply, and you didn’t make a recent donation to a GoFundMe, please contact our team here.

Cards can be compromised and used at any merchant across the web, so it’s important to also contact your card provider to report the activity if you think you may need to cancel your card.

Received a donation receipt but did not make a donation

We always recommend reviewing your bank statement first. If you don’t see any unrecognized charges, it’s possible someone entered the wrong email when making their donation. A beneficial owner is someone with significant responsibility to control, manage, or direct the organization (e.g., a Chief Executive Officer, Vice President, Treasurer, or a controller).

Raising money for multiple nonprofits at once

GoFundMe nonprofit fundraisers can only connect to one benefitting organization per fundraiser. If you would like to raise money for multiple organizations, you can do one of the following:

  • Create a different fundraiser for each organization that you would like to fundraise for. Since the fundraisers will be separate, you can choose a different nonprofit for each.
  • Create one personal fundraiser and transfer funds to your own bank account. Once you have received the funds, you can split the money between the organizations. Keep in mind, you will need to be explicit in your story about how the funds will be shared between the entities.

Raising money for a nonprofit that isn’t eligible for a nonprofit fundraiser

If you want to use GoFundMe to fundraise for your nonprofit, but your organization is not a United States 501(c)3 public charity, registered charity with the UK Charity Commission, Canada Revenue Agency, or Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission, one option available to you is fiscal sponsorship.

Fiscal sponsorship means that you would set up a nonprofit fundraiser for an eligible nonprofit. This nonprofit (known as your fiscal sponsor) will collect funds raised on your behalf, then honor a predetermined agreement to give those funds to you at the end of the fundraising period. Use the steps in the section above to create a nonprofit fundraiser using the fiscal sponsor’s tax ID. You will be the fundraiser organizer, so you will be responsible for sharing the fundraiser and asking for donations. Be sure to understand how your fiscal sponsor receives funds and the scheduled frequency to plan how funds will be transferred to your intended organization in the end, and within what time frame.

All language on GoFundMe (including confirmation emails and donation receipts) will indicate that the fiscal sponsor is the benefiting organization. It is the responsibility of the recipient organization to work with the fiscal sponsor to communicate to donors how their funds will be used. We recommend including language in the story section of your fundraiser to explain the fiscal sponsorship relationship.

Crowdfunding is when a group of people come together and donate to a cause they care about. How to share your fundraiser with the most potential donors

We all have circles of connections in our lives. Robin Dunbar is a psychologist who created a theory about the idea of having inner, middle, and outer circles of relationships–each with varying degrees of closeness. We’ve listed out some ways you can share your fundraiser to connect with each circle:

  • Your inner circle
  • Your middle circle
  • Your outer circle

Alert your inner circle

Your inner circle knows you the best. They likely already know why you’re fundraising and need little convincing to help. Let them know about your fundraiser directly: talk to them face-to-face, give them a call, or send a text.

You should also consider this group as partners on your fundraiser. You might send them co-organizer invites so they can actively participate. You can also enlist their help to share your fundraiser with people you might not know personally. Here are some ways you might ask your inner circle for help:

  • Have them generate a GoFundMe sharing poster with the “Share” button on the fundraiser page, and ask them to post it along with a personal story on their social accounts (Facebook, Instagram, etc.)
  • Print QR code fliers and ask them to share them with groups they’re a part of. For example, a pickleball group, school community, or book club.

Connect with your middle circle

For your middle circle, these are people you speak to often, but they might not know what’s happening in your personal life. This is where you’ll start sharing on social media and in more public ways. You’ll share more information as you explain what’s happening and how people can help.

This is a good time to take stock of what communities you’re a part of. We interact with more people than we might initially think. Some examples of community groups that might fall into your middle circle are coworkers, extended family in other states, high school or college alumni groups, sports leagues you take part in, faith-based groups, etc.

Take advantage of group forums (Facebook pages, Slack groups, Newsletters) and call on the connection you have with them. Leaning into your connection and shared experiences is what will inspire potential supporters to learn more about your cause and lead to more donations. Let’s look at two different ways to share with different communities:

Your high school alumni group

“Hi all, it’s been a while since I’ve talked to some of you, but I wanted to take a moment to share the GoFundMe I’ve started [Insert Link]. I’ve already felt a great deal of love and support from the generous people who’ve donated, but we’re still working towards reaching our goal. I always felt our graduating class had a strong connection, so if you’re able to spare a moment to read through my story and consider donating, I’d deeply appreciate it. Hope you’re all doing well, and go Tigers!”

Your faith-based group

“Hello. Some unforeseen circumstances have led me to need to lean on my church family for support. I’ve chatted to and volunteered with many of you, and I feel so welcomed by this congregation. If you’re able, please read more about why I’m raising funds and consider making a donation: [Insert Link] I’d also greatly appreciate if you could share my fundraiser with your friends and family. Please feel free to reach out and ask questions at our next service.”

Reach your outer circle

If you’ve already had some success, it’s time to start focusing on your outer circle. Keep in mind that repetition is important for connecting with people in your outer circle and beyond. They might need to hear about your fundraiser a few times before they fully understand and connect to your need. Let’s look at some specific ways to share on and off social media to reach your outer circle.

Offline
  • Take advantage of the QR flier GoFundMe provides within your sharing options. Print a few copies and post them on bulletin boards and other (legal) public places.
  • Talk about your fundraiser. Word of mouth works, so continue to bring your fundraiser up in conversation.
  • Put together a pitch for your local news outlet.
Instagram
  • Use Instagram stories where links are clickable. They’re the first thing people see when they open Instagram, and they disappear after 24 hours, so posting multiple times feels natural.
    • Stories are the perfect place to use the GoFundMe sharing posters because they’re a great visual, and you can easily add your link below the poster.
  • Add your GoFundMe link to your Instagram bio, and be sure to reference it when posting to your grid so viewers can access a clickable link.
  • Post once or twice to your grid, and then reference those posts in your stories. Choose encouraging and uplifting photos since photos of smiling people tend to lead to more donations.
  • What to post:
    • Use your stories to post goal updates.
    • Share anecdotes about what your fundraiser means to you.
    • Tag and thank donors to show your appreciation.
TikTok
  • Keep it short and sweet. Include important details about why you’re raising funds, be sure to include your link, and ask people to share your post and fundraiser link.
  • Ask people to leave comments with details and words of support for your cause. Comments are highly favored in TikTok’s algorithm, so they’ll help boost your post.
  • Keep your posts fresh. If your video is more than a week old, it likely won’t show on feeds, so post at least once a week or so.
  • Post at the right time. The best time is usually in the afternoon to evening, when more people have time to scroll.
  • Use trending hashtags or sounds that relate to your fundraiser. This will help TikTok surface your video to people that might resonate with your content.
Facebook
  • Share to Facebook groups you’re a part of. This is a great way to reach more people compared to just posting to your own timeline.
  • Post at the right time. Studies suggest that mid-morning, between 9am-12pm EST, on weekdays tends to be the best time to post.
  • Post multiple times a week to reach those who might miss your posts, or to remind those who saw and were too busy to take a look.
  • Create a Facebook event, include your link in the description, and invite your Facebook friends to it.
  • For other helpful tips, check out our Blog post with Facebook Fundraising tips

Remember, you’ll need to share often and in multiple places for the length of your fundraiser in order to meet your goal. Hopefully these tips will help you to connect with potential donors within your widest circle of acquaintances, but it’s also important to ask other people to share so you can reach their potential connections as well. In crowdfunding, the amount of funds you receive is largely dependent on the amount of people who see your fundraiser, so asking others to share through social media, over text, or just when talking to them can be incredibly impactful. Learn about GoFundMe fees

No fee to create a fundraiser

It’s always free to start and manage a fundraiser on GoFundMe. To help us cover payment processing, there’s a transaction fee per donation. For more information about fees in your country, check out our pricing page where you can see example fees for different amounts raised. When donors make a single donation, they can leave an optional contribution to help GoFundMe remain free for people who start a fundraiser. Transaction fees are deducted automatically from donations; the person receiving funds will receive all funds raised minus these transaction fees. They can review what fees have been deducted under the “Breakdown” portion of the “Transfers” page within their GoFundMe account.

Fee example: If a donor makes a $50 donation to a US fundraiser, the individual receiving funds will receive $48.25 once transaction fees (2.9% + $0.30) are deducted. (Use the fee calculator on our pricing page to see a fee example for your country.)

What is a transaction fee?

The transaction fee covers the costs of credit, debit, and ACH charges, safely delivering donations, and helps us offer more ways to donate—through credit, debit, ACH, PayPal, Venmo, Apple Pay, or Google Pay (for most fundraisers). This is the only fee deducted on GoFundMe for a single donation to cover all your fundraising needs.

Voluntary tipping by donors

Donors have the option to leave a voluntary tip for GoFundMe. By leaving a voluntary tip, you’re helping us to do more good in the world, and your generosity helps us provide a safe and secure experience for you and others in our community.

Tipping is optional, and the tip amount can be changed to 0. The GoFundMe organizer will not see whether or not their donors leave a tip to GoFundMe. Instructions for how to edit the tip amount are here. If you’ve made an unintentional tip, you can request a refund.

Running your fundraiser with recurring donations

Please note: Some fundraisers in Italy and Canada may not have recurring donations as an option. Thank you for your patience as we work to bring this feature to all of our customers.

Recurring donations are a great way to receive sustainable, long-term support. If supporters choose recurring donations, they pay a fee of 5% per donation to help make this feature possible for our community. In this article, we’ll address:

  • What are recurring donations?
  • Understanding the recurring donation fee
  • Managing recurring donors
  • Running a fundraiser for a nonprofit with recurring donations
  • Asking supporters to become recurring donors
  • Keeping recurring donors engaged

What are recurring donations?

Recurring donations are an opt-in feature for your supporters to automatically donate to you on a monthly basis. New fundraisers allow both single and recurring donations, but you can also choose to only allow one-time or monthly donations within your fundraiser settings.

Follow these steps to edit what types of donations your fundraiser can receive:

  • From your “Today” page, choose the “Fundraiser” tab
  • Then choose “Change fundraiser settings”
  • You’ll see the option to “Allow donations”
  • With “Allow donations” toggled on, you can then choose between “Monthly and one-time,” “One-time only,” and “Monthly only”
  • Choose the setting you’d like for your GoFundMe and then click “Save”

If you choose to allow both donation types on your GoFundMe, you can also choose your default donation type under “Default donation type.” This setting controls which option will be pre-selected for supporters when they click “Donate” from your fundraiser. We suggest using our “Smart default” option, as we use supporter data to automatically pre-set the best option for them, but you can also manually set one-time or monthly as your default.

We recommend, and all fundraisers will default to, having one time and monthly donations turned on. Check out this article for other common questions about recurring donations.

Understanding the recurring donation fee

Recurring donations are a way for supporters to contribute to fundraisers on an ongoing basis. This feature allows donors to make automatic monthly contributions, providing fundraisers with steady, long-term support.

Unlike one-time donations, which have a one-time processing fee of 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction (varies by country, check out our pricing page for your country’s fee), recurring donations have an additional 5% fee that repeats with each monthly payment. This additional donor-paid fee helps cover the costs of maintaining and managing this feature, including:

  • Automated payment processing – Ensuring secure and seamless transactions every month.
  • Improved support for fundraisers - Helping fundraisers engage with recurring donors more effectively.
  • Sustained platform improvements – Continuing to build tools that make fundraising easier and more impactful.

Supporters opt-in to recurring donations and the 5% fee when they donate, and this fee won’t affect funds received by the fundraiser. By opting into recurring donations, supporters help fundraisers gain reliable, ongoing contributions that make a lasting difference.

If you allow recurring donations on your fundraiser, you can check in on those supporters from the “Supporters” tab of your GoFundMe account.

From this tab you can filter to just view monthly donors so you can see how many times they’ve contributed and send them messages.

Asking supporters to become recurring donors

Recurring donations are great for fundraisers that have an ongoing need. To encourage supporters to opt in to monthly donations, you’ll want to clearly explain how funds will continue to be used over time. For example, if you’re raising funds to pay for schooling, we suggest adding a breakdown and timeline for why funds will be needed more than just once.

Ex: “I’ll use next month’s recurring donations to buy books for my next semester of classes. I’ll also put some of the donations aside to continue saving for my new laptop. With three more months of recurring donations, I’ll be able to purchase it!”

You can also ask supporters to become recurring donors in these additional ways:
  • You can include the ask in your fundraiser story. This will make clear to everyone who visits your fundraiser page that you have a long-term cause they can continue to contribute to. You might include a line in your story like:

“Please consider opting in to monthly donations to my fundraiser. I would truly appreciate the continued support, and even just $5 or $10 on a monthly basis can really make a difference.”

  • Reach out directly to people you know personally. Your closest friends and family will likely be thrilled by the opportunity to support you on an ongoing basis. We suggest texting or calling them to tell them about your fundraiser and how their monthly support would make a difference to you.
  • Ask donors to become monthly supporters in updates. Updates will remain publicly on your fundraiser page, and it’s a chance to highlight your recurring donation request to make sure it isn’t lost in the content of your fundraiser story. Here’s an example of how you might ask visitors to your page to become recurring supporters:
  • “Thank you so much for taking a moment to read about my fundraiser for my classroom! I wanted to share with visitors to my page that my fundraiser accepts monthly donations as well, and I’d love for you to consider becoming a monthly supporter. Monthly supporters will help fund field trips, school fairs, and other monthly activities for the students. And if you aren’t able to contribute right now, please still consider sharing my fundraiser with your social networks to help spread the word. Thank you!”

Keeping recurring donors engaged

Running a fundraiser is never a ‘set it and forget it’ affair, but you’ll want to be particularly purposeful about keeping your fundraiser up-to-date with recurring supporters, because they’re providing ongoing support. You can keep recurring supporters engaged by posting updates, sharing new photos in your story, posting a video about your progress, and more.

Your recurring supporters want to know they’re continuing to make a difference, so here are some ideas for keeping them engaged:

  • Draft a personalized thank you just for your recurring supporters. Whenever a recurring donation comes in for the month, be sure to thank them for their continued support, and share a little bit about how you’ve used their funds.
  • Shout out your recurring supporters in an update. YouTubers and podcasters will often read off the names of their monthly donors at the end of an episode, and you can do something similar with a monthly shout out in the form of an update to your fundraiser page. All subscribed donors receive an email when you post an update, so it will be a nice gesture of your gratitude.
  • Ask your recurring supporters to leave comments and include a blurb about why they choose to donate monthly. This could encourage other donors to opt in to monthly donations as well.
  • Take to social media to thank your monthly supporters if you’re already friends with them on socials. Be sure to tag them in your post, and ask them to share it as well to reach their social networks, too.

There are so many ways you can make the most of recurring donations, and we’re excited to see how our organizers rally their supporters and communities around this feature. What to include

Having two to three paragraphs should give you enough space to share your thoughts, but our data shows you’ll want your story to be at least 100 words. Shorter stories tend not to perform as well. Important things to include are 1) who you are and what is your need, 2) some details about what happened or what will be happening, 3) how the funds will be spent or reach the person you’re raising money for, and 4) a thank you and share request. If you’re raising money for others, share how you’re connected to the recipient.

We know there are a wide range of reasons why you may be starting a GoFundMe - and with that can come many different emotions. Telling people how you really feel about the situation you’re in can help supporters relate to your cause, and your readers will appreciate the sincerity. Being personable and honest in your story can help supporters feel connected to your cause and more inclined to donate.

Double check grammar, tone, and readability

Spelling and grammar mistakes can be distracting to your supporters, so make sure that you double check your story before publishing it. Using an online spell check is a great way to catch any hidden typos!

Additionally, you can further improve the impact and readability of your story by using editing features. Use bold and italic distinctions, and break your text into paragraphs or sections so it’s easier to read.

Your first week of fundraising

The first week after creating your fundraiser is an opportunity to build interest and momentum. The amount of time you put into sharing your fundraiser and interacting with supporters can have a big impact on your success. Let’s take a look at 5 steps you can take in your first week:

  • Make a plan
  • Share your fundraiser
  • Invite co-organizers
  • Ask others to share
  • Set up transfers

Make a plan

Making a plan will help keep your fundraiser on track and help you stay organized.

Try scheduling 20 minutes of fundraiser related tasks every day. This might include posting on social media, sending texts or calling people you know personally, posting updates, or thanking donors.

Share your fundraiser

No matter how and where you decide to share, you should share your fundraiser multiple times in your first week of fundraising, and then continue to share for as long as you run your fundraiser. GoFundMe provides sharing posters when you use the “Share fundraiser” button from your manage dashboard. These will include your main image, a goal bar, and a call to donate. We encourage using these posters for a great visual to share, and you can share in many other ways as well–check out this video for tips on sharing well on social media.

Some additional must-haves for sharing are:

  • Include your link
  • Share a short message about why you’re fundraising
  • Ask others to share as well

We also suggest checking out this blog post on how to ask for donations.

Invite co-organizers

Co-organizers can make fundraising less overwhelming. They can help with fundraiser maintenance so you can focus on your cause and putting the funds to use. We encourage you to invite 2-3 co-organizers who you trust and have some time to help you out.

Ask others to share

Continually sharing your fundraiser is key to reaching your goal, and you can vastly expand your reach by asking friends, family, and co-organizers to share your fundraiser with their own networks. You can expand your individual network by tapping into the communities of others.

Set up transfers

At some point in the first week, take a few minutes to set up your transfers. Fully setting up transfers sometimes includes submitting identity and bank documents and can take a few days, so the sooner you get started setting up your transfers, the better.

Stay safe while sharing

GoFundMe and any social media platforms you share on are public, which means bad actors have the opportunity to view both your fundraiser and posts you make about it. Occasionally, bad actors may try to reach out through social media to advertise crowdfunding help. GoFundMe will never offer fundraiser promotion for a fee, and we don’t condone or suggest using any services that do.

Keep the following tips in mind when sharing on social media:
  • Never share your GoFundMe account or personal and banking details over social media.
  • Don’t engage with individuals offering to promote your fundraiser for a fee.
  • Don’t click links sent in DMs unless you trust the sender.

Sharing your fundraiser on social media is incredibly important to reach your goal, and we want you to feel confident and safe while sharing. Following our tips will help you to avoid bad actors, but if you do come across something suspicious, please let our team know at accountsecurity@gofundme.com.

Transferring funds

Bank transfer deadlines

Once the first donation is made to your fundraiser, your bank transfer information must be fully verified by the date noted within your transfer notification emails from GoFundMe, or your donations could be automatically refunded. If you aren’t sure if you’ve received an email notification, you can search for the subject “Action Required: Set up withdrawals”.

There is a transfer deadline because our payment partners are online payment processors only and, unlike a bank, are not able to hold your funds for long periods of time.

Even after starting bank transfers, you can continue to raise funds, and the amount raised displayed on your fundraiser will not decrease.

How long will it take for transfers to be fully verified?

The process of setting up and verifying transfers can take up to 7 business days or longer to complete. Then, once a transfer is sent, it will take 2-5 business days for the funds to safely be deposited into the bank account on file.

Changing bank transfer frequency

Once bank transfers are fully verified, they’ll automatically start to send on a daily, weekly, or monthly frequency and can’t be paused. This is due to laws and regulations that limit the amount of time our payment processors can hold your funds. We also strive to make payouts reliable and fast. This means that depending on our payment partners, you may receive multiple bank transfers on a given day.

Follow these steps to update your transfer frequency:

  • Log into your GoFundMe account
  • Select “Your fundraisers” from the top right menu and choose the fundraiser you want to change transfer frequency for
    • US fundraisers - From your “Today” page, select “Fundraiser” > select the “Transfers” button
    • All other countries - From your fundraiser dashboard, select “Transfers”
  • Select the button to the right of “Payout” (It will display your current choice of frequency.)
  • Choose your preference and save changes

Weekly transfers initiate on Mondays, and monthly transfers initiate on the 1st of each month. Once initiated, transfers take 2-5 business days to reach the bank account on file.

The first transfer will always initiate the day after you’ve successfully verified your bank transfer information, and will arrive in your account 2-5 business days after it’s sent. Every transfer after the first will then send according to the frequency you’ve chosen, as funds are received and verified.

The beneficiary has passed away

In the unfortunate situation that a beneficiary has passed away, our team is here to help. This article details your options in the event of a deceased beneficiary.

Choosing a transfer type

Choosing the right transfer type allows your funds to arrive smoothly. After navigating to your “Transfers” page, there are a couple of options to choose from, depending on where funds are being sent.

Who will receive the funds - Myself, a business, or someone else

Your bank account:

Choosing “Your bank account” means you’ll be transferring funds to your own personal bank account. (Do not select “Your bank account” and then add another individual’s personal banking information.)

A business bank account:

This option allows you to send funds to a bank account owned by a business or organization. (You must be a financial administrator for the organization or business to add their banking information.)

Someone else’s bank account:

Choosing “Someone else’s bank account” means funds will be transferred to someone else’s personal or business bank account. This option lets you add that person as your beneficiary, giving them sole bank transfer control and allowing them to enter their bank information directly.

Choose wisely! Only one person can receive funds from each fundraiser at a time, and the current beneficiary and organizer’s consent is required to change a beneficiary.

The person receiving funds will need to meet bank transfer requirements.

What type of bank account is right for you - Personal vs Business

  • Personal: This means an individual will be receiving funds to their own bank account. Also, in the US, sole proprietorships under a social security number.
  • Business, Company, or Partnership: This means funds are going to an organization’s bank account. Also sole proprietorships under an EIN.

Deceased organizer or beneficiary of a fundraiser

In the unfortunate event that the organizer or beneficiary of a GoFundMe has passed away, we can help. We can transfer the fundraiser to someone else, end the fundraiser, or potentially refund all donations.

We know this is a very challenging time, and we want to make the change process as simple as possible. To make any of these changes, the executor or administrator of the deceased organizer or beneficiary’s estate will need to be prepared with the following documents:

  • Government-issued photo ID: A clear photo of the executor or administrator’s valid driver’s license, national identification card, or passport, laid on a piece of paper with the day’s date written on it.
  • Obituary or death certificate: A copy of the deceased organizer or beneficiary’s obituary or death certificate.
  • Will or legal documentation: A copy of the deceased organizer or beneficiary’s will or other legal documentation appointing the executor.

Click “Contact Us” to get started with this process.

Transfer the fundraiser and funds to the executor or administrator

The fundraiser and funds can be transferred to the executor or administrator of the deceased organizer or beneficiary’s estate. Transferring the fundraiser would allow the executor or administrator to manage the fundraiser, which includes making public updates to the fundraiser, and if the original organizer is listed as the recipient of the funds, to set up bank transfers to receive the funds.

This is a good option if the fundraiser will continue and bank transfers weren’t set up by the original organizer.

End the fundraiser and transfer funds to the deceased organizer or beneficiary’s account

By ending the fundraiser, no one will be able to continue donating, and the account will remain in the original organizer’s name. If bank transfers were set up and verified, any remaining funds will be transferred to the bank account on file. If the original organizer was listed as the recipient of the funds, then the executor or administrator can work with the original organizer’s bank to manage the funds.

End the fundraiser and refund donations

The decision to end the fundraiser and refund all untransferred funds can be made by the executor or administrator. Refunding donors is a good option if the donated funds are no longer needed for the purpose(s) stated in the fundraiser.

Transfer FAQs

Can I withdraw money from GoFundMe before I reach my goal?

Yes. You don’t need to reach your goal to receive funds. You can set up transfers at any time, and funds are sent after we verify your information.

How much does GoFundMe charge to withdraw funds?

There is no fee to withdraw your GoFundMe funds. At the time of donation, a one-time fee of 2.9%+$.30 per donation will be deducted (based on US fundraisers, for your countries specific fees, visit our Pricing Page), but there is no fee to recieve your funds. See our fees article.

Can I transfer to PayPal, Venmo, or Cash App?

Funds are sent by direct deposit to a bank account only. While they may have account and routing numbers, online payment processors like PayPal, Venmo, and Cash App often don’t meet our document requirements, so it’s best to use a bank account instead.

Do I need a bank account to receive funds?

Yes. We can’t send checks.

Can I change the bank account for a fundraiser?

Yes. Edit the bank account on file or add a beneficiary to the fundraiser.

Handling chargebacks

What is a chargeback?

A chargeback happens when a donor contacts their card provider and disputes the payment associated with their donation. Their bank or card issuer then removes the funds from your GoFundMe payment account and sets them aside while they review the chargeback request.

Depending on the amount of donations currently processing from your fundraiser, this can sometimes result in a negative balance in your GoFundMe payment account. Future donations will first be used to cover the negative balance, and then your donations will continue to process and send to your account on file as usual.

Chargebacks usually happen when a donor doesn’t recognize the charge on their account and calls their card company out of concern. You can use the following options if you’d like to dispute or challenge the chargeback.

How do I handle a chargeback on my GoFundMe?

Depending on the situation, you can handle a chargeback of a donation to your GoFundMe in a few ways.

I know the donor, and they did not intentionally dispute the donation

If you know the donor personally, try reaching out to them to remind them what their donation was for. Then, they can call their card provider to drop the chargeback or donate to your fundraiser again.

I don’t know the donor, but I want to challenge the chargeback

If you don’t know the donor and wish to challenge the chargeback, please contact our team by clicking “Contact us”, and we’ll work with the parties involved to try to resolve this.

Otherwise, you can concede to the chargeback and return your donor’s payment back to them.

We understand the need to be careful with your information due to privacy concerns. Switching to a private fundraiser means your fundraiser will be removed from GoFundMe’s public search directory and will only be visible to those with whom you’ve shared your fundraiser link. However, please keep in mind that if your fundraiser’s direct link is posted on social media accounts, such as Facebook, anyone who clicks your fundraiser link will be able to access the fundraiser.

Please also note that if you set your fundraiser to private, you won’t be eligible to feature on any GoFundMe Communities linked to your cause either.

Follow these steps to make your fundraiser private:

  • Log into your GoFundMe account and choose “Your fundraisers” from the menu in the top right corner
  • Choose the fundraiser you’d like to make private
    • US fundraisers - Select “Fundraiser” > “Change fundraiser settings”
    • All other countries - Select “Edit” below your fundraiser title > toggle to “Settings”
  • Turn off the toggle next to “Allow my fundraiser to be shown in search, discovery, and communities”, and click “Save”

If you’re looking to create an anonymous fundraiser or hide your name, then we highly recommend you reach out to a close friend or family member who can create the fundraiser on your behalf. They can also withdraw the money and send the funds to you outside of GoFundMe, but they’ll want to make clear in the fundraiser who the fundraiser is for.

It’s important to keep in mind that we promote transparency between GoFundMe organizers and their supporters.

Supporters feel more comfortable donating to a fundraiser when they know exactly who is running it and in charge of the funds. This also provides them with a main point of contact to come to with any questions. So, if you’re comfortable, it’s best to keep the fundraiser public.

See your impact with unique share links

GoFundMe’s unique share links allow GoFundMe supporters to use their platform for good and celebrate the impact their community has in the world. When you’re logged in and share your unique share link, you’ll be able to see any donations made from it in your impact dashboard.

What is a unique share link?

Your unique share link is your personal link that measures the amount of donations raised by your community in supporting fundraisers on GoFundMe. When someone else uses your unique link to donate, that donation will display in your impact dashboard. Donors and supporters will receive an email letting them know someone donated with their link. GoFundMe organizers and beneficiaries won’t receive an email, but they’ll be able to see donations attributed to their unique share links on their impact dashboard.

There are two ways to generate a unique share link:
  • Immediately after donating to a fundraiser, you’ll be prompted to share the fundraiser with your community. The link provided will be a unique share link.
  • Log into your GoFundMe account and visit the fundraiser page. So long as you’re signed in, using any of the sharing options will generate your unique link.

Not only will you be able to see the amount you donated, but you’ll also be able to see how many other donations you brought to the fundraiser!

How do I get to my impact dashboard?

Your impact dashboard will be within your GoFundMe account. If you don’t already have a GoFundMe account, just create an account with the email address you used to donate to access your impact dashboard. Understanding Account Deactivation versus Data Deletion

Understanding the difference between deactivating your GoFundMe account and permanently deleting your data is key to managing your presence on the platform.

Before moving forward with either action, please review the information below and consider any unclaimed funds or future access needs.

Account Deactivation

If you deactivate your account:

  • You won’t be able to log in
  • Your fundraiser(s) will no longer be visible on GoFundMe
  • No one will be able to see your Profile
  • Your non-anonymous donations will still list your name as the donor
  • Your information will still be stored in GoFundMe’s systems
  • You will need to request to reactivate your account
If you’d like to deactivate your GoFundMe account, please follow these steps:
  • Log in to your account
  • Navigate to the menu in the top-right corner of your account
  • Select “Account settings”
  • Scroll to the bottom and click “Deactivate account”
  • Confirm by selecting “Deactivate my account”

If you decide to come back to GoFundMe after you’ve deactivated your account, you can reactivate your account at any time by visiting our Help Center and selecting “Contact us.” A member of our Support Team will be able to assist you.

Account Deletion

If you would like to close your account and permanently delete your data:

  • You won’t be able to reactivate your account
  • Your account, profile, fundraisers, and select data will be permanently removed from the platform
  • Your non-anonymous donations will no longer list your name as the donor
  • Your personal data will be deleted from GoFundMe’s internal and third-party systems
  • A minimum of accounting and compliance assurance data may be retained as necessary for tax and legal operational purposes

If you’d like to permanently delete your data, you can submit a data request here to have your account and applicable associated data removed from GoFundMe and our third-party systems.

Important: This action is irreversible, and any unclaimed funds may be lost.

Make sure transfers for any remaining funds have initiated before submitting a deletion request. Review this article to learn how to set up transfers.

You can learn more at our Privacy Choices page.
