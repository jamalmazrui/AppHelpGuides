What I found in your files that was not on my list

Reading all 251 guides turned up five widget families I had not seen, and each is now named in the script: HelpDocs (TuneIn, Downcast), Amazon, Verizon, Guardian, and Freshdesk’s apology line.

Two things I got wrong, caught before they reached you

The first attempt deleted five real article headings. “Send feedback” is both a widget’s word and the title of genuine articles — YouTube’s Send feedback to YouTube, Windows’ Send feedback to Microsoft with the Feedback Hub app, Zoom’s Send feedback specific to AI responses. My rule matched the opening of a line, so it took them. The rule now matches only a whole line, and never a heading or a contents entry, and those three guides are untouched in the end.

The second was Wikipedia. Its guide carries archived reader feedback where the widget’s question is followed by the reader’s own words on the same line: “Was this page helpful ? Please change the spelling of Billy’s Lakota name…”. A rule that matched the opening would have deleted that reader. Six such lines survive, deliberately — the sweep removes the question only when nothing but yes or no follows it.

The checks

For every changed guide I compared before and after:

  • Headings lost: only the widgets. Downcast lost 55 How did we do? headings and Muse 5 Was this article helpful? headings. Every other guide kept every heading it had.
  • Contents lists unchanged in all twelve — the same number of entries before and after, so no article vanished.
  • No new heading jumps. Wikipedia has 41, and it had 41 before; nothing else has any.
  • Every .htm rebuilt with Pandoc, contents page included, and checked for size and structure.
  • No widget phrase remains in eleven of the twelve, and the six in Wikipedia are the reader comments described above.
