ChatGPT dots help

This guide holds the documentation OpenAI publishes for dots, the always-on agents in ChatGPT, gathered on 31 October 2026 from the Markdown copies OpenAI publishes of its ChatGPT documentation: 6 articles, the whole of its Dots section. Left out: administrator pages for setting up dots in a workspace (2 pages) and weekly release notes, which announce changes rather than explain them (2 pages). A dot keeps working between conversations and acts through the apps and computer you connect, so read Control your dot before connecting accounts; dots are rolling out gradually and change week by week, so treat this as a snapshot. The ChatGPT guide in this collection covers ChatGPT itself, and OpenAI’s Help Center keeps three more dots articles (getting started, privacy and safety questions, and workspace management) that the next gathering of that guide will hold; the Claude Duet, Grok Bot and Muse guides cover similar agents.

Dots

Connect computers and apps to your dot

For the complete documentation index, see llms.txt. Markdown versions of documentation pages are available by appending .md to the page URL.

Your dot lives in the cloud and has its own computer and browser. It stays available when your computer is off. Connecting your own computer and apps gives it access to more of your work. These connections are separate from the channels you use to message your dot. You can send a request from your phone while your dot works in the cloud or coordinates work on a connected computer.

Use the cloud computer

Open your dot’s profile and look under Computers to inspect its cloud computer. You can also select Open computer when your dot offers a browser handoff.

Opening the computer doesn’t give you control. Select Take over to use its mouse and keyboard, then Return control when your dot can continue.

Use the cloud computer for research, documents, data analysis, or software work. Cloud work can continue while your devices are off, and the computer can keep its state between periods of use. It has its own files, software, and browser sessions. Signing in to a website on your computer doesn’t sign your dot in to that website.

Cloud threads

Your dot can start separate threads for cloud work. These appear in your desktop, web, and mobile apps, where you can review results and give further instructions while your dot continues coordinating. For coding work, it can use a Codex cloud environment you’ve set up with the repository and configuration it needs.

Your dot can work on several things in parallel, and you can keep talking to it while those threads run. See Tasks and memory.

Sign in to a website

Your dot uses the same private sign-in flow as ChatGPT Work. When a website needs a login, your dot can send a request with a private form. Enter your credentials there and complete any verification the website requires, such as a verification code. The form sends this information to the remote browser outside your conversation.

You can also choose to sign in on the website instead. This opens your dot’s browser in takeover mode so you can complete the steps yourself. Return control when you’re ready for your dot to continue.

Staying signed in

A website session can remain available for later work until you sign out or the website expires it. Your dot’s cloud browser has its own sessions; it doesn’t inherit logins from your personal browser. Some websites block cloud browsers or require further verification.

Saving and reusing a login

When offered, Save to Passwords lets you save a login for future sign-ins. Saving is optional. If a matching saved login is available for a new sign-in request, you can confirm that you want to use it or enter another account.

A saved login and an active website session are different. Your dot can continue using a valid session without signing in again. Reusing a saved login to sign in requires your confirmation.

Connect your computer

Connect your computer once to make its local files, code, and apps available for your dot’s work:

  1. Open your dot’s profile in the ChatGPT app on that computer.
  2. Under Computers, find Your computer and select Allow access.
  3. Review the confirmation and select Allow access to connect it.

You can connect only one personal computer at a time. Your dot’s cloud computer stays connected. Access to your computer applies wherever you message the same dot, including from your phone.

Your dot can create local Work or Codex tasks and continue existing local Codex tasks. A task can be managed in the cloud while using files and tools on your connected computer. Open the task to inspect where the work is happening or give instructions directly. See Tasks and memory for task types and connection requirements.

If a website blocks the cloud browser, ask your dot to try your connected computer. This may create a separate local task. It doesn’t transfer the cloud browser’s session or guarantee that a particular local browser is supported.

The connection persists between tasks. Keep your computer online and the ChatGPT app open while your dot uses it. An Offline status means the computer is unavailable; it doesn’t revoke the connection. Select Revoke access to remove access. Your dot’s local-computer permission is separate from connecting a computer to Codex or enabling Work Sync. Neither connection alone gives your dot access.

Connect apps

Your dot can use supported plugins installed and enabled for your account. Access depends on the plugin’s connected account, permissions, and the environment where the work runs. Local skills require a connected computer.

For example, connect Gmail to find relevant email, Google Drive to read and update documents, or GitHub to investigate an issue and prepare a pull request. Install and connect each plugin you want your dot to use.

Review the connected account and its allowed actions. Your existing ChatGPT app permissions apply to your dot. For example, you can allow it to read email without allowing it to send email. If a connection expires, open the request to reconnect it before continuing.

Adding a contact method doesn’t grant access to your apps or computer. For example, messaging your dot in Slack doesn’t connect your inbox.

Control your dot

For the complete documentation index, see llms.txt. Markdown versions of documentation pages are available by appending .md to the page URL.

Built-in safeguards, existing app permissions, and automatic approval checks apply from the start. You can review ongoing work, add instructions, or set optional custom rules.

Review work

In the desktop app, open your dot’s profile and select Activity. Select a task to review its progress, files, and results, including work happening in the background. If it’s waiting for a decision, app connection, sign-in, or approval, open the request and respond to continue. You can change direction as work continues.

Open Scheduled to review your dot’s scheduled tasks. Check the task’s instructions, timing, and destination. See tasks and memory for recurring work.

How action review works

Before your dot takes an action that could affect your accounts or share information, an automatic review checks it against your instructions, permissions, custom rules, and built-in safety requirements. The review determines whether the action can proceed, needs your approval, or includes a step you must do yourself. For example, you must change a password yourself.

Give ongoing instructions a clear scope: who can take part, what should happen, and when. Asking your dot to draft replies doesn’t give it permission to send them. A specific instruction can cover future actions within that scope; an action outside it needs another decision.

Your dot can also research information from apps you’ve connected to look for ways to help. The tools it uses for that research can’t send messages, change app content, or control your browser or computer. Any follow-up action is subject to the same permissions and safety requirements.

Set custom rules

Custom rules are optional controls for specific ongoing boundaries. Start with clear instructions in your conversation; you don’t need to create a rule for every approval.

After setting up your dot, open custom rules for your app:

  • Desktop and web: Open Settings > Personalization and select Custom rules under Permissions.
  • iOS and Android: Open the sidebar, then select Customize > Custom rules.

Select Add, describe the action, choose how your dot should handle it, and select Add rule. The choices are:

Rule Intended behavior
Take action without asking Take the specified action without asking for approval.
Take action when you say so Proceed when you explicitly request the action; otherwise ask immediately before acting.
Ask before taking action Ask for approval before taking the specified action.
Hand off to you Ask you to take the action instead.

For example, you could choose Ask before taking action for sending messages to customers, or Hand off to you for deleting shared project files. Use your conversation for preferences such as writing style or how you like updates; custom rules control when your dot can take an action.

Saved custom rules apply to your dot in the same account. They can give your dot permission for particular actions, require it to check with you, or prevent it from taking an action. They are instructions your dot tries to follow, and it can make mistakes. They don’t grant access to an app or computer, override built-in safety requirements, or remove required confirmations such as approval to use a saved login.

Use a rule’s menu to edit or delete it. Select Open Plugins to review Plugin permissions, which control app actions separately from custom rules. If your workspace disables custom rules, you can’t edit saved rules, and they don’t apply. See Configure dots permissions.

For workspace admins: See Set up dots for work for workspace permissions, setup, and security and governance guidance.

Manage data settings

Your ChatGPT data controls also apply to eligible conversations with your dot and the work it carries out. OpenAI doesn’t train models directly on proactive research or its private notes. If information from that research becomes part of an eligible conversation or task, your data settings apply. See data controls in ChatGPT.

Stop work

Pausing your dot, stopping a delegated task, and canceling a schedule have different effects:

  • Pause stops your dot’s current main task. It doesn’t stop every delegated task or cancel future scheduled runs.
  • Open a delegated task in Activity to inspect and stop that task.
  • Open Scheduled to disable or delete the recurring task you want to end.

Use Resume to continue a paused dot. Stopping work doesn’t undo completed actions. Review active tasks and schedules separately.

Delete your dot

Use Delete to delete your dot. Read the confirmation for how deletion handles conversations, memories, and scheduled tasks. Save any results you need before confirming; the action cannot be undone.

Deleting your dot doesn’t undo changes already made in connected apps or recall messages already delivered to other people.

Connect tools and contact methods

Connect what your dot needs:

These connections are separate. Connecting a messaging channel doesn’t automatically grant access to an inbox, other apps, or your computer. Your dot lives in the cloud and has its own computer and browser for work that doesn’t need your device.

It starts with relevant ChatGPT memory and keeps notes as you work together. See Tasks and memory.

How dots work

Your dot works between conversations. It tracks progress, works out what needs to happen next, and follows through as things change. Once you’ve asked it to handle something, you can return with new information or priorities without restarting the work. Your dot brings back results and turns to you for decisions that need your judgment.

Illustration: A dot explains how it follows up between conversations and asks for decisions.

Your dot can decide when to pause and wake up to continue work; you don’t need a fixed schedule for every follow-up. It can use background agents to work on several things in parallel while you keep talking to it. It also researches connected information to suggest useful next steps.

For example, if your dot is helping organize an offsite, it can update the headcount as people confirm and ask before sending a request for revised pricing.

Memory and context

Your dot draws on the conversation, relevant ChatGPT memory, and its own saved notes about your preferences, decisions, and ongoing work. These notes carry forward across conversations and contact methods, so it can build on what you’ve discussed as your work changes.

It can also research connected information and keep private notes to identify useful next steps. That research is read-only; follow-up actions require the applicable permissions. See Tasks and memory.

Its computer and yours

Your dot has its own computer and browser in the cloud for research, files, and running software. Cloud work can continue while your devices are off. When you need to inspect the browser or do a step yourself, open its computer and select Take over. Select Return control when you’re ready for your dot to continue. See computers and apps for connection details.

Connect your computer once through your dot’s profile to make its local files, code, and apps available for your dot’s work. You can connect only one personal computer at a time. The connection stays in place between tasks. Your computer must be online with the ChatGPT app open for your dot to use it. This permission is separate from connecting your computer to Codex or enabling Work Sync.

Your dot can use supported plugins installed and enabled for your account, with their connected accounts and existing permissions. For example, use Gmail to find relevant email, Google Drive to work with documents, and GitHub to investigate code and prepare changes. Each plugin must be connected and permitted to perform the actions you request. Local skills require a connected computer. Connecting a messaging channel doesn’t automatically grant access to your apps or computer.

Each connection serves a different purpose:

Connection What it enables
Messaging channel Talk to your dot and receive updates there.
Connected app or plugin Use that service’s information and tools within its permissions.
Local computer Work with that computer’s files, apps, and tasks while it’s available.

Connecting a messaging channel doesn’t also connect your inbox, other apps, or local computer. Your dot’s cloud computer is separate from your devices and their signed-in browser sessions.

Signing in to websites

Your dot uses the same private browser sign-in flow as ChatGPT Work. When a website needs a login, it can send you a sign-in request. Open the request to enter your credentials and any requested verification in a private form. Your credentials go to the browser outside the conversation. You can also select Take over to sign in to complete the steps on the website yourself, then select Done when you’ve finished.

The cloud browser has its own sessions, separate from the browser on your computer. Once you sign in, your dot can use that session for later work until you sign out or the website requires another login. When available, Save to Passwords lets you optionally save the login. Using a saved login for a new sign-in requires your confirmation. See website sign-in.

Some websites block cloud browsers or require extra verification. You can ask your dot to try a connected computer instead. This may create a separate task on that computer; it doesn’t transfer the cloud browser’s session.

Tasks and conversations

Your dot can use background agents, start new cloud threads, and create Work or Codex tasks on a connected computer. It can also continue existing local Codex tasks. For cloud coding work that needs your repository and setup, it can use a Codex cloud environment you’ve created.

Cloud threads appear as separate conversations in the desktop, web, and mobile apps, where you can review results and give further instructions. Local Codex tasks appear on the connected computer. You can keep talking to your dot while these tasks run.

In the desktop app, open your dot’s profile and select Activity, then open a task to inspect its progress, results, or requests for input. Your dot can check results and send follow-up instructions to tasks it created. See Tasks and memory for where tasks run and how to continue them.

Connecting your computer lets your dot work with its tasks and files. It doesn’t give your dot access to every conversation in every app.

Permissions and approvals

Built-in safeguards, your existing ChatGPT app permissions, and automatic approval checks apply from the start. Before an action affects your accounts or shares information, review determines whether your dot can proceed, needs your approval, or must hand a step over to you.

You can add instructions for how you want your dot to work, such as asking it to show you drafts before sending them. Custom rules are optional controls for more specific ongoing boundaries. Neither instructions nor custom rules override built-in safety requirements. See Control your dot.

How dots compare with ChatGPT Work and Codex

Here’s how dots compare with ChatGPT Work and Codex, and how they can work together.

Experience When to use it
Chat When you want an answer, an explanation, ideas, or help drafting something in conversation.
ChatGPT Work When you have a task to delegate, such as research, analysis, or creating a file, either now or on a schedule.
Codex When you want to build, debug, test, or review software and work directly with code and developer tools.
Your dot When you want an agent to keep track of an ongoing responsibility, figure out next steps as things change, and follow up between conversations. It can keep you updated in ChatGPT, Slack, or Teams and delegate tasks to ChatGPT Work or Codex.

Work with your dot

Start with work you regularly need to check or update. Describe the responsibility and share the source material it needs, then review its work and explain what needs to change.

Give it a responsibility

For an offsite, share the plan and venue emails, then ask your dot to identify upcoming decisions and deadlines.

Review the first result and correct any missing details before expanding the responsibility.

Choose when and where to hear from it

Tell your dot what to keep current and which changes deserve your attention. It can decide when to follow up. If you want an update at a specific time, include a time zone and a duration or end date, and ask it to confirm the schedule.

Review and redirect

When priorities change, tell your dot what to change in the work already underway.

Ask your dot what it’s working on and what needs your input. In the desktop app, open its profile and use Activity to review delegated tasks. Ask it to list recurring work and confirm what’s scheduled.

Use Pause to stop your dot’s current main task. Stop delegated tasks in Activity and cancel recurring tasks in Scheduled separately. Ending a voice call doesn’t necessarily stop assigned work. See Stop work.

Review important results and their sources. Your dot can make mistakes, and stopping a task doesn’t undo actions it has already completed.

Ways to work with your dot

Give your dot access to the relevant sources and tell it what to keep track of. For example:

  • Keep a sales proposal current. Share the proposal and customer requirements. As the requested seat count or terms change, have your dot update the figures, track outstanding security and technical reviews, and bring you the next decisions.
  • Prepare content from each interview. Share the interview material and photos. Ask your dot to draft a caption, show notes, and clip ideas for review. You can refine the title or tone from your phone before publishing.
  • Keep launch materials aligned with the product. Connect the launch channel and share the brief, deck, and approved visuals. Have your dot flag product changes that affect the campaign and prepare revised copy, slides, and speaker notes for your review.
  • Update a study as results arrive. Share the study and new measurements. Have your dot update the comparison chart, explain where repeat results differ from the first batch, and draft a findings summary and proposed next tests for review.
  • Follow up on software feedback. Connect the feedback source and code project. Ask your dot to investigate reported problems, such as a missing item in a sidebar, and prepare a fix for code review before merging.

iOS widgets and shortcuts

Open your dot from your iPhone Home Screen, or use Apple Shortcuts to choose how to start a conversation. Use the latest ChatGPT iOS app and sign in to the account where you set up your dot. These features are rolling out with ChatGPT for iOS 1.2026.279 and require dot access.

Add the Your dot widget

Illustration: The small Your dot widget with Alfred’s yellow character above his name and shortcut buttons hidden.

The Your dot widget shows your dot’s name and appearance in a small Home Screen widget.

  1. Touch and hold an empty area of your Home Screen, then select Edit > Add Widget.
  2. Find ChatGPT, choose Your dot, and select Add Widget.
  3. Place the widget, then select Done.

Tap your dot to open its conversation with the message composer ready. For example, open it after a meeting to add a decision to work you’re already doing together.

Create an Apple shortcut

ChatGPT provides one Open your dot action with three Mode choices:

Mode What it does
Messaging Opens your dot’s conversation for typing a message. This is the default.
Dictation Starts speech-to-text input for a message to your dot.
Call Starts a live voice conversation or returns to an active call.
  1. Open Apple’s Shortcuts app and create a new shortcut.
  2. Add an action, search for Open your dot, and select the ChatGPT action.
  3. Select your dot and choose a Mode.
  4. Name and save the shortcut, then run it to open ChatGPT in that mode.

For example, save a shortcut named “Talk to my dot” with Call mode for a spoken progress check. Use Dictation for a quick spoken note instead. Dictation isn’t available during an active call.

On an iPhone with an Action button, open Settings > Action Button, choose Shortcut, then Choose a Shortcut and select your saved shortcut. You can also run a saved shortcut from Apple’s Shortcuts Home Screen widget.

The action opens ChatGPT and requires authentication. If you switch accounts and a saved shortcut says the dot is no longer available, sign in to the account that owns it.

Set up texting
  1. Open your dot’s profile in the ChatGPT mobile app, desktop app, or a desktop web browser and select Texting.
  2. Enter your phone number and follow the steps to verify it.
  3. Save your dot’s contact card when you receive it. Send your dot a message to start chatting.

For iMessage, make sure you’re sending from the phone number you verified. On Android, enable RCS in your messaging app.

You can send text, photos, and voice messages. If your dot needs approval, it sends you a link to review the request in ChatGPT.

Your dot uses context from your conversations to choose where to reach you. You can also ask it to use a particular channel, such as texting you when something needs your attention and keeping routine updates in ChatGPT.

Your dot can only text you, and you’re the only one who can text your dot. Setting up texting doesn’t give your dot or OpenAI access to your other text conversations.

Group chats, phone calls, and FaceTime aren’t currently supported through texting. You can call your dot in ChatGPT.

Assigned work

Your dot keeps working between conversations and follows through as things change. It can decide when to pause and wake up to continue, so you don’t need to put every follow-up on a fixed schedule. You can also specify a time or a supported event that should trigger work. Tell it which results deserve an update and which decisions need your input.

Your dot can divide work among background agents that run in parallel and report back to it. You can keep talking to your dot while they work. It can also create separate, visible threads and send follow-up instructions.

New cloud threads appear in your desktop, web, and mobile apps, where you can open them and continue steering the work. Local Codex threads are available on the connected computer. For coding work that needs a particular repository and setup, your dot can use a Codex cloud environment you’ve created.

Task How your dot can work with it
New local Work or Codex task Create a task on a computer connected to your dot. Keep that computer online with the ChatGPT app open.
Existing local Codex task Continue the task on a connected computer. Identify the task and what you want to change.
New cloud coding task Create a task in a Codex cloud environment you have already set up. Your computer can be offline.
Task your dot created Check results and send follow-up instructions using the task’s original computer or cloud environment.

For cloud coding work, create the environment in Codex first, including its repository and setup configuration. Changing your dot’s selected computer doesn’t move an existing task to that computer.

In the desktop app, open your dot’s profile, select Activity, and open a task to inspect its progress, files, results, or requests for input. You can also give instructions directly. Your dot can continue coordinating the task when you step away.

A new task receives instructions and context from your dot for that work. It has its own conversation; it doesn’t automatically receive every conversation you’ve had with your dot. Connecting a computer doesn’t grant access to every existing ChatGPT or Codex conversation.

Review the output and any reported errors even after a run completes. A completed run doesn’t by itself confirm that the requested result was achieved or delivered. See Review work.

Recurring tasks

Work that repeats at fixed times needs a saved schedule. Specify:

  • What your dot should check or update.
  • When it should run, including the time zone and any end date.
  • Which changes deserve a notification.
  • Where to deliver results.

For example:

Check the connected planning channel each weekday at 9 AM Pacific for the next four weeks. Update the project checklist when a deadline changes. Message me in ChatGPT only if a deadline is at risk or you need a decision. Confirm the schedule.

Ask your dot to confirm what it saved. Use Scheduled to review recurring work, or ask your dot to list, change, or cancel its scheduled tasks.

Your dot can also read and edit your existing ChatGPT Work automations. Ask it to review what’s scheduled or change a recurring task’s instructions or timing. Identify the automation you want to change and ask it to confirm the update.

Stopping active work and canceling a schedule are separate actions. See Controls.

Event monitoring

When a connected service supports event monitoring, you can instead ask your dot to respond to a specific event. For example, ask it to investigate new bug reports in a connected Slack channel and prepare fixes for your review. Tell it which events to watch for and when to notify you. Ask it to confirm which events it can follow. Connecting Slack or another source alone doesn’t create a monitoring task.

Context and memory

Your dot uses the conversation it is working in, relevant ChatGPT memory, and its own saved notes to keep track of your preferences and ongoing responsibilities. These serve different purposes.

Conversation context

Context is the information available to your dot while it responds or works: messages, instructions, relevant source material, and results from its tools or delegated work. It can use this information to connect a new request to work already underway.

Your dot can search your past ChatGPT conversations for relevant context. For example, ask it to find an earlier discussion and use the decisions from it in your current task. Retrieved conversation content is context for the work; it isn’t the same as persistent memory or a complete history included in every task.

The context available for an interaction is a selection of this information. Calls use selected conversation context, which can differ from the information available to a task working in the background.

Persistent memory

Your dot starts with relevant context from ChatGPT memory. It also keeps its own notes about preferences, decisions, and ongoing work so it can use them in later conversations. These notes are separate from ChatGPT’s saved memory and are not a complete transcript of everything you’ve said.

As you work together, your dot can update these notes to reflect new decisions and changing priorities. Changing a ChatGPT saved-memory setting doesn’t necessarily change the notes your dot has already made.

Across messaging channels

ChatGPT, Slack, and Teams are ways to reach the same dot. Switching between them doesn’t create a separate dot or reset its saved notes. Information you share through one contact method can inform its work and replies through another.

The visible conversations remain distinct. A message in ChatGPT isn’t automatically copied into your Slack conversation. Your dot’s ability to use information also doesn’t grant permission to disclose it to another audience. For example, it can use a deadline you shared privately to help you prepare an update, but sharing private details in a team channel still requires permission.

This continuity applies to conversations with your dot. Connecting a computer doesn’t make every conversation in every app available to it. See Message your dot for contact methods and availability.

Proactive research

Alongside assigned work, your dot can research information it has permission to read and keep private notes about useful findings. This background research can connect new information to earlier work. For example, your dot might flag that a release decision conflicts with a draft you shared last week.

Proactive research itself doesn’t send messages, change connected apps, or control a browser or computer. The background agents report their findings to your dot, which can bring you a suggestion or question. Any follow-up action is subject to the permissions and approvals that apply to that work.

Assigned recurring tasks can include actions you’ve authorized. Their permissions and schedule are separate from proactive research.
