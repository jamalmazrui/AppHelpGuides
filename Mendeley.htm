How can I merge multiple accounts using Mendeley Desktop?

Last updated on December 02, 2024

It’s not currently possible to merge multiple accounts, however you can export your library from one account to your local machine and then import it to the other account via Mendeley desktop. Follow these steps to export and import your library.

How

  • Login to one of your accounts in Mendeley Desktop.
  • Press CTRL + A to highlight all references.
  • In the ‘File’ tab, click ‘Export’.
  • Save the exported file to your local device.
  • Login to your second account in Mendeley Desktop.
  • In the ‘File’ tab, click ‘Import’. Only references can be exported and imported, PDFs and folder organization will not be included.

How do I create a Mendeley account and sign in/out?

Last updated on May 01, 2025

If you don’t already have a Mendeley account you can create one for free: visit Mendeley.com and click ‘Create account’. When you have created this account you can also use it to sign in to Mendeley Desktop and other Elsevier services including Science Direct or Scopus.

If you have downloaded Mendeley Desktop without first creating an account you’ll need to create an account. To do this click ‘Register’ on the Mendeley Desktop sign in screen.

If you already have an Elsevier account, e.g. for Science Direct or Scopus, you can use this to sign in to Mendeley Desktop and Mendeley.com.

You will only be able to sign in to Mendeley Desktop with an institutional (Shibboleth) credential using a connected Mendeley/Elsevier account. Please refer to this FAQ for more help.

If you’re using Mendeley.com from a shared machine we recommend you sign out at the end of your session. To do this click the drop-down arrow next to your name and click ‘Logout’.

How do I delete my Mendeley account?

Last updated on May 27, 2025

How

Follow these steps to delete your account:

  • Sign in to Mendeley.com. Click the down arrow next to your name in the top right-hand corner.
  • Click ‘Settings & Privacy’. This will open your Account page.
  • Scroll down to the ‘Close Mendeley account’ section. Click on the ‘Close Mendeley account’ link and follow the on-screen instructions and sign out. Please note: If you have access to other Elsevier products (such as ScienceDirect), please remember not to log in to Mendeley using your email address as this will activate the association of your Mendeley account, as Mendeley is part of Elsevier’s “one login for all” policy.

Footnote Citation Style Issues in Mendeley Cite

Last updated on July 02, 2026

Some users are currently experiencing issues when using certain footnote-based citation styles in Mendeley Cite. These issues may affect inserting citations, switching citation styles, or syncing references within a document. Our team is actively investigating the problem and working on a permanent fix.

This FAQ outlines the known symptoms, affected citation styles, temporary guidance, and the latest information available to help you continue working while we resolve the issue.

  1. What issue is currently affecting Mendeley Cite?

Some users are experiencing problems with citation styles, particularly footnote-based styles. These issues may prevent users from:

  • Inserting citations
  • Switching to certain citation styles
  • Syncing citations, often indicated by a red sync icon in Mendeley Cite Our team is aware of the issue and is actively working on a resolution.
  1. Which citation styles are affected?

Many footnote citation styles are potentially affected. Identified examples include:

  • Chicago Manual of Style
  • Turabian 8th edition (full note, no ibid) Most of the citation styles affected are footnote-based styles impacted as well.
  1. What symptoms might I see if my document is affected?

You may experience one or more of the following:

  • Inability to insert new citations
  • Errors or crashes when selecting a citation style
  • A red sync icon appearing in Mendeley Cite
  • The add-in becoming unresponsive
  1. Can document mode affect this issue?

Yes. If your document is opened in View mode instead of Edit mode, the document may crash.

This occurs because the Mendeley Cite add-in requires edit permissions to write citations to the document.

Tip: Always ensure your document is in Edit mode before using Mendeley Cite.

  1. Can a corrupted citation style cause problems?

Yes. If a citation style is corrupted or has previously caused errors when selected, it may contribute to the issue you are experiencing.

  1. Is there a workaround while waiting for a fix?

At this time, there is no workaround for using the potentially broken footnote citation styles.

However, some users have successfully continued working by:

  • Switching to a functioning citation style such as APA or any other working styles
  • Reloading the Mendeley Cite add-in
    • On Windows: use the Refresh option This may resolve the issue for affected documents, though it does not restore functionality for broken styles.
  1. Can I continue working on my document?

Yes. While the issue persists, we recommend using a working citation style (e.g., APA) as a temporary alternative so you can continue drafting and citing sources.

You can switch back to your preferred footnote style once the official fix is released.

  1. Is a permanent fix coming?

Yes. Our team is actively investigating and working on a permanent solution. We appreciate your patience and recommend checking official channels for updates. We will also update this FAQ or you may keep an eye for our updates on: mendeley.com

How can I prevent Author initials being added to my citations in Word?

Last updated on May 01, 2025

Mendeley adds the author’s initials when:

  • There is more than one author with the same surname.
  • There are multiple cases of the same author being treated as different authors by the program. The program will disambiguate the authors by adding initials or the first name.
  • For example, Smith, J, Smith, J., and Smith, John would be treated as different authors by Mendeley.

How

You can prevent Mendeley adding author initials to your citation by ensuring all authors are added in the same format.

If this doesn’t prevent the initials being added, please send us:

  • Your Mendeley database.
  • The document with your citations.
  • A list with the citations with initials.

How do I change the citation style or citation language used by Mendeley Cite?

Last updated on March 29, 2019

In Mendeley Cite, click on the ‘Citation Settings’ tab. The current citation style will be displayed, along with the current citation language. You will also see a preview of what your citation style will look like. You can select a different citation style by clicking ‘Change citation style’ where you can choose a style from the list and click ‘Update Citation Style’ to update the document. To search for more citation styles, click ‘Search for another style’ where you will be able to search for your preferred citation style. You can also select a custom style from the ‘Add a custom style’ option.

To change the language of your citation style, you can click ‘Change citation language’ where you will see a list of all the available languages. To update, you can choose a language from the list and click ‘Update citation language’ to update the document.

How do I use the Mendeley Desktop citation plugin?

Last updated on May 01, 2025

Once the plugin has been installed, you can open up your word processor. The tool will appear in a different location depending on the software you’re using.The citation plugin is automatically installed when you download Mendeley Desktop if you have a supported version of work of LibreOffice installed.

In Libre Office it will appear as a toolbar across the top of the screen. If you can’t see the toolbar, or if you remove it by accident, you can restore it via the ‘View’ menu and selecting the ‘Mendeley’ option from the ‘Toolbars’ list.

In Microsoft Word for Mac, the tool will appear as as a ribbon under Add-ins or under References, it depends on the version of MSWord.

In Microsoft Word for Windows, the tool can also be accessed via the References tab on the Ribbon. You should see a new set of tools for the Mendeley Cite-o-Matic.

The toolbar is comprised of a number of buttons and menus (please note: the specific order varies between word processors):

  • Insert Citation (or Insert/Edit Citation) - Inserts a citation on the location of the cursor.
    • Use the Citation tool to search your Mendeley library for a specific reference.
    • When the cursor is positioned within an existing Mendeley Citation, you can edit that citation.
    • The current Citation Style controls the format of the citation. See ‘Citation Styles’ below for more details.
  • Undo Edit - Reverts any changes made to Mendeley citations.
  • Merge Citations - Combines two adjacent highlighted citations into a single, properly formatted citation.
  • Insert Bibliography- Creates a bibliography of all the Mendeley citations inserted into the current document.
    • The bibliography will be inserted at the cursor’s current location.
    • The current Citation Style controls the format of the bibliography. See ‘Citation Styles’ below for more details.
  • Refresh - Updates all the Mendeley citations and bibliography to use the most current details for each reference.
    • If you correct an Author Name in Mendeley Desktop, for example, you will need to press Refresh for that change to be updated in any citations added to the current document.
    • Note that inserting another citation, or bibliography, as well as updating the current Citation Style will also cause the document to refresh.
  • Citation Styles - Lists the most popular styles Citation Styles available.
    • Click ‘More Styles’ to see the full styles menu in Mendeley Desktop. This allows you to download additional citation styles from a repository of over 7,000 community-generated styles.
  • Export -Saves a separate copy of your document.
    • This can be used to carry across Mendeley citations into a format compatible with another word processor (e.g. from Microsoft Word to LibreOffice, or vice versa).
    • It can also be used to save a copy without Mendeley Fields. This process is sometimes known as ‘flattening’.

Mendeley Cite Issue with Track Changes

Last updated on July 02, 2026

Mendeley Cite currently does not support the Track Changes feature in Microsoft Word due to a known compatibility limitation. When Track Changes is used while inserting or editing citations, it may result in duplicated or corrupted citations. Users may experience repeated or duplicated in-text citations, citations not being properly deleted, and incorrect or inconsistent formatting. These behaviors are widely reported when Track Changes is active.

This limitation stems from the way Microsoft Word processes tracked edits compared to how citation fields are managed, which can lead to unexpected behavior such as duplication or leftover citation fields.

To avoid these issues, the most reliable workaround is to turn off Track Changes when inserting or editing citations, as consistently recommended in support guidance. Please be assured that we are actively coordinating with Microsoft to improve compatibility and address this limitation; however, there is currently no confirmed timeline for a complete fix.

Why are my citation styles not appearing correctly?

Last updated on June 02, 2025

How does a citation style work?

The format that a citation style follows is defined by an instructional XML file that belongs to that citation style. This file is called a CSL (Citation Style Language) file. A CSL file contains a set of instructions that explain to Mendeley’s citation software how the citation style should appear when used within our software. These instructions follow a scripted format that our citation software can understand, helping us to ensure that we correctly display your citations and bibliographies in the specific format of that citation style. Each CSL file contains hundreds specific details that ensure your citation and bibliography appear in the correct format.

Where does Mendeley get citation styles from?

Mendeley obtains the citation styles available within its products from the open source community. We chose to the open source community because of the sheer number of citation styles that exist (numbering in their thousands) and the frequency at which new ones appear, old ones changed or even made obsolete. This open source community are committed to providing a universal citation style solution, this is why we contribute and use the open source solutions for our own products. More information can be found here.

Why do I need to update my Mendeley Desktop Word plugin document to work with Mendeley Cite?

Last updated on August 12, 2025

To use Mendeley Cite with a document containing citations made with the Mendeley Desktop plugin you will have to update your document. Please continue reading for more details on why this is necessary and how to avoid any data loss.

Existing citations created with the Mendeley Desktop plugin are built using an old technology that is not compatible with Mendeley Cite. These citations have to be updated to start working with Mendeley Cite.

Microsoft made a decisions to stop supporting the technology that the Mendeley Desktop citation plugin was built upon (as well as many other popular reference managers) in the modern versions of Microsoft Word, such as Office 365, Word 2019+ and online versions of Word.

We made the decision to build Mendeley Cite using Microsoft’s newer and preferred technology, where add-ins must published through the AppSource store. This new technology gives us the ability to build additional tools that we previously couldn’t, but it also means that users can now use our citation software on Office 365, online versions of Word, Word 2019+ and now also Word for iPad.

As with any update and conversion process, it is imperative that you create a back-up copy of the document you wish to update.

We have built the update process to ensure that we validate all processes are successful before any changes are made, but there are always external issues that can occur during a conversion process which we cannot detect.

Unfortunately, Mendeley Cite is unable to save instances of back-up Word documents. Mendeley Cite is only able to access the content within the body of a document. We are restricted to only access your current working document and are not able to control things such as saving, creating or cloning documents from within the add-in pane. Therefore we require users to back-up their own documents before we update your document.

  • Quit Mendeley Reference Manager.

  • Open your Downloads folder (or wherever you opened the installer from) and drag the Mendeley app into your Applications folder. If you originally opened it straight from the .dmg installer window, open the installer again and drag the app onto the Applications shortcut.

  • Launch Mendeley from the Applications folder.

  • The app should now update on its own, or you can trigger the update from within the app. Follow these steps to move Mendeley to the correct location and complete the update on Windows:

  • Close Mendeley Reference Manager.

  • Reinstall or move Mendeley Reference Manager into your system-recommended installation folder, rather than running it from Downloads or a temporary location.

  • Once Mendeley is opened from its installed location, let it update. Follow these steps to move Mendeley to the correct location and complete the update on Linux:

  • Close Mendeley Reference Manager.

  • Reinstall or move Mendeley Reference Manager into your system-recommended installation folder rather than running it from Downloads or a temporary location.

How do I migrate from the original Mendeley Desktop to Mendeley Reference Manager?

Last updated on March 17, 2025

Mendeley Reference Manager and Mendeley Cite are new reference management applications from Mendeley. They have been developed using valuable feedback from our users to provide a more resilient, streamlined and intuitive experience.

This guide offers instruction and advice for those of you currently using the original Mendeley Desktop and the citation plug-in to assist you with transitioning to the new applications.

Key Points

  • You can download and begin using the new Mendeley Reference Manager for desktop.
  • You can keep using your existing Mendeley Desktop for now. There is no need to uninstall Mendeley Desktop to also use Mendeley Reference Manager on the same computer.

How to transition to Mendeley Reference Manager and Mendeley Cite

Start using Mendeley Reference Manager desktop app with your existing library

  • Open your existing Mendeley Desktop application and sync your library.
  • Watch the bottom of the Mendeley Desktop window to see when the synchronize process is complete.
  • Download and install Mendeley Reference Manager.
  • Open Mendeley Reference Manager. Sign in with your Mendeley ID if prompted. All of your folders (now called collections), as well as groups, references, metadata, files and annotations will appear in the new Mendeley Reference Manager window. You can now use Mendeley Reference Manager with your existing library.

Review the Mendeley Guides for details on how to use various features, which may vary from those in the original Mendeley Desktop. One key difference: changes you make in Mendeley Reference Manager are now synced automatically to your library, which is based in the cloud.

While transitioning to Mendeley Reference Manager, you can continue to use Mendeley Desktop if you need to. Simply open the original Mendeley Desktop application and manually sync as usual. Changes you made using Mendeley Reference Manager will appear when syncing is complete.

Add Mendeley Cite to your workflow

Mendeley Cite is an add-in application for Microsoft® Word that supports citing references in your papers while you write, much like the citation plug-in that came with Mendeley Desktop. Unlike the citation plug-in, Mendeley Cite runs independently of the reference manager. It accesses your reference library in the cloud to load the metadata it needs to insert citations or a bibliography in your manuscript.

You can use Mendeley Cite with either Mendeley Desktop or Mendeley Reference Manager. (The citation plug-in only works with Mendeley Desktop.)

How to install Mendeley Cite

  • Visit Mendeley.com or launch Microsoft Word, go to the Insert menu and select “Get Add-ins”.

  • The Microsoft AppSource page for Mendeley Cite opens. Select the “Get it now” button.

  • The app will install in your version of Microsoft Word.* It will appear in the ‘References’ ribbon near the right side.

  • Launch Mendeley Cite to see and search the references in your library, and insert citations or a bibliography into your document. Sign in to your Mendeley Account if prompted. Read the Mendeley Guides for detailed information on using Mendeley Cite.

  • If you cannot access the Microsoft AppSource or install Add-ins, check this Mendeley FAQ for advice.

Transition considerations

Mendeley Reference Manager and Mendeley Cite feature all essential reference management and citation functions. However, there are some additional functions from Mendeley Desktop and the citation plug-in that are not available in the new applications. We are working hard to make available the functions that were used and valued in the previous applications - you can find out about our newest releases here.

Please find below details of other factors you should be aware of when transitioning to the new applications.

Mendeley Reference Manager (desktop app)
  • Mendeley Reference Manager’s desktop app will not run on 32-bit processor computers, and there is no plan to add this capability.
  • There is no BibTeX sync option to keep external BibTeX files up to date with the content of your library.
  • Older computer operating systems and older versions of MS Word are not supported (see version details in the Mendeley Support Center). However, via a web browser you can use the web version of Mendeley Reference Manager to manage your library and reading, and the free Word Online application with Mendeley Cite.
Converting citations made with the plug-in to Mendeley Cite

Mendeley Cite can convert citations in a document that were created using the legacy citation plug-in. First save a duplicate copy of your document as a backup, as the conversion is not reversible.

You may not want to convert your document in these circumstances:

  • You’re in the middle of writing an important paper with a nearby deadline that uses the citation plug-in.
  • You’re collaborating on a paper(s) with multiple contributors: once you convert a paper’s citations to work with Mendeley Cite, this means everyone working on that paper has to have Mendeley Cite installed – otherwise authors with the old citation plug-in will not be able to add citations.
Institutional users of Microsoft Word

Some institutions’ IT administrators do not permit accessing or downloading add-ins from the Microsoft AppSource. You can read more about this here. In the meantime, the free MS Word Online is a possible workaround. See “Using the free Word Online app with Mendeley Cite” below.

Other text processors besides Word

At this time Mendeley Cite is not available for LibreOffice, Google Docs, or other text processors besides Microsoft Word. An option is to use Microsoft’s free Word Online application. See “Using the free Word Online app with Mendeley Cite” below.

Using the free Word Online app with Mendeley Cite

An option for installing Mendeley Cite is to use Microsoft’s free Word Online app (available with a free personal Microsoft Account), which works on all operating systems, including Linux. You can install Mendeley Cite in this version of Word. Save your documents created in other text processors in .docx format and open them in Word Online. Then you can insert citations from your Mendeley library and add a bibliography.

Advice for Working offline

In Mendeley Reference Manager, your library is in the cloud so it can be accessed anywhere and is not lost if something happens to your computer.

You can download one, some, or all of your library’s files to a local drive for offline reading and annotating in Mendeley Reference Manager. Any annotations, notes, or other changes you make while offline will sync automatically with your cloud library once you reconnect to the internet.

Downloading your entire library is possible but can take time and also uses a lot of local storage. In most cases, it’s more efficient to work with a selection of downloaded files if you need to work offline, rather than all of your files.

Mendeley Cite needs an internet connection to launch and load your library’s metadata for citing. However, after Mendeley Cite has finished loading, you can disconnect and continue working on your manuscript. This works only for that writing session. If you close the document, you will have to reconnect to the internet and re-load Cite.

How to ensure Mendeley Web Importer can read documents from your website

Mendeley Web Importer tries to get document metadata from websites in four ways.

1. DOI lookup (Recommended)

2. HTML Meta Elements (Supported)

Sometimes a document does not have a DOI, so Mendeley Web Importer also supports reading other META elements directly.

For catalog pages, HTML meta elements should be specified in the HTML HEAD section.

There are 5 supported “standards” for describing articles in the META elements:

  • Highwire Press tags (citation_*)
  • bepress tags (bepress_citation_*)
  • Eprints tags (eprints.*)
  • PRISM tags (prism.*)
  • Dublin Core (dc./dcterms.) Presenting more than one set of tags is not a problem. Use Dublin Core tags only as a last resort because they lack unambiguous fields for journal articles.

3. Metadata extraction (Fallback)

It is sometimes not possible or feasible to implement HTML META elements for standalone PDF content.

In this case, Mendeley Web Importer will upload the PDF file to its PDF metadata extraction service to identify metadata heuristically. (The uploaded PDF is discarded after processing and not stored on the server.)

It is not possible for this service to operate with 100% accuracy. Providing DOI or HTML META elements specifying metadata is preferred.

4. COinS – ContextObjects in Spans (Legacy)

COinS was a method of making metadata available within elements in HTML, but is now largely unsupported. We are no longer officially supporting COinS, but we retain our legacy COinS parsers within the application as a last resort for legacy compatibility.

Please note that we only currently support the following OpenURL metadata description formats:

  • fmt:kev:mtx:journal
  • fmt:kev:mtx:book
  • fmt:kev:mtx:dissertation
  • fmt:kev:mtx:dc Both 0.1 and 1.0 OpenURL versions should work, but no assurances is made regarding compatibility.

How Mendeley Web Importer retrieves full text PDFs from websites

Mendeley Web Importer retrieves full text PDFs on behalf of the user in order to save them time and energy in “clicking through”.

Following is a brief overview of how this works. This is the technical approach behind both the “View PDF” button injection and full text PDF retrieval from Mendeley Web Importer upon import to library.

Elsevier (Mendeley) is part of the coalition for responsible sharing. Mendeley does not source full text PDFs from illicit or grey area sources, such as Sci-Hub.

1. Mendeley Web Importer automatically runs a script on allowlisted web pages

Mendeley Web Importer automatically runs a script on a selected allowlist of webpages to detect article identifiers.

It also runs scripts to detect article identifiers any time that the user manually invokes the Mendeley Web Importer window from the browser toolbar.

The detected identifiers are passed to the next step.

2. The extension uses proprietary mechanisms to locate full text PDF links in real time

If one or more articles is detected and the site is allowlisted, Mendeley Web Importer attempts to locate a valid full text PDF for the top-most or main article only.

The extension draws upon Mendeley’s Open Access links catalogue and a custom library of link-follow and page-parsing strategies in order to perform this function.

If a PDF link is successfully located, this is indicated to the user through the user interface. Mendeley Web Importer displays either a red “View PDF” button on the web page, or displays a red “PDF” icon adjacent to the detected reference within the Mendeley Web Importer window.

3. Requests for full text are made at the point of need

If the user chooses to click the “View PDF” button or import a reference along with a full text PDF, then the browser extension at that time makes a request for the PDF on behalf of the user.

This is functionally equivalent to the user clicking the download link on your website by themselves and ensures full counter compliant.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are my data privacy and security protected?

We strictly follow GDPR and all other privacy laws and legislation that might apply to ensure responsible use of data and to safeguard the privacy of our users.

For your own privacy, we do recommend leaving out personal, confidential, or sensitive information when submitting a question. This also applies to the nature of the documents you query by using GenAI features in Mendeley. For information on the types of documents that can be stored and used within Mendeley, as well as guidance on what not to store, please refer to our Terms of Use.

How do I check the accuracy of AI-generated content?

Please note: While Mendeley AI strives to ground its information and knowledge discovery on your personal Mendeley library content, there may be occasional discrepancies. It is possible for Mendeley AI to generate results that could be seen as incorrect, misleading, biased or even offensive. Mendeley AI is not meant to provide medical advice. Users should not solely rely on Mendeley AI outputs without exercising their own judgment before acting on or applying any responses. If you want to utilize Mendeley AI generated content in your work, please consult your institutional or workplace guidelines. Content is in English and while Mendeley AI may support browser translated inputs and responses, translations are not validated. Do not enter personal, confidential, protected health information (PHI) or sensitive information into Mendeley AI.

  • Check the sources: Verify the references and citations provided in the content.
  • Cross-reference information: Compare the AI-generated content with other reliable sources. Look for consistency in data and conclusions.
  • Consult experts: If possible, ask subject matter experts to validate the information.
  • Use fact-checking tools: Use online fact-checking tools and databases to confirm the accuracy of specific claims or data points. What type of documents can i use AI in Mendeley with?

We currently support PDF format, non-text content (tables, images, PDF scans etc.) are not supported. We recommend using research documents for the best results.

While using features of Mendeley AI with the documents in your personal Mendeley library, you are advised to comply with and take into account Elsevier’s Terms & Conditions. For detailed information, please refer to the following sections under Terms & Conditions:

  • Content provided to us or posted on or through the service
  • Disclaimer of warranties and liability How do I acknowledge using AI in Mendeley in my research?

You should not reproduce or republish generated responses verbatim, or misrepresent them as human-authored content. Do not cite responses for academic or research purposes - instead, cite the original referenced source material.

What to do if i see an error message?

An error message can occur for several reasons. Please check the limitations of the feature you are using and rerun your query following the instructions in the message, if applicable. If the problem persists, contact a Customer Support representative for further assistance with one of the buttons at the bottom of this page.

How can I add new references to my Library in Mendeley Reference Manager?

Last updated on September 18, 2025

How

  • Drag and drop files – add references by dragging and dropping a PDF into your library, the application will automatically extract the details from the document and create a library entry.
  • Drag and drop folders - add references by dragging and dropping a folder containing PDFs into your library, the application will automatically extract the details from the documents in the folder and create a library entry/entries.
  • Add file(s) – use the ‘Add References’ button and select ‘Import File(s)’ to select a specific paper from a location on your computer and add it to your library.
  • Add folder(s) – use the ‘Add References’ button and select ‘Import Folder(s)’ to select a specific folder from a location on your computer and add its contents to your library.
  • Manually create an entry – use ‘Add New’ and click on ‘Add Entry Manually’ to manually input the details of a reference. For the beta you are only able to add Journal articles and Books, the ability to create documents of a different type will be available in a future release. You can use a DOI to look up details of a reference automatically; enter the identifier into the DOI field and an automatic lookup will be performed and if the DOI is found then the metadata will be populated. Please note that adding authors manually is now on multiple lines.
  • Import a BibTeX, RIS or EndNote XML file - there are several ways you can do this:
    • Via Drag and Drop – Drag and drop the file from your local state and the application will automatically extract the details from the documents and create a library entry or multiple entries.
    • Add directly from your computer – use ‘Add New’ and select ‘Import Library’ and then select the type of file you wish to import. Select the file on your local drive and then click open and the file will import.
  • Create a watched folder – there are several ways you can do this:
    • Use the ‘Add References’ button and select ‘Set up watched folder’. You will be prompted to choose a folder from a location on your computer. Any PDFs kept in this folder will automatically be imported to your Library.
    • Go to ‘Account & Help’ and select ‘Preferences’. In the Watched Folder tab, select ‘Choose folder to watch’. You will be prompted to choose a folder from a location on your computer. Any PDFs kept in this folder will automatically be imported to your Library.

How can I export my library?

Last updated on April 06, 2022

Mendeley provides the ability to export your library in one of three formats:

  • BibTeX (.bib)
  • RIS – Research Information Systems (.ris)
  • Endnote XML (.xml)

To export your library from Mendeley Desktop:

  • Highlight the references you wish to export.
  • Go to ‘File’ and click ‘export’.
  • Select the format you wish to export as and click ‘save’. This will generate a file you can then save to your machine. The export contains reference Metadata. PDFs are referenced, but not directly exported.

To export your library from Mendeley Reference Manager:

  • Select the references you wish to export.
  • Select the ‘Export’ option in the action panel.
  • Select the format you wish to export as. This will generate a file that is saved to your machine. Additionally in the Mendeley Reference Manager you have the option to export in Microsoft Word (.xml) format. The export contains reference Metadata. PDFs are referenced, but not directly exported.

  • Go to Tools then select ‘’Extension Manager’

How do I add a file(s) to Mendeley Desktop?

Last updated on July 26, 2017

There are several ways you can add references and files to your Mendeley Library:

  • Drag and drop files from your machine
  • Use the ‘Add’ button
  • Create entry manually
  • Import library data exported from other Reference managers

How

Drag and drop files

Add papers to Mendeley by dragging and dropping a PDF into the Mendeley Desktop window. Mendeley will automatically extract the details from the document and create a library entry.

You can also drag and drop a folder containing multiple papers - Mendeley will work its way through the contents, creating library entries for the PDFs it finds.

Use the ‘Add’ button

Use the ‘Add’ button on the top left hand side of your screen. Use this option to browse and select a specific paper from a location on your computer and add it to your Mendeley library.

Manually create an entry

Use File - ‘Add Entry Manually…’ to manually input the details of a reference. Select the appropriate document type and complete the fields to create a library entry using the details you provide. This can be used to create library entries for items that you do not hold in PDF form - such as books, articles and other types of media.

Tip: You can use a DOI, PMID or ArXiv ID to look up the details of a reference. Paste the identifier into the appropriate field, and click on the magnifying glass. This will look up the item in the Mendeley Catalog and return the details provided by other Mendeley users. If the reference is completely new to Mendeley, the details will be retrieved directly from the appropriate identification service.

Import from other software

You can transfer your library from EndNote, Papers, Zotero, Refworks, Jabref and more.

  • Use the Export option within the other software to extract your references to RIS, BibTeX, or EndNote XML file format.
  • In Mendeley Desktop, click File - ‘Import’ and select the exported file to add its contents to your Mendeley library.

How do I clear my cache and all cookies?

Last updated on October 02, 2025

In general, your browser stores information, and over time it may cause problems with logging in or displaying pages. If you experience issues such as slow browser sessions, HTTP errors, or other problems while accessing a website or resetting your password, they may be caused by the cache and cookies your browser saves.

  • What is cache?
  • A browser saves elements of websites in a cache, sometimes referred to as Temporary Files, to improve speed when elements are reused on other pages or you visit the same site again later. For example, if a menu appears on all pages of a site, the browser will save that menu for quicker loading. Browsers typically retain temporary files until a space limit is reached and then delete the oldest. Periodically clearing out these temporary files might make the next time you load a site take a few seconds longer, but will also eliminate issues caused by conflicts with older temporary files.
  • What are cookies?
  • A cookie is a small piece of data from a website, stored in your browser to record your preferences and other information. Every time you pull up that same website, the browser sends the cookie back to the website with the saved information from the last visit. Cookies can have a defined expiration date, and can be cleared out manually using browser settings. Please note: By following the instructions for your browser to clear only the cookies and cache, you will not delete saved webpages or login credentials. You may need to reselect your preferences the next time you visit a website.

How

To clear your cache and cookies, select your browser for step-by-step instructions:

  • Chrome: How to clear cache and cookies in Chrome
  • Firefox: How to clear cache and cookies in Firefox
  • Safari: How to clear cache and cookies in Safari
  • Microsoft Edge: How to clear cache and cookies in Edge

How do I generate a verbose log from Mendeley Desktop?

Last updated on March 17, 2025

Mendeley has a facility to generate to a ‘verbose log’ which includes all the data sent to and received from Mendeley Web during a sync. This does not happen automatically, so you need to set it to do this yourself.

To use this option in Windows and Linux:

  • Open Mendeley Desktop as normal.

  • Press Ctrl + Shift + D on your keyboard.

  • Click “Logging” in the dialog box that appears.

  • Check the box marked “Enable Verbose Logging” and sync your library.

  • Once the sync is complete, click View Log File in the dialog box and save this log as a text file. To use this option on Mac OS X:

  • Open Mendeley Desktop as normal.

  • Press Cmd + Shift + D on your keyboard.

  • Click “Logging” in the dialog box that appears.

  • Check the box marked “Enable Verbose Logging” and sync your library.

  • Once the sync is complete, click View Log File in the dialog box and save this log as a text file. . You can then send the resulting text file to us for further investigation if you encounter any issues.

Mendeley Ask My Library (AML)

Last updated on November 25, 2025

For more information on other AI features in Mendeley, detailed FAQs and a technical overview, please visit our Getting started with AI features in Mendeley

The Ask My Library feature provides insights based on your query from across the entirety of the PDF documents that you have stored in your personal Mendeley library.

Get Started

Start your journey by describing what you are looking for in your own words. The system will optimize your query to return the most relevant results, more detailed and in-depth queries will generally result in better responses.

Some suggestions for making your query more in-depth:

  • Be specific: Clearly define the scope of your question. Instead of asking a broad question like “What is AI?”, you might ask “What are the latest advancements in reinforcement learning algorithms for autonomous vehicles?”
  • Contextualize your question: Provide context to help the AI understand the background of your query. For example, “In the context of healthcare, how is machine learning being used to predict patient outcomes?”
  • Ask for comparisons: If you’re looking for a comparative analysis, frame your question to reflect that. For example, “How do convolutional neural networks compare to recurrent neural networks in image recognition tasks?”
  • Seek specific applications: Focus on particular applications or case studies. For example, “What are the applications of natural language processing in legal document analysis?”
  • Request detailed explanations: If you need a thorough explanation, make that clear. For example, “Can you explain the key differences between supervised and unsupervised learning with examples?” Ask My Library processes your query and returns up to 20 relevant documents from your personal library. The Large Language Model (LLM) distills insights from these documents and generates a response, referencing specific passages.

Not all returned documents are used in the summary.

Use the copy functionality to save and store the response.

Technical Details

The Ask My Library AI feature uses a technique called RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) to generate reliable responses. It optimizes your query, runs it across a safety check and then uses vector search (semantic similarity) to find relevant text snippets. These snippets are then sent to a Large Language Model (LLM) to generate an answer based on them. The answer will reference the original documents for transparency.

Only the twenty most relevant snippets are sent to the LLM, which decides how many to use for the summary answer.

Limitations

  • Each submitted query has a 500-character limit.
  • Filtering the content used by collection, or specific documents are not yet supported.
  • Non-text content (tables, images, etc.) are not supported.
  • Works best on academic papers
  • Works on PDF files only

Mendeley Desktop can’t connect to the internet, what should I do?

Last updated on May 01, 2025

When you encounter this error message, it’s usually indicative of one of two problems that are preventing Mendeley Desktop from contacting the Mendeley Web servers.

  • A temporary issue either on our servers, or with your ISP routing to our servers, is preventing a connection. Check that you can access mendeley.com within your web browser to verify if this is the case.
  • Local settings are blocking Mendeley Desktop from connecting out. If you’re on a private network, you should be able to configure this yourself.

What is Mendeley Reference Manager?

Last updated on June 02, 2025

Mendeley Reference Manager is the new version of our reference manager that replaced Mendeley Web Library on September 07, 2020. Mendeley Desktop will continue to be available. The new Mendeley Reference Manager is constantly improving with regular updates, responding to users’ direct feedback and through the regular user research and usability testing we do.

Why has Mendeley rebuilt the reference manager?

The solutions we offer must address challenges you face in your daily reality. It’s become increasingly difficult to keep developing the original Mendeley Desktop in the way we and you need. The new Mendeley Reference Manager is built with the latest technology that will allow us to provide regular updates and to act on user feedback at an accelerated rate.

We have also introduced some new features, such as Mendeley Notebook, and refreshed the look of the interface so that it is simpler for you to perform your key reference management tasks.

We have also built Mendeley Cite, a new citation add-in for Microsoft® Word. The new citation add-in now exists as a standalone application, which means that you can use it without having to open or even install Mendeley Reference Manager, as well being able to use it with online versions of Word.

What is happening to Mendeley Desktop and Mendeley Web Library?

While we work hard to bring all the features you use and care about to Mendeley Reference Manager, Mendeley Desktop is still available for use. Mendeley Web Library was replaced with Mendeley Reference Manager on September 07, 2020. We have now stopped all development work on Mendeley Desktop, and there will be no further updates to Mendeley Desktop beyond the current version. Our full development effort is now focused on the new Mendeley Reference Manager so that we can add the features users are telling us they want.

When will Mendeley Desktop stop being available?

Once we are confident that the new Mendeley Reference Manager meets our users’ reference management needs we will start to begin the migration process by notifying you via emails and other forms of communication. To migrate over to the new Mendeley Reference Manager, you will need to make sure you have synced your current Mendeley Desktop and then download the new Mendeley Reference Manager. Having synced your Desktop, all of your work will then exist in the new Mendeley Reference Manager.

Key to helping us make the decision of when to start the migration is user feedback. The feedback we receive from users is vital in helping us make the right product development decisions. To provide feedback, open Mendeley Reference Manager, go to ‘Account & Help’ and select ‘Give Feedback’.

Why are not all my PDFs stored locally by default?

The new Mendeley Reference Manager library is cloud-based. This is to ensure your latest changes are always saved and that you can access your references on multiple devices. If you would like a specific file attachment stored locally, you can download that file to your local machine by opening it in the reader and selecting the download icon. We are also already looking at ways to improve support for local file storage and will have updates out soon.

Sometimes you may wish to import a local file with a URL such as:

via the browser using Mendeley Web Importer. This is possible but requires the user to give Web Importer additional permissions.

  • Enter chrome://extensions into the URL bar.

  • Open the “…” menu in the top-right.
  • Select ‘Extensions’.
  • Locate ‘Mendeley Web Importer’ and click ‘Details’.
  • Check the checkbox named ‘Allow access to file URLs’. You may need to restart your browser.

Due to restrictions at the browser level, it is not possible to import local files using Mozilla Firefox. Please drag the local file to Mendeley Desktop or use the import function within Mendeley Reference Manager.

On Safari:

  • Select ‘Develop’ in the Safari menu.
  • Turn ‘Disable Local File Restrictions’ off. You may need to refresh the page before Web Importer is able to import.

Why is Web Importer failing to retrieve the PDF full text I want?

Last updated on December 23, 2019

Mendeley Web Importer attempts to find a legal full text PDF for each reference it detects. We call this process PDF location.

PDF location is an intrinsically difficult task because the real world of document links, access entitlements and authorisation is complicated. Inaccuracies can occasionally happen.

Reasons for this include:

  • Unexpected PDFs, for example, where the full text is correctly located but it is an unexpected version such as a preprint.
  • Partial PDFs, for example, where the publisher has provided a preview or a sample of a full text but Web Importer mistakenly thinks that it is a complete full text.
  • Incorrect PDFs, for example, where poor quality linking data causes mismatches in document metadata and full text PDF links.
  • False negatives, for example, where Web Importer fails to detect a PDF that is available.
  • Lack of linking data, for example, where a library has purchased access to a given article but Web Importer cannot determine the location of it.
  • Lack of entitlement, for example, where PDF location is successful but the provider has refused authorization to retrieve it. The Web Importer team monitors broken links and attempts to provide as many correct and complete PDF full texts as possible. We are continually working to improve PDF location and retrieval to make the process of accessing and importing references as simple and convenient as possible.

How can I submit my research dataset along with an Elsevier article?

Last updated on August 05, 2026

Mendeley Data is an open, free-to-use research data repository, which enables researchers to make their research data publicly available. There are currently different methods for authors to submit data with their article.

A dataset is the term we use for a collection of research data files produced in the course of research for a paper or project, and accompanying descriptive metadata (title, description, contributors etc).

How

Many Elsevier journals now offer authors the ability to submit research data as part of the article submission process, and research datasets submitted in this way will be stored and independently available on Mendeley Data, linked to/from your published article. The Guide for Authors for the journal you are planning to submit to will indicate if this is available.

Follow these steps to check the availability for your journal:

  • Use the search function on ScienceDirect to locate your chosen publication, by entering the title in the ‘In this journal or book title’ field and clicking ‘Search’.

    • Click on the image of the journal to go to the journal homepage.

  • Click on ‘Guide for Authors’ in the right-hand side section of the menu ribbon on the journal page. Follow these steps to upload your dataset directly to Mendeley Data:

  • Sign in or register to Mendeley Data.

  • Click ‘New dataset’.

  • Upload data files. There is a limit of 10GB per dataset.

  • Add metadata (including Title, Description and Contributors) for the dataset.

  • Click ‘Save’. Please note: Do not click ‘Publish’ yet as this would make the data publicly available.

After uploading your dataset:

  • Depending on the journal policy, your data may not be reviewed, however it will be available to reviewers.
  • When you submit your final, revised version, you can formally publish your dataset on Mendeley Data, which makes it fully open access to everyone, and provides the final dataset DOI in your article. Optionally, you may add details about how to reproduce the research represented in your dataset by entering these steps in the Steps to reproduce field.

You can also add additional metadata by adding details in the Date the data was collected and Nature of the data fields, respectively.

The License field defaults to ‘CC BY 4.0’ but you can change that by clicking Change and making a selection from the drop-down menu, and then clicking Change License. When drafting a dataset, a user may choose to defer the date at which the data becomes available to ensure it is only available at the same time as an associated article, meaning the dataset description and files are not publicly available until that date.

To place a dataset under embargo, it must be in the draft state. Click Set Embargo Date at the bottom right area of the page. The Set Embargo Date dialogue box opens, prompting you to indicate the time length of the embargo by either selecting 3 months, 6 months, or 1 year; or by setting a date using the calendar feature. Once a selection is made, click Set Embargo.

What are the requirements for datasets?

Last updated on May 20, 2026

Datasets are required to have a title, description, and at least one named contributor. Datasets must be scientific in nature and consist of research data. For example, raw or processed experimental or observational data is acceptable, rather than the narrative research article which may have resulted from the research. Datasets must NOT have already been published and therefore may not already possess a DOI. They must NOT contain:

  • Executable files or archives that are not accompanied by individually detailed file descriptions
  • Copyrighted content (audio, video, image, etc.) to which you do not own the copyright
  • Sensitive information (for example, but not limited to, patient details, dates of birth, etc.)

You can upload files of any format, up to a maximum of 10GB per dataset. The types of files you can upload include individual and zipped files as well as folders. Note: Zipped files preview is not supported.

What is the difference between a “draft” dataset and a “published” dataset?

Last updated on March 04, 2026

Once a dataset is drafted, it is in what is referred to as a draft state, meaning it has not yet been published. While in this state datasets can be edited and deleted. However, once a dataset is published, it is not possible to edit or delete it. For example, when you draft a dataset but do not publish it, it appears in My Data under the tab Datasets and has a status of Draft in the Published Version column.

If you publish a dataset and need to modify it, you will do so by using a new version of it. Once you begin editing the dataset, for instance to change any of the metadata, or to add or remove files, you are actually editing the next version which is a replication of the original one.

For example, your published dataset appears in My Data and has a status of Version 1 in the Published Version column. If you click Edit draft, the dataset opens, allowing you to make changes. However, you will see in the right panel under the Published information section that the Status field shows Draft (Version 2), the Published version field shows Version 1, and the Visibility field is Private.

This new version is visible only to yourself and anyone you invite as a collaborator or with whom you share the link. This new version will also be in the draft state until it is ultimately published. If you start to edit the dataset again after it is again published, you will be editing the next iteration - Draft (Version 3), for example. All published versions of a dataset can be viewed and compared by clicking the links in the Version history.
