The Guardian Help Guide

This guide gathers the Guardian’s official help for readers and subscribers: subscriptions and contributions, signing in and your account, the apps, the website, print delivery, contacting the newspaper’s journalists and desks, accessibility, and the terms and privacy policy. It was taken from the Guardian’s own help pages on 29 July 2026.

The Guardian’s help lives in two places, and both are here. Reader help sits on theguardian.com/help, and a separate help centre for subscriptions and contributions sits on manage.theguardian.com. Between them they cover the website, the news apps for iPhone, iPad and Android, the Guardian Editions app, the Feast app, and the printed paper and Guardian Weekly. There is no Windows program and no Alexa skill.

The sections below are editorial. Neither help estate publishes a category tree that survives being taken out of its pages — the help centre shows its topics as a row of buttons rather than in each article — so the grouping here is by subject, and that choice is mine rather than the Guardian’s.

Gathering this needed a real browser: the Guardian sends plain clients a page of about 1,900 characters and fills it in afterwards. It also cost one earlier attempt, which ended when a fragment of the page’s own code was mistaken for a web address; that is fixed, and this is the complete run.

One page was left out deliberately: an article titled “TEST Article”, which is exactly what it sounds like.

Headings run three deep: this title, then a section, then a page, with each page’s own subheadings nested beneath. Every page is listed in the table of contents below.

Feast Beta app

Submitting feedback and reporting issues

How do I supply feedback or report bugs on the Feast app beta?

For the iOS (Apple) devices:

  • From the Feast app settings: Click on the profile icon in the top left corner of the Feast app. Then, click on “Feedback” - it will open an email field with the pre-populated address that will reach the Feast team.
  • Through the Feast app banner messages: Sometimes you will see a banner with a question in some sections of the app. If you want to help us by answering it, you can click on it directly, and it will open an email form.
  • By screenshot: You can take a screenshot in the app, click on the miniature and click Done on the top left corner. Then, select “Share Beta Feedback” and it will open a text field that you can send directly to the Feast team.
  • Through TestFlight: Go to the TestFlight app, click on Feast and then on “Send Beta Feedback”
  • By writing an email: You can also simply write us an email to feast.ios.beta@guardian.co.uk

Feast Beta app

What is the Feast app beta?

The Feast app beta is a pre-release version of the new recipe app, Guardian Feast. It allows users to try out new features before they are made available to the general public. This phase is crucial for gathering feedback and making improvements to ensure the best user experience. We have a limited number of beta invites and your help is invaluable and much appreciated.

How do I become a beta tester?

We have completed the initial round of signups for the beta programme, and the opportunity is currently closed. Should we need to expand the beta community in the future, adequate communications will be published throughout the Guardian channels.

Which devices is the Feast app available on?

Currently, our beta testing is open to iOS users only. You can use it on iPhone and iPad devices running on iOS18 or higher.

Can I access the Feast app beta app on more than one device?

Yes, if they run on iOS 18 and if you sign in with the same account on all of them. However, the beta doesn’t currently sync between devices, which means that all your settings, saved recipes and preferences would not automatically transfer to the other device.

Will there be updates to the Feast beta app?

Yes, the app will be periodically updated to introduce new features, fixes and improvements to enhance your experience, which the beta community members will be the first ones to try out. If the update will feature significant changes or new functionalities, we may notify testers by email highlighting what’s new, and asking for feedback.

How can I leave the Feast beta app?

If you decide to leave the Feast beta, you can opt out via the TestFlight app on your iOS device. Click on TestFlight, then on Feast and scroll to the bottom. Then, click on the Stop Testing. Once you leave, you will no longer receive beta updates. You can also opt out of emails by clicking Unsubscribe at the bottom of emails you receive.

Guardian Editions App

This page answers frequently asked questions about the new Guardian Editions app.

Recent changes to the Editions App:

Why Am I Being Asked to Sign In to the Editions App?

Device Compatibility:

  • The Guardian Editions app will work on a mobile or tablet running iOS 16.0 and later, or Android 7.0 and later.

  • If you are an iOS user and do not see an update pending, search for Guardian Editions in the App Store and tap on the cloud icon.

  • For Amazon users, the app is only supported on Kindle Fire devices older than 2022. It is not compatible with the Kindle Paperwhite.

Publication Times & Updating:

  • PDFs of each day’s editions are available from 4.30am.

  • Interactive features for the newspapers and magazines are usually published by 6.30am every day.

  • If you downloaded a PDF before the interactive features were applied, you can refresh your app by dragging down from the top of the ‘Home’ or ‘Previous editions’ screen.

Offline Reading & Puzzles:

  • All editions can be downloaded for offline reading, which includes our puzzle pages.

  • The interactive ‘puzzles’ feature will not enable offline playing due to the crossword player.

  • The most recent quick and cryptic crosswords remain playable via the interactive ‘puzzles’ tab in the bottom navigation bar.

  • For Apple users, crosswords from each issue can be printed to complete by hand.

How to Sign In:

  • To access your subscription in the app, open the app, tap “more”, and then tap “sign in”. You may have to select “continue” after doing this.

  • If your Guardian account is linked, you can use the Google or Apple buttons to sign in.

  • If your account is not linked, you can enter your email address and select “continue with email”, which will send a six-digit code to your account. Retrieve this code, return to the app, enter it in the box, and you will be signed in.

  • If you have set up a password, you can choose to sign in with that instead by using the link just below the “continue with password” button. This link will take you to a page where you can enter your email address and password and tap “sign in”.

  • If you have an in-app subscription, it should automatically be recognised as long as you are signed into the correct Apple account in your main iOS settings, under “media and purchases”. It is not necessary to sign into an account to access your subscription on an iOS device.

Editions App Access:

  • The Editions app is included as part of our Digital Plus, Print Newspaper and Guardian Weekly subscriptions. It is also available to Guardian Patrons.

  • Readers on certain legacy subscription products will also have access. These legacy subscriptions include the Digital Pack and iOS in-app subscriptions to the Daily Edition app that have been running since before November 2019.

  • If you are unsure of your subscription type, you can check your billing at 0.

    • In-app subscriptions for the app will not appear on this billing page. To check if you have an in-app subscription to the Editions app, open the main settings on your iPhone or iPad. In-app subscriptions were never available on Android.

  1. Tap on your name at the top of the iOS settings page.

  2. Tap on “subscriptions” to check your active subscriptions.

  3. Look for a subscription listed as the Guardian Daily Edition app.

  • If you have an All Access Digital subscription you can upgrade to Digital Plus by visiting the “My Account” section on the Guardian website: 0.

  • If you are a regular supporter or a new subscriber, please follow the link below to sign up for a new subscription: 0

  • Please note: Subscriptions to the standard Guardian app or Feast do not include access to the Editions app. Editions app access is not available for those who hold an all-access digital subscription.

Sign In Troubleshooting:

  • We use a web sign-in for our apps, which means your browser is involved in the process. Ensure you have a compatible browser installed (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge, or Opera) and that it is set to accept cookies.

  • If you are still having trouble signing in, please clear any Guardian cookies from your browser and try again.

  • If you are having trouble receiving our passcode or password reset emails, try whitelisting the email address registration-reply@theguardian.com. Whitelisting instructions for the most common email providers can be found at 0.

  • If you continue to experience difficulties, please contact us through the app by tapping “more” followed by “report a bug”. Please select “yes” when asked if you want to include your anonymous technical logs.

Guardian Feast app

What devices does the app work on?

The Guardian Feast app works on all Apple devices running iOS 18 and above, as well as Android devices 8 and above.

How does the app work?

The app is split into three main sections:

  • Home - be inspired by our fresh selection of hand-picked recipes by our food editors.
  • Search - looking for something in particular Search or browse our constantly expanding library of recipes using a selection of easy to use filters.
  • My Feast - this is where your saved and cooked recipes live.

Is the app available in all countries?

Yes, the app is available in every country through the App Store. We currently have recipes curated for both northern and southern hemispheres to bring you the best seasonal recipes.

How often is the app updated?

The app is editorially curated daily, with new recipes, collections and cooks being added regularly to bring you the best recipes from The Guardian.

Subscribing to Guardian Feast

How much does Feast cost?

Guardian Feast costs £2.99 in the UK, €2.99 in EU, $3.99 in the US and Australia, and $2.99 in Canada for a monthly subscription, with annual options also available.

How do I sign in?

You will be prompted to sign in or create an account when you download the Feast app. We ask you to sign in so that it’s easier for you to return to your preferences and saved recipes within the app.

How do I subscribe to Feast?

When you download the app, you will be asked to sign in with your Guardian account or to create a new one. You do not need a subscription to navigate the home screen but once you tap a recipe, you will be prompted to subscribe through Apple or Google Play. You can choose a monthly or annual subscription.

How do I cancel my subscription?

In-app subscriptions on iPhone and iPad are managed by Apple. To cancel, please go here. In-app subscriptions on Android devices are managed by Google. To cancel, please go here.

Will I get free access as an existing Guardian supporter?

Please log into the Feast app with your Guardian account to check if you are eligible for free access.

Using the app

How do I save recipes in the app?

You will find a save icon at the bottom right of recipe pages within the app. To find these recipes later, tap ‘My Feast’ on the bottom right of the home screen.

How do I use Cook Mode?

Cook Mode prevents your phone from being automatically locked while you are cooking, and allows you to navigate recipes step by step. To use Cook Mode, tap ‘Start cooking’ within a recipe page, and tap the individual steps as you progress.

Personalising your app

Follow and access your favourite topics, writers and journalism series in the app today within My Guardian – a customisable space in the Guardian app that helps signed in readers catch up on the journalism they care about faster.

Can I personalise what’s seen on my homepage?

We’re rolling out the ability for readers to personalise which sections are shown or hidden on the homepage. Where enabled, a ‘Homepage Customisation’ option is available within the app settings menu (accessed by tapping the profile icon on the app menu screen).

You can vary the sections shown or hidden across the UK, US, Australia, Europe and International editions. Preferences are associated with your user account so if you change devices they will be preserved.

If you’re not yet part of the Homepage Customisation rollout, you can adjust visibility for sections by using the show-hide controls for sections on the homepage.

What is My Guardian?

My Guardian incorporates the earlier ‘Save for later’ functionality and provides a new single destination for you to track and consume journalism from your favourite topics, writers and series.

Followed tab

  • Topics: All the stories published and categorised into the writers and topics you follow.
  • Latest: All the stories published by the writers and topics you follow in reverse chronological order.
  • Manage the topics and writers you already follow.

Saved tab

  • Browse articles you have bookmarked earlier to read at a time suitable for you.

Please do share any further thoughts or suggestions based on your experience to customer.help@theguardian.com.

How do I add topics to My Guardian?

Manage topic tags from within My Guardian’s ‘Followed’ tab by tapping on the plus icon.

You may also ‘Follow’ writers directly within articles and other content types from the byline (eg ‘Follow Marina Hyde’).

How do I remove topics from My Guardian?

Manage topic tags from within My Guardian’s ‘Followed’ tab by tapping Edit Topics.

You can also ‘Follow’ writers directly within articles and other content types from the byline (eg ‘Follow Marina Hyde in My Guardian’).

How do I save items for later?

Bookmark items that you’d like to read at a time that suits you by tapping on the bookmark icon in an article page, or on the three dots on article cards (iOS) or long press menu on article cards (Android).

Saved articles are listed in the ‘Saved’ tab within My Guardian.

What does Follow on article pages mean?

Following from the byline of an article means you will see the latest articles from the author of the article in your My Guardian space.

Is My Guardian a paid-for feature?

No. My Guardian - like the old ‘save for later’ functionality which it incorporates - is free to all readers although it does require you to be signed in so that we can maintain preferences and saved content across devices. Note that usual metering rules apply and articles viewed through My Guardian will count towards any metering thresholds.

Do I need to sign-in to use My Guardian?

Yes. Personalised functionality such as My Guardian relies upon being signed in so that we can save your preferences. (This also means you don’t lose your preferences or saved articles when moving between devices.)

How can I choose which notifications I get?

As well as breaking news, you can choose to get notifications from your favourite writers, liveblogs, series, and football (soccer) teams and matches. To do this, just tap the notifications button.

  • For writers, open an article. The ‘Notifications’ option is near the author byline and can be toggled on or off. This will send you a notification when that person publishes a new article
  • Notifications for a football team will automatically send you goal alerts when they score. Search for your team or tap on their crest on a football page. The ‘Notifications’ option is at the top of the page
  • Notifications for a liveblog will send you a notification when a key event happens in that story. To enable this, tap the ‘Get alerts on this story’ option near the top of any liveblog

How can I manage or stop the notifications I receive?

You can manage your notifications from the settings area. Here, you can choose the notifications you no longer wish to receive or unsubscribe from all of them.

How can I change the font-size of articles?

In any article, tap on the font-size icon and then the larger or smaller buttons. This will change the size of the text in the main portion or the article. Your choice will be remembered for every article you then visit.

How can I enable ‘dark mode’ in the Apple iOS app?

IOS ‘dark mode’ is enabled in your specific device settings, rather than within any of the Guardian apps.

To change to ‘dark mode’ please navigate to your devices setting menu.

Finding articles from the past in digital format

If you’re interested in viewing Guardian articles and other Guardian related content from the past in digital format, you will find the right place to look on this page. If you are interested in accessing past newspaper editions, visit our section on back issues.

1821 to 2003

Digital reproductions of every page, article and advertisement published in the Guardian from 1821-2003 and in the Observer from 1791-2003 can be viewed online via the subscription service, newspapers.com.

Members of the British Library can access the professional version of the archive via ProQuest in the reading rooms at St Pancras, London, while many school, university and public libraries also have a subscription.

The Observer Magazine is not included in the digital archive for the years 1964-1993. Issues for these years are held in hardcopy by the British Library and other libraries of deposit.

September 1998 onwards

Content that appeared in the Guardian and Observer print editions and online since September 1998 is available on theguardian.com. Most articles are included, apart from a few which have been removed for copyright reasons (eg a book serialisation).

Historical Guardian News & Media archives

We publish a daily selection of historical articles from the Guardian archive. See also From the Observer archive.

How to make a complaint about Guardian content

Find out about our readers’ editor, how to complain about a piece that appeared online or in print and how the process works

Please note: the readers’ editor does not handle queries about subscriptions, deliveries, moderation or technical matters.

The readers’ editor

The readers’ editor is Guardian News & Media (GNM) internal ombudsman. The Guardian was the first UK newspaper to adopt a readers’ editor in 1997.

The job of the readers’ editor is “to collect, consider, investigate, respond to, and where appropriate come to a conclusion about readers’ comments, concerns, and complaints in a prompt and timely manner, from a position of independence within the paper”. The global readers’ editor role covers the Guardian (including Guardian US and Guardian Australia) and Guardian Weekly.

For complaints about Observer editorial content published on the Guardian’s website, please see the relevant section further down this page.

Elisabeth Ribbans is the Guardian’s fifth readers’ editor and took up the global role in January 2020.

GNM policy is to correct substantial errors as soon as possible. Corrections appear on the relevant web page and/or in the newspaper and significant corrections are collated in the Guardian corrections and clarifications column.

What does the readers’ editor cover?

The readers’ editor can look into complaints about articles in our newspapers and the online services that we control. The readers’ editor looks at complaints and queries against the GNM editorial code of practice.

The GNM editorial code of practice builds upon the Editors’ Code of Practice followed by members of Ipso, which is itself an updated version of the Press Complaints Commission Editors’ Code to which GNM provided input over many years.

Before contacting the readers’ editor, you should read through GNM’s editorial code to understand whether the readers’ editor is the correct route for your complaint. The editorial code handles complaints in 16 areas. They are:

  1. Accuracy

  2. *Privacy

  3. *Harassment

  4. Intrusion into grief or shock

  5. Reporting suicide and self-harm

  6. *Children

  7. *Children in sex cases

  8. *Hospitals

  9. *Reporting of crime

  10. *Clandestine devices and subterfuge

  11. Victims of sexual assault

  12. Discrimination

  13. Financial journalism

  14. Confidential sources

  15. Witness payments in criminal trials

  16. *Payment to criminals

Where there are asterisks above, the editorial code recognises that complaints should be weighed against a judgment as to whether the story is in the public interest.

What the readers’ editor won’t look at

The readers’ editor won’t look at complaints that fall outside of the GNM editorial code (see above).

The readers’ editor office was created to resolve substantial complaints and queries from individuals who feel we’ve breached the Guardian’s editorial standards. As such, the readers’ editor’s time, effort and resources will be focused on such complaints, and she reserves the right not to consider complaints:

  • From anyone not personally and directly affected by the matter which forms the subject of the complaint.

  • That are trivial, hypothetical or otherwise vexatious or insignificant.

  • That are without justification (such as an attempt to argue a point of view or to lobby).

  • About advertising (unless in exceptional circumstances).

  • About Guardian reader offers.

  • That are legal complaints.

How do I complain?

Please read the GNM editorial code first to understand whether the readers’ editor can look into your complaint. You can then email the readers’ editor’s office directly on guardian.readers@theguardian.com or write to The readers’ editor, The Guardian, Kings Place, 90 York Way, London, N1 9GU outlining the following points:

  • The article you’re complaining about.

  • The date on which it appeared.

  • Whether the article appeared in print or online (and whether through a browser or via one of our mobile apps).

  • The nature of your complaint in no more than 500 words.

  • Which part of the Editorial Code it breaches.

You can also leave a voicemail on +44 (0)20 3353 4736 from Monday to Friday.

Please note that in making a complaint, you agree to respond promptly to any request for further information. Our complaints process is free of charge, regardless of outcome.

We reserve the right to amend this policy as required. We will publish the current policy on our website. Your complaint will be considered against the published policy on the date of receipt of your complaint.

What happens to your complaint?

Once we’ve received your complaint, you should receive an automated reply acknowledging receipt. If your complaint is being taken up, we aim to contact you within 72 hours.

We aim to give a substantive response to your complaint within 28 days of receiving all the necessary information to allow us to investigate. However, this may take longer in more complex cases where more information is required, or where journalists are away or unreachable.

If we receive multiple complaints about the same issue we may not respond to all.

If the readers’ editor deems there to have been a breach of the GNM editorial code, she will suggest an appropriate remedy. Corrections and clarifications will appear on the relevant web page and/or in the newspaper.

We will always aim to handle your complaint fairly, courteously and with respect. We expect the same behaviour from complainants and reserve the right to decline to consider complaints that are abusive or gratuitously offensive.

If at any stage of your complaint we do not hear back from you within 28 days, we will consider your complaint satisfied and closed.

Appeal process

If you are unhappy with our final response to your complaint the readers’ editor will direct you to the Review Panel, which provides an external review of the decision of the readers’ editor. A request for review will be considered where a substantive complaint was brought to the readers’ editor within four months from the date of publication in print, or 12 months from the date of publication online.

Should your complaint reach this stage, the readers’ editor will send you all the information you need to appeal to the Review Panel.
