Ramblio Help Guide

Ramblio is a social network built around sound. People post short voice recordings called rambles, talk live in hangouts, gather in communities called bubbles, and play games by ear. Many of its members are blind or have low vision, and the app is made to work well with screen readers.

About this guide. Gathered 27 November 2026. Ramblio has no help centre on the web. The Windows and Mac app has a help page of its own (F1), but its address is not published, so it could not be gathered. This guide is built from what Ramblio’s developer, Joshua Stirland, has published elsewhere:

  • The App Store listing, including its notes for each version up to 2.0.0
  • The desktop download page
  • The Safety Standards
  • The Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy, for the rules and choices a member needs; the legal language is left out

It covers the iPhone app, the Windows app and the website. Ramblio also has a Mac app and a test version for Android, which this guide does not cover. Ramblio changes often, so a step here may differ slightly from what you hear.

Getting started

Installing on iPhone

Ramblio is free in the App Store and needs iOS 16.4 or later. Search the App Store for Ramblio, or open Ramblio in the App Store. The listing describes it as built for iPhone.

The first time you record or join a hangout, allow Ramblio to use the microphone.

Installing on Windows

The Windows app needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. It offers rambles, messages, hangouts, games and more.

  1. Download the Ramblio installer from the desktop page.
  2. Open ramblio-desktop-setup.exe. If your browser warns about the file, choose to keep it.
  3. If Windows says it protected your PC, choose More info, then Run anyway. Ramblio says its installer is not signed yet, which is why Windows warns.
  4. Follow the installer. You can add a desktop icon or change where it installs.
  5. Ramblio starts when installation finishes. Later, open it from the Start menu or the desktop.

Ramblio checks for new versions by itself and offers to install them. To check yourself, choose Help, then Check for updates.

Signing in on the web

You can also use Ramblio in a web browser: go to Ramblio’s sign-in page. The home page lets anyone hear public rambles, switch between the All and Popular feeds, browse zones, and search for rambles, people or bubbles.

Who can join

Ramblio is for adults 18 and older. The sign-in screen says so, and using the app confirms that you are. Accounts found to belong to minors are deleted.

You may have more than one account, but not to harass people, get around a block or ban, manipulate others, or pretend to be someone else.

Hangouts, bubbles and games

Bubbles

A bubble is a community. In a bubble you can post rambles or start hangouts for its members. A bubble may be open to anyone, open by request, or by invitation only. Search can find bubbles as well as rambles and people.

Games

Ramblio has card, dice, board and trivia games, all played by ear. You can play against bots, with friends in a chat, or with everyone in a hangout.

Hangouts

A hangout is a live audio room. You can host one, take part, or drop in just to listen.

  • Speaking. Listeners ask to speak, and the host or a moderator lets them in. Ramblio keeps track of who has asked and who is speaking.
  • Text chat. Each hangout has a text chat beside the voices.
  • Sound. Hangouts offer spatial audio, a music mode, and background noise removal.
  • Ending. A host is asked to confirm before ending a hangout.
  • Dropped connections. If your connection drops, Ramblio tries to put you back in the room.

Do not record or share hangout audio unless everyone in it has agreed. The Terms of Service forbid it.

Listening

Feeds and zones

The Following feed has rambles from people you follow. All has every new ramble, and Popular has the ones getting the most attention. Your place in a feed is kept when you move to another screen and back.

Zones group rambles by interest, such as Music, Soundscapes, Books and Literature, and Education. Browse a zone to hear only that kind of ramble.

Playlists, bookmarks and transcripts

Bookmark a ramble to find it again later. Put rambles in a playlist to hear them in order. A ramble of speech has a transcript, so you can read what was said as well as hear it.

Searching

Search finds rambles, people and bubbles.

The Now Playing bar

When a ramble plays, the Now Playing bar stays available wherever you go in the app. Auto-play goes on to the next ramble by itself. You can set the playing speed; the website offers half speed up to double speed.

You can control playback without opening the app: from the lock screen, from headphone buttons, or with the VoiceOver magic tap, a two-finger double tap.

People and messages

Chats

Chats are private messages, by voice or text, with one person or with a group. You choose who may message you.

Comments and reactions

Reply to a ramble with a text comment or a voice comment. Reactions are a quick way to show how a ramble made you feel.

Profiles and private accounts

Your profile can have a photo and pinned rambles, which stay at the top for visitors to hear first.

Make your account private if you want to approve each person who asks to follow you. You can also hide rambles made with AI from what you hear.

Ramblio checks each profile, bubble and ramble photo automatically before others see it. The same check writes a description of the photo, so screen reader users can know what it shows.

Posting rambles

Choosing who hears a ramble

When you post a ramble, choose who can hear it:

  • Public: anyone, including people not signed in.
  • Logged-in users: anyone with a Ramblio account.
  • Followers: only the people who follow you.
  • Unlisted: left out of feeds; share it by its link.
  • Private: only you.

Editing a ramble after posting

You can edit a ramble after it is published.

Marking a ramble explicit or AI-made

A ramble with adult themes must be marked with the Explicit flag. This applies to anything made with AI as well. A ramble made with AI should also be labelled as AI-made, so members who choose to hide such rambles do not hear it.

Recording a ramble

A ramble is a short audio post: something you made, captured, or just felt like saying.

  • Recording. Record in the app, pausing and resuming as you go.
  • Importing. Instead of recording, bring in an audio file or a link.
  • Photos. Add a photo if you like. Ramblio describes it for screen reader users.
  • Zones. Choose the zone where your ramble belongs.
  • Waiting between rambles. Ramblio may make you wait a short time between rambles. If you post too soon, it says so.

Post only what is yours or what you have the right to share, including music, sound effects, voices and artwork.

Safety and privacy

Blocking and reporting

  • Block. You can block anyone, at any time, to stop all contact from them. Making a new account to reach someone who blocked you is against the rules.
  • Report. Profiles, rambles and comments each have a Report choice. The Ramblio team reads every report. Possible outcomes are removing the content, a suspension or a permanent ban.
  • Emergencies. If someone is in immediate danger, call your local emergency services first, then report it in the app or by email.

Ramblio’s address for safety concerns is chef@tweesecake.app. Serious reports are reviewed quickly, and cases of imminent risk come first.

Community rules

Ramblio removes content, and may ban the person who posted it, for:

  • Sexual exploitation, grooming or abuse of anyone, and any sexual content involving children
  • Illegal material, material shared without consent, or pornography
  • Harassment, hate, bullying, or threats of violence
  • Encouraging self-harm, suicide, crime or dangerous acts
  • Using someone else’s work, voice or likeness without the right to, including sexual or harmful deepfakes of real people
  • Spam, scams or harmful links
  • Recording or sharing hangout audio without everyone’s consent

Your data and deleting your account

Ramblio keeps your account details, what you post, and the audio you upload until you delete them or your account. When a photo is changed or removed, the old one is deleted.

You can delete your account at any time in the app, or by emailing chef@tweesecake.app. The same address handles requests to see, correct or erase your data.

Miscellaneous

Getting help and sending a report

  • The Windows app’s help. Press F1, or choose Help, then Ramblio help. It opens in your web browser and covers every feature and key.
  • Reporting a problem. In the Windows app, choose Help, then Send a report. Describe what happened; Ramblio adds its recent log so the developer can see the cause.
  • Following Ramblio’s news. Ramblio posts on Mastodon, Bluesky and Facebook.

Supporting Ramblio

The More tab has a Share button for telling others about the app and a Tip Jar for supporting its developer through Ko-fi.
